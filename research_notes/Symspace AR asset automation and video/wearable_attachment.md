## Key Question 1 — Per-product-type geometric analysis of an arbitrary generated mesh to find the attachment frame (anchor point, axis, scale) automatically

### Takeaway
No off-the-shelf open-source tool does "accessory attachment-frame detection" end to end; the practical route is per-type classical geometry (topology/genus test, RANSAC cylinder/plane fits, cross-sections, principal axes) built on trimesh/pyRANSAC-3D, optionally seeded by a 3D part segmenter (P3-SAM/PartField/SAMPart3D) or a VLM/MLLM keypoint namer (ZeroKey), then scaled by anthropometric priors (ring size tables, wrist circumference, frame widths, foot length) or by landmark-measured dimensions.

### Cited Findings

Open-source geometry tooling
- trimesh provides ray–mesh queries ("location, triangle index"), cross sections ("Calculate cross sections for a 2D outline, or slice a mesh"), convex hulls, "approximate minimum volume oriented bounding boxes", "principal components of inertia", watertight and Euler-number checks, voxelization, booleans (via Manifold3D/Blender), repair of winding/normals/holes, and arbitrary 4×4 transforms — [trimesh README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/README.md)
- The Euler characteristic χ = V − E + F relates to genus; "for a simple watertight solid χ = 2 … For a block with a single through-hole (topologically equivalent to a torus), the genus g = 1 and the Euler characteristic χ = 0"; trimesh exposes `euler_number` and `is_watertight` — [tessl trimesh core-operations doc](https://tessl.io/registry/tessl/pypi-trimesh/4.8.0/files/docs/core-operations.md); [RapidMade, Robust Hole Detection in STL Meshes](https://rapidmade.com/robust-hole-detection-in-stl-meshes/)
- pyRANSAC-3D (Apache 2.0) fits plane, cylinder, sphere, circle, cuboid, line, point and cone; the cylinder fit returns centre, axis direction, radius and inlier indices — [pyRANSAC-3D README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leomariga/pyRANSAC-3D/master/README.md)
- RANSAC is the standard approach for recognising cylinders/tori in point clouds; cylinder fitting "is not reliable as a cylinder detector without filtering false detections" (needs pre-segmentation) — [Fitting and recognition of geometric primitives … localized voting (arXiv 2205.15426)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15426); [MathWorks pcfitcylinder](https://www.mathworks.com/help/vision/ref/pcfitcylinder.html)

3D part segmentation / keypoint tools (to isolate the band, lens, sole, hook etc. before fitting)
- Hunyuan3D-Part (Tencent) was publicly released on September 25, 2025 and contains P3-SAM and X-Part; P3-SAM is "a native 3D Point-Promptable Part segmentation model designed to fully automate the segmentation of any complex 3D objects", trained on ~3.7 million models; automatic mode samples point prompts by farthest-point sampling and merges masks with NMS — [P3-SAM paper (arXiv 2509.06784)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06784v4); [Hunyuan3D-Part on HF](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-Part); input is a mesh, output is part masks (P3-SAM) or individual part meshes (X-Part); X-Part "works best with scanned or AI-generated meshes" — [Hunyuan3D-Part README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Part/main/README.md)
- PartField (NVIDIA, ICCV 2025) "predicts part-based feature fields for 3D shapes" and returns hierarchical clusterings you can traverse for granularity; accepts .obj/.glb meshes, .ply point clouds and Gaussian splats; needs Python 3.10 / PyTorch 2.4 / CUDA 12.4; the PartNet-trained model is not released — [PartField README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nv-tlabs/PartField/main/README.md)
- SAMPart3D (Nov 2024, arXiv 2411.07184) renders 16 views of a .glb and uses a Point Transformer V3 backbone with DINOv2/SAM-derived features for multi-granularity part segmentation — [SAMPart3D README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Pointcept/SAMPart3D/main/README.md)
- SAMPart3D and PartField "train feature learning networks through distillation or contrastive learning and achieve 3D segmentation by feature clustering"; P3-SAM uses them as baselines — [P3-SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06784v4)
- KeypointNet (MIT licence) has 8,234 ShapeNet models and 103K keypoints over 16 categories (airplane … vessel); glasses, shoes and rings are NOT among them — [KeypointNet README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qq456cvb/KeypointNet/master/README.md)
- ZeroKey (ICCV 2025) shows "pixel-level annotations used to train MLLMs can be exploited for extracting and naming salient keypoints on 3D models without ground truth labels", requiring multi-view aggregation, back-projection and filtering because detections and captions are noisy — [ZeroKey (arXiv 2412.06292)](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06292v1)
- "Leveraging VLM-Based Pipelines to Annotate 3D Objects" (ICML 2024) captions unlabeled 3D objects from multiple rendered views; text aggregation across views "is susceptible to hallucinations" — [arXiv 2311.17851](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17851v2); Holodeck used GPT-4V on four orthogonal renders (0/90/180/270°) to output category, dimensions and descriptions per asset — [Holodeck (arXiv 2312.09067)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09067)

Scale priors per product type
- Ring: ISO 8653:2016 defines sizes by inner circumference in mm; US whole sizes differ by 0.032 in (0.81 mm) internal diameter; e.g. US 6 ≈ 16.5 mm, US 7 ≈ 17.3 mm, US 8 ≈ 18.2 mm, US 9 ≈ 19.0 mm, US 10 ≈ 19.8 mm, US 11 ≈ 20.6 mm inner diameter — [Wikipedia, Ring size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_size)
- Wrist: average male wrist circumference 17.2 cm (US Army Natick 2017 data as reported); women typically 14.0–16.5 cm — [FitnessVolt](https://fitnessvolt.com/average-wrist-size-males-females/); a Colombian anthropometric study reports wrist perimeter medians of 14.68 cm (F) and 16.53 cm (M), 5th–95th percentile 13.53–16.10 cm (F) and 15.20–17.98 cm (M) — [PMC10308009 Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10308009/table/T1)
- Glasses: lens width 40–60 mm (S 40–48, M 49–54, L 55–62), bridge 14–24 mm, temple length 120–150 mm, written on the temple as e.g. "52-18-140" — [Warby Parker](https://www.warbyparker.com/learn/eyeglasses-measurements); [All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/eyewear/eyeglasses/styles/glasses-frame-size/)
- Shoes: Mondopoint is foot length in mm; charts map 225 mm → US 5 / EU 36.5, 250 mm → US 7 / EU 39–40, 280 mm → US 10–11 / EU 44–45 — [SkatePro size guide](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/z192.htm); Paris point = 2/3 cm and EU size ≈ (foot length cm + 1.5 cm allowance) × 1.5 — [Haflinger](https://haflinger.com/en/blogs/news/schuhgroessee-berechnen-mit-dem-pariser-stich)
- Foot length priors: Hong Kong adults 24.3 cm (M, 5–95th pct 22.5–26.2) and 22.6 cm (F, 21.0–24.1) — [HK Practitioner table](https://www.hkcfp.org.hk/Upload/HK_Practitioner/2006/hkp2006vol28may/images/p205t07.html); Montenegrin adults 26.85±1.55 cm (M), 24.13±1.29 cm (F) — [UDG resource](https://empowerhr4inno.udg.edu.me/en/resources/show/c8d8770e-d631-4e81-8854-80b025e3434c); US summary tables list ~25.6 cm (M) / ~23.4 cm (F) — [NCSU Anthropometric Summary Data Tables](https://www.ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2017/09/Anthropometric-Summary-Data-Tables.pdf)

How existing try-on systems define the frame (evidence of conventions)
- A glasses VTO patent attaches via "a nose point and at least one ear point" on the face model and "a nose connection point and at least one earpiece connection point" on the product model — [US9990780](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9990780)
- The 2026 "Virtual Ring Try-On" paper's pipeline is: hand landmark detection (MediaPipe, 21 points) → ring object localisation (YOLO-v8) → geometric alignment and resizing to an RoI defined from the target finger's landmarks → synthesis; no GPU required — [arXiv 2606.28792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28792)
- An edge wristwatch framework (IJIMAI 2026) estimates "the watch model's 3D position from three key hand landmarks" with "buffer-based geometric parameter smoothing", >30 fps on phones/browsers — [UNIR repository](https://reunir.unir.net/items/55e33ae7-685c-40e6-a9f5-a0f4285706ae/full)
- Amazon's virtual-shoe patent predicts "an orientation, scale, and position" of the foot plus a segmentation mask for foot/leg/garments, then "removes portions of the 3D model of the shoe that intersect" with the foot-leg mask — [US11900559B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11900559)
- DeepAR's shoe setup expects a .glTF shoe under a foot-tracking node, with the Foot Occluder Mesh mirrored by "setting the X scale to −1" for the other foot — [DeepAR shoe try-on quick setup (search excerpt)](https://docs.deepar.ai/archive/shoe-try-on-quick-setup)
- "3D Reconstruction of Shoes for Augmented Reality" (Jan 2025) builds VTONShoes with "dense keypoints, joint keypoint localization, and silhouette segmentation" for 6-DoF foot pose and introduces the Diverse-Shoes dataset; runs 25–45 fps — [arXiv 2501.18643](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18643v2)

### Inferences (proposed automatic attachment-frame recipes; not from a single source)
- Ring: (1) make watertight (trimesh repair / voxel remesh), compute `euler_number`; a ring should be genus-1 (χ = 0). (2) Find the hole axis: take the smallest principal-inertia axis of the band region, or RANSAC-fit a cylinder (pyRANSAC-3D) to the inward-facing vertices (vertices whose normal points toward the centroid); the fitted axis is the finger axis, the fitted radius the inner radius. Cross-check by casting rays through the centroid along the candidate axis: a true through-hole has no hits. (3) Pivot = hole centre; local +Y = finger axis; local +Z = direction from hole centre to the farthest vertex (the stone/head) so the setting faces outward. (4) Scale so inner diameter = target (from the ISO/US table, e.g. 17.3 mm for US 7) or = measured finger width from landmarks × a small clearance.
- Watch/bracelet: genus-1 or an open "C". Fit the inner surface as a cylinder/ellipse (RANSAC); inner circumference ≈ 2πr. The case/dial is the largest planar, outward-facing region (RANSAC plane on the outward vertices of the segment that P3-SAM/PartField isolates as the case); dial normal = local "up" (dorsal side of the wrist); loop axis = forearm axis. Scale inner circumference to measured wrist width/thickness from landmarks or to the ~14.7–17.2 cm priors above.
- Glasses: the mesh is bilaterally symmetric; mirror-plane detection (PCA + reflect-and-ICP) gives the sagittal plane. Lens plane = RANSAC plane through the two largest flat regions; bridge point = lowest point of the frame on the sagittal plane between lenses; temple tips = the two farthest vertices along the −Z (posterior) axis. Frame width = 2×lens + bridge + end-pieces ≈ 125–150 mm from the Warby Parker ranges; scale the mesh so its total width matches the face-mesh width at the temples (ARKit/ARCore mesh) or keep metric size if the generator outputs metric units.
- Earrings: find the thin, topmost component (hook/post) via part segmentation or by thin-feature analysis (small cross-section area near the top of the principal axis); the attachment point is its top; +Y = world up, hanging under gravity (attach as a pendulum pivot and let physics/simple damped rotation handle swing).
- Necklace: for a closed chain (genus 1, large inner radius) the frame is the loop centre with +Y along the loop axis; the pendant (farthest vertex from the loop plane along −Y) marks the front; drape by sampling the loop against a neck/chest proxy, or use a simple two-point attachment (clasp back at neck-nape, pendant at sternum).
- Shoes: sole plane = RANSAC plane with the largest planar inlier area and downward normal (after orienting by PCA; the long axis is heel→toe, the up axis is the sole normal); heel point and toe point = extreme vertices along the long axis on the sole plane; opening = boundary loop of the largest non-sole hole (if the mesh is open) or the topmost ring of the upper. Scale so heel→toe length = foot length (landmarks) + ~1–1.5 cm (Paris point allowance) or to a chosen size table. For a single generated shoe, mirror across the sagittal plane (scale X = −1) and flip triangle winding/normals (trimesh `repair`), as DeepAR does for occluders.
- Hats: crown axis = PCA minor axis of the inner cavity; attachment = inner brim centre; scale inner circumference to head circumference measured from the face mesh (ARKit/ARCore) plus margin — no sources found specific to hats (gap).
- Because KeypointNet does not cover accessories and no public dataset of "accessory attachment keypoints" was found, a learned keypoint model would need in-house labels; a VLM-on-multiview-renders approach (Holodeck/ZeroKey style) is the quickest way to get category + "which part is the band/hook/sole" labels without training, with geometric fitting providing the precise frame.

### Gaps
- No paper or library found that directly outputs "attachment frame for ring/watch/glasses/shoe" from an arbitrary mesh; all recipes above are inferences.
- Could not open trimesh/Open3D API docs (blocked); Open3D's built-in RANSAC is plane-only to my knowledge — unverified here.
- Hat attachment conventions: no sources found.
- Average male/female foot-length figures for the US are attributed to a summary table whose primary source I could not open.

## Key Question 2 — Landmark sources per platform (face, hands, feet, body) and how attachment maps onto them

### Takeaway
Face: ARKit (1220-vertex mesh, blend shapes, eye transforms) and ARCore (468-vertex mesh + centre pose + 3 region poses) have different topologies, so vertex-index anchors must be per-provider; neither includes ears, and ARKit's mesh is explicitly "coarse". Hands: Apple Vision gives 21 2D points only; ARKit hand tracking is visionOS-only; MediaPipe Hand Landmarker (21 image + 21 metric world landmarks, handedness) via homuler's plugin or Unity Inference Engine BlazeHand is the cross-platform path, with AR depth supplying absolute Z. Feet: no native phone foot tracking on either platform; MediaPipe Pose gives ankle/heel/foot-index per foot (3 points), while research systems (ARShoe, VTONShoes, 2025 CIS paper) use dedicated foot-keypoint + PnP networks that are not open-sourced. Body: ARKit 3D body (rear camera, hip-rooted, scale factor) vs MediaPipe Pose/RTMPose on both; iOS 17 Vision adds a 3D body-pose request.

### Cited Findings

Face — ARKit
- ARFaceTrackingConfiguration "tracks facial movement and expressions using the front camera", detects faces within 3 m; "Face tracking supports devices with Apple Neural Engine in iOS 14 … and requires a device with a TrueDepth camera on iOS 13 and earlier" — [Apple ARFaceTrackingConfiguration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfacetrackingconfiguration)
- Face coordinate space: metres, origin "centered behind the face", right-handed, +X to the viewer's right, +Y up relative to the face, +Z outward toward the viewer; the anchor exposes `geometry`, `blendShapes`, `leftEyeTransform`, `rightEyeTransform`, `lookAtPoint`; the geometry is "a coarse triangle mesh representing the topology of the detected face" that can also serve as occlusion geometry — [Apple ARFaceAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor)
- "Face mesh topology is constant across ARFaceGeometry instances" — only vertex positions change — [Apple ARFaceGeometry](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfacegeometry); ARFaceGeometry has 1220 vertices and "index 9 is on the nose" — [Kodeco AR Face Tracking tutorial](https://www.kodeco.com/5491-avplayer-tutorial-for-ios-getting-started?page=2)
- Community reports: ARKit lacks ear landmarks, so earring apps "supplement ARKit's face mesh with ML facial landmarks (such as MediaPipe) … combined with ARKit head pose" — [search excerpt, AR Unity developer posting](https://djinni.co/jobs/791072-ar-unity-developer) (weak source)
- AR Foundation samples issue: developers who hard-coded face-mesh indices for eyebrows/nose found it "only works on specific devices because the face mesh can vary across different devices" — [arfoundation-samples issue #754](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/arfoundation-samples/issues/754)

Face — ARCore / AR Foundation

Face — MediaPipe (platform-independent alternative)
- Face Landmarker outputs 478 3D landmarks, 52 blendshape scores and a "facial transformation matrix to apply face effects on the detected landmarks" — [MediaPipe Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)

- "A real-time mobile solution for shoe try-on using foot pose estimation and 3D processing techniques" (Complex & Intelligent Systems, 2025): deep model "operating in real-time on the CPU", PnP for 6-DoF foot pose, deployed in a smartphone app — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-025-02188-x)
- "Virtual Footwear Try-On in Augmented Reality Using Deep Learning Models" (JCISE 2024): estimates 6-DoF foot pose from a colour image — [ASME](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/computingengineering/article-abstract/24/3/031002/1163540/)
- ShoeFit (NeurIPS 2025) notes "traditional open-source pose estimation methods (e.g., Open-Pose, DWPose) struggle to capture lower-body/foot poses" for footwear try-on and proposes a 2D diffusion approach with a new dataset — [NeurIPS 2025 paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/2e9f9cde1b709281a06dd14f679e4c51-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Snap's footwear try-on "has been updated to a SnapML model that better detects the user's foot" (closed) — [Snap Footwear Try-On (search excerpt)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/foot-tracking)

Body (for necklaces)
- Sapiens (Meta, ECCV 2024) provides 2D pose, part segmentation, depth and normals at 0.3B–2B params; Sapiens-Lite is "4× faster" inference-only; a Sapiens2 release exists — [Sapiens README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sapiens/main/README.md); the Sapiens2 pose head outputs 308 keypoints covering face, hands and feet — [RunComfy node description](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-Sapiens2/sapiens2-pose)
- RTMPose-s reaches 72.2 AP on COCO at 70+ fps on a Snapdragon 865; RTMPose-m/l have COCO-WholeBody variants (with foot and hand keypoints); backends include ONNXRuntime, TensorRT, ncnn — [RTMPose (arXiv 2303.07399)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07399)

### Inferences
- Face anchors on both providers: use pose-level anchors wherever possible (ARKit anchor origin + eye transforms; ARCore centre pose + nose-tip/forehead region poses) and derive glasses/earring/hat/necklace points as fixed metric offsets in the face frame; use vertex indices only as per-provider lookup tables (ARKit 1220 vs ARCore 468 differ, and issue #754 shows index reuse across providers breaks). Both meshes are front-camera only.
- Ear anchors: neither ARKit nor ARCore meshes contain ears; the ARCore/MediaPipe 468 topology's face-oval points 234/127/162 (left) and 454/356/389 (right) sit at the cheek/temple edge, from which an earlobe can be estimated as a fixed lateral/downward offset in the face frame. On ARKit a comparable edge vertex must be picked once per app from the canonical mesh (gap: no published index list).
- Necklace anchor: approximate as chin vertex (152 on the 468 mesh) projected down the face −Y axis by ~8–12 cm plus −Z offset, or better from a pose model's shoulder/neck joints (MediaPipe 11/12 shoulders; ARKit neck/shoulder joints) when the body is in frame.
- Hand 3D on Android: MediaPipe world landmarks give metric relative geometry (finger widths, orientation) but not absolute position; sample the ARCore depth image at the image landmark (as the existing iOS plugin does with AROcclusionManager) to place the hand frame absolutely. Depth is rear-camera on ARCore in practice (unverified), so selfie-camera ring try-on on Android would rely on MediaPipe world-landmark scale or a hand-size prior.
- Feet with open tools: ankle/heel/foot-index (3 points per foot) from MediaPipe Pose give position, yaw and pitch of the foot but weak roll; combining with ARCore/ARKit depth at those points and an assumed foot length yields a usable 6-DoF estimate. True foot-specific networks (ARShoe/VTONShoes/CIS-2025) are not open-sourced; replicating them needs a labelled foot-keypoint dataset.

### Gaps
- Unity's AR Foundation 6.2 per-platform support tables (face regions, eye transforms, body tracking) could not be opened; statements rely on Google/Apple docs and community posts.
- No authoritative list of ARKit 1220-vertex indices by anatomical feature was found.
- Whether ARKit face tracking can run concurrently with body tracking was not verifiable.
- ARCore Depth API camera restriction (rear only) not verified from a primary page.
- Exact release dates for homuler plugin v0.16.3 and model weights for the foot-pose papers are unknown (GitHub API access was restricted in this session).

## Key Question 3 — Occlusion and realism techniques (fingers over rings, trouser legs over shoes, hair over earrings)

### Takeaway
iOS gives people-segmentation depth natively (hands included) on A12+; ARCore in AR Foundation only gives environment depth, so Android hand/leg occlusion must come from either an invisible proxy occluder mesh driven by landmarks or a segmentation model (MediaPipe hand segmentation / pose segmentation / Lightship semantics). Commercial systems use both: Snap ships Hand Occluder, foot "feet removal" occluder meshes and "lower garment occlusion"; Amazon's and ARShoe's approaches cut the shoe mesh against a foot/leg/garment segmentation mask.

### Cited Findings
- ARKit people occlusion works on A12+ via `personSegmentationWithDepth` (occludes only when the person is closer than the virtual object) or `personSegmentation` (always occludes) — [Apple, Occluding virtual content with people](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/occluding-virtual-content-with-people)
- AR Foundation AROcclusionManager supports Environment Depth, Human Depth and Human Stencil textures; "the ARCore implementation … supports AROcclusionManager.environmentDepthTexture but does not support the other Textures related to human segmentation" — [AR Foundation / ARCore occlusion docs (search excerpt)](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.arcore@6.1/manual/features/occlusion.html)
- ARCore Depth and Raw Depth APIs are "only supported on a subset of ARCore enabled devices"; Raw Depth is unfiltered and "may contain missing data" — [ARCore Raw Depth codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.cn/codelabs/arcore-rawdepthapi?hl=en); [ARCore supported devices](https://developers.google.com/ar/devices)
- ARKit's face mesh can be used "to create occlusion geometry … that obstructs the camera's view of other virtual content" (e.g. glasses temples behind the head) — [Apple ARFaceAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor)
- Lightship ARDK 3 integrates with the AR Foundation Occlusion Manager, offers 20 semantic-segmentation channels and "Occlusion Suppression" to stop noisy depth of chosen classes from occluding content — [Niantic blog](https://niantic.dev/blog/lightship-ardk-3-for-all); [Lightship occlusion how-to](https://lightship.dev/docs/ardk/how-to/ar/setup_real_world_occlusion)
- Snap Lens Studio: Hand Mesh is "a 3D mesh that mimics the user's hand in real time"; the Hand Occluder "hides AR objects behind the center of the hand's palm"; Hand Segmentation texture can occlude "an object as it moves behind the hand" — [Snap hand mesh / hand segmentation (search excerpts)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/ar-tracking/hand/hand-mesh)
- Snap Footwear Try-On: "Feet Removal: a component adds to the scene occluder mesh that represent the tracked feet"; "Lower Garment Occlusion: when on, will attempt to use the lower garment to occlude the 3D mesh of the footwear" — [Snap Footwear Try-On (search excerpt)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/foot-tracking)
- Snap Earring Try-On: "Hair occlusion can occlude the earrings using the Head Visibility Custom Component" — [Snap Earring Try-On (search excerpt)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/earring-try-on)
- Snap Necklace Try-On: Neck Binding "adds neck and chest colliders and out of body occlusion"; necklaces can be deformed to body-mesh reference models in T/A pose — [Snap Necklace Try-On (search excerpt)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/references/templates/object/Try-On/necklace-try-on)
- DeepAR: Foot Occluder Mesh as child of the foot node, mirrored with X scale −1 for the other foot — [DeepAR quick setup (search excerpt)](https://docs.deepar.ai/archive/shoe-try-on-quick-setup)
- Amazon patent: segmentation mask for foot/leg/garments; intersect with virtual objects; "removes portions of the 3D model of the shoe that intersect with the second segmentation mask" — [US11900559B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11900559); a separate patent "Method and system of virtual footwear try-on with improved occlusion" exists — [US10943365](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10943365)
- ARShoe generates "realistic 3D shoe model occlusion" from its segmentation branch — [ARShoe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10515); VTONShoes uses silhouette segmentation — [arXiv 2501.18643](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18643v2)
- BytePlus ring try-on uses "occlusion relation analysis algorithms, based on 3D mesh and 2D masking, [to] simulate the interaction between fingers and the ring" — [BytePlus AR try-on features (search excerpt)](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/byteplus-ar/ar-try-on-features)
- Patents on "Fast hand meshing for dynamic occlusion" exist (US11238651, US11620792) — [USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11620792)

### Inferences
- iOS: keep AROcclusionManager human-depth for rings (fingers over the band) and shoes; add the ARKit face mesh as an invisible depth-only occluder for glasses temples and earrings.
- Android: build a landmark-driven proxy occluder: capsules per finger segment from MediaPipe landmarks (finger radius from a prior or from landmark spacing), a wrist/forearm capsule for watches, a foot wedge (heel-toe-ankle) for shoes, rendered to depth only (ColorMask 0, ZWrite On, render queue before the product). Supplement with MediaPipe hand/pose segmentation masks or Lightship person/garment semantics for trouser-over-shoe cases.
- Realism beyond occlusion in the sources: contact shadows (Snap/BytePlus mention shadows), environment reflections (ARKit environment texturing on face sessions), physically based gem shaders — not detailed in any open source found.

### Gaps
- Specific occluder geometry/shader parameters used by Snap/DeepAR not readable (sites blocked).
- MediaPipe Hand segmentation availability in the Tasks API (vs legacy) not verified.

## Key Question 4 — Smoothing and stability (filters and typical values)

### Takeaway
The One-Euro filter is the de-facto standard; MediaPipe's own pose pipeline ships concrete values (min_cutoff 0.05 / beta 80 for normalized landmarks; 0.1 / 40 for world landmarks; 0.01 / 10 for auxiliary ROI landmarks), and try-on papers add "origin stabilization" or buffer averaging on the derived rigid transform rather than per-landmark smoothing.

### Cited Findings
- MediaPipe pose_landmark_filtering.pbtxt: normalized landmarks One-Euro min_cutoff 0.05, beta 80.0, derivate_cutoff 1.0; world landmarks min_cutoff 0.1, beta 40.0, derivate_cutoff 1.0 (value scaling disabled); auxiliary landmarks min_cutoff 0.01, beta 10.0, derivate_cutoff 1.0 — [pose_landmark_filtering.pbtxt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/pose_landmark/pose_landmark_filtering.pbtxt)
- One-Euro tuning procedure (Casiez): set beta = 0, mincutoff ≈ 1 Hz; hold still/move slowly and lower mincutoff until jitter is acceptable; then move fast and raise beta (start 0.001–0.0001, ×10 steps) to kill lag; "lower [mincutoff] values allow to remove more jitter", "higher [beta] values make the filter react faster" — [1€ filter page](https://cristal.univ-lille.fr/~casiez/1euro); [Needle Engine OneEuroFilter API](https://engine.needle.tools/docs/api/OneEuroFilter)
- ARShoe: "an origin stabilization method is presented to smooth the trajectory of overlaid shoes and eliminate jitters" — [ARShoe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10515)
- Wristwatch edge framework: "a buffer-based method for smoothing geometric parameters during movement" at >30 fps — [UNIR 2026](https://reunir.unir.net/items/55e33ae7-685c-40e6-a9f5-a0f4285706ae/full)
- Snap Neck Binding "binds objects to the neck using Body Tracking with additional smoothing" — [Snap Necklace Try-On (search excerpt)](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/references/templates/object/Try-On/necklace-try-on)
- MediaPipe Hand Landmarker graph has no smoothing stage; Face Landmarker smooths only in stream mode for a single face — [hand_landmarker_graph.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/hand_landmarker/hand_landmarker_graph.cc); [face_landmarker_graph.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/face_landmarker/face_landmarker_graph.cc)

### Inferences
- Filter the derived rigid transform (position via One-Euro per axis; rotation via One-Euro on quaternion components or slerp toward the target with velocity-adaptive alpha), not only the raw landmarks; add a dead-band (hysteresis, e.g. ignore translation deltas below a few mm and rotation deltas below ~1°) and confidence gating (hold last pose for N frames when landmark confidence drops) — this is what "origin stabilization" and "buffer-based smoothing" amount to.
- Scale (ring/wrist/foot size) should be estimated once with a median over the first seconds and then locked, since per-frame scale jitter is highly visible.

### Gaps
- No commercial SDK publishes its filter constants; Kalman-filter parameters used in try-on apps not found.

### Inferences
- The common commercial design is: a tracked surface mesh (hand mesh / body mesh / face mesh) → a fixed catalogue of named binding points on that mesh (14 ring points, left/right wrist, ear landmark, neck) → product prefab with its pivot pre-authored to sit at that point → occluder copies of the tracked mesh. The "automatic" part the client wants (placing an arbitrary generated GLB's pivot correctly) is exactly what these SDKs leave to the artist; hence the need for the geometric/VLM pipeline in Key Question 1.
- Perfect Corp's automatic wrist/finger sizing implies that estimating metric hand dimensions from landmarks (plus depth) is viable at product quality; MediaPipe world landmarks + AR depth are the open approximation.
