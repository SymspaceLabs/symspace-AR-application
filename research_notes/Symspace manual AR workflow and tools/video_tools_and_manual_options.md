## KQ1. Templated / deterministic product-video rendering (identity-safe)

### Takeaway
For an interim, identity-safe pipeline the open-source stack is mature: Blender headless (bpy) with EEVEE for ~1 s/frame-class speed or Cycles for photoreal, CC0 Poly Haven HDRIs, a GPL turntable add-on or a ~50-line bpy camera-orbit script, then FFmpeg/MoviePy for overlays and music. Remotion is the strongest "programmatic titles/overlays" layer but needs a paid company licence above 3 employees; Motion Canvas (MIT) and MoviePy (MIT) are fully free. Unity's Recorder is explicitly not usable in `-batchmode`, which rules it out for server-side automation. Hosted template APIs (Shotstack, Creatomate, JSON2Video, Plainly, Bannerbear) are cheap per minute but closed and cannot render 3D; they are an overlay/assembly layer only.

### Cited Findings

**Blender (GPL, open source)**
- EEVEE vs Cycles throughput: one animation test at 1930x1080 rendered ~1 s/frame in EEVEE (AO, bloom, SSR on) vs 7–8 s/frame in Cycles without denoising; another comparison showed Cycles at 1:26 and 1:14 per frame while EEVEE took 0:06 per frame — [GameDev.tv community thread](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/rendering-an-animation-challenge-eevee-vs-cycles/185423); [Super Renders Farm EEVEE vs Cycles 2026](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/eevee-vs-cycles-cloud-render-farm-comparison-2026) (page itself unreachable; figures from search snippet). Hardware for these figures is not stated in snippets — **unverified hardware**.
- Super Renders Farm frames it as: Cycles for archviz and product renderings, EEVEE for motion design/previz; "a 300-frame previz at 1080p that renders in 12 s/frame on a single workstation is something EEVEE handles effortlessly" — [Super Renders Farm](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/eevee-vs-cycles-cloud-render-farm-comparison-2026).
- Blender Open Data–style benchmark numbers (Cycles): Ryzen 9 7950X + RTX 4090, BMW27 scene ≈ 0.855 s with OptiX, ≈ 1.32 s CPU-only (as reported; CUDA figure of 5.88 s in the same snippet looks anomalous — treat with caution); RTX 4090 OptiX per-frame estimates: Junkshop 6.02 s, Monster 3.08 s, Classroom 7.39 s; OptiX roughly 6–7x faster than CPU-only on BMW27 — [OpenBenchmarking result](https://mail.openbenchmarking.org/result/2509258-NE-GPUV4409013); [CGDirector Blender benchmark results](https://www.cgdirector.com/blender-benchmark-results-updated-scores/); [RenderJuice RTX 4090 for Blender](https://www.development.renderjuice.com/gpus/rtx-4090-for-blender).
- Turntable add-ons: SpinWiz (Blender Market, commercial) automates 360° turntable setup, background/lighting presets and a render queue with batch rendering of multiple models; compatible with Blender 4.1+ — [SpinWiz FAQ](https://blendermarket.com/products/spinwiz/faq); [SpinWiz docs](https://blendermarket.com/products/spinwiz/docs).
- "Carousel" add-on (GPL-3.0, GitHub) creates 360 spin animations in automatic mode (select objects, press Turntable; camera auto-positioned at object centre) or manual mode; README lists Blender 2.91 support — [Korchy/blender_carousel](https://github.com/Korchy/blender_carousel). **Maintenance against Blender 4.x/5.x unverified.**
- "Turntable Camera" on extensions.blender.org adds a camera panel that auto-generates keyframe sequences for turntables, built for product gallery/carousel rendering; "Turntable Camera Extended" adds cinematic sequences with dynamic lighting; "Sloth's Turntable-Control" offers 6 view modes, 1–15 camera multi-angle generator, 12+ interpolation styles and batch render with smart file naming — [extensions.blender.org Turntable Camera](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/turntable-camera/) (page unreachable; from search snippet); [Superhive Sloth's Turntable-Control](https://superhivemarket.com/products/sloths-turntable-control); [Turntable Camera Extended (itch.io)](https://picoroid.itch.io/turntablecamera-blenderaddon).
- HDRI library: all Poly Haven assets are CC0 — usable for any purpose including commercial, no attribution required; only restrictions are not claiming authorship or re-licensing; Poly Haven asks that you tell customers the assets are free/public domain and link back if possible; library is 980+ HDRIs and 780+ textures — [Poly Haven FAQ](https://polyhaven.com/faq); [Poly Haven license](https://polyhaven.com/ja/license); [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-textures-hdris-materials).

**Unity**
- Unity Recorder: "When you launch the Unity Editor from the command line with the -batchmode argument, the graphics pipeline is not initialized, and the Recorder cannot capture frames properly and the recording never starts"; Recorder works only in the Editor, not in standalone players/builds; Recorder Track in Timeline can record a specific Cinemachine camera; outputs H.264 MP4, VP8 WebM or ProRes MOV — [Unity Recorder 5.1 Known Issues](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.recorder@5.1/manual/KnownIssues.html) (unreachable; from search snippet); [Unity Recorder 4.0 Recording Video](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.recorder@4.0/manual/RecordingVideo.html).
- OTOY Octane for Unity documents cinematic rendering through Timeline and Recorder in play mode — [OTOY Unity Recorder docs](https://docs.otoy.com/UnityH/Unity/Recorder.htm). No source found for a managed "Unity Cloud Rendering" video service — see Gaps.

**three.js / Babylon.js headless capture**
- Deterministic frame capture pattern: open a headless browser with Puppeteer, call `seekToFrame(n)` per frame, screenshot, pipe to FFmpeg without temp files; works for HTML, SVG, Canvas and WebGL; targets 1080p60 — [andreybo/html5-animation-video-renderer](https://github.com/andreybo/html5-animation-video-renderer) (repo URL returned 404 on direct fetch; description from search snippet — **repo may be renamed/removed**).

- Cenvero Cetus (commercial) bundles headless Chrome + FFmpeg for CSS/GSAP/Three.js/WebGL — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/cenvero-cetus/makers).
- Basic encode: `ffmpeg -framerate 30 -i frame-%04d.png -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4` — [screenshotapi.net Puppeteer guide](https://screenshotapi.net/blog/how-to-record-videos-with-puppeteer-a-simple-step-by-step-guide).

### Inferences
- A 10 s, 24 fps, 1080p turntable = 240 frames. Using the cited ~1 s/frame EEVEE figure and ~7–8 s/frame (unspecified GPU) or ~3–7 s/frame (RTX 4090 OptiX, benchmark-scene class) Cycles figures: EEVEE ≈ 4 min; Cycles GPU ≈ 15–30 min; Cycles CPU-only (cited 6–7x slower than OptiX) ≈ 1.5–3.5 h. These are extrapolations from benchmark scenes, not product scenes; a lightly textured product on an HDRI with denoising at 64–128 samples will typically be faster than Junkshop/Classroom. Treat as order-of-magnitude.
- Unity is a poor fit for a server pipeline: Recorder needs an interactive editor/graphics context. Use Unity only if an artist renders locally; otherwise Blender bpy or a headless browser capture.
- Remotion is the best fit for brand-kit titles/overlays if Symspace has >3 employees and is willing to pay the ~$100/mo minimum; otherwise Motion Canvas (MIT) or pure FFmpeg `drawtext`/`overlay` filters (FFmpeg docs were not fetched; these filter names are from general knowledge — **uncited**).
- The hosted template APIs give the fastest route to "turntable + text + music" if the product turntable is pre-rendered as an MP4 with alpha/transparent-PNG sequence and uploaded as an asset; none of them render 3D natively.

### Gaps
- No primary-source per-frame timings for EEVEE Next (Blender 4.2+) or Blender 5.x on specific CPUs/GPUs were obtained; blenderartists thread and Super Renders Farm pages were blocked. Blender 5.x release status itself was not verified.
- Blender VSE text-strip/titling workflow from the command line: not researched (search budget exhausted). Known from general knowledge to exist but **uncited**.
- Unity Cloud rendering as a managed video service: no source found.
- FFmpeg filter/templating documentation not fetched.
- Whether Carousel/Turntable Camera add-ons are maintained for Blender 4.x/5.x: unverified.
- Editframe and Vidyard were not researched (budget).

## KQ2. Hosted AI video services — pricing, terms, identity, watermarking, quality rankings

### Takeaway
Per-second API prices in Oct 2026 span roughly $0.01/s (Vidu Q2 Turbo 540p, Seedance 1.0 Pro Fast 480p) to $0.60/s (Veo 3.1 4K) with a dense middle band at $0.05–$0.12/s (Veo 3.1 Fast/Lite, Gen-4 Turbo, Kling 2.5 Turbo, Sora 2 standard, Wan 2.6, Nova Reel). A 10 s clip therefore costs ~$0.10–$6 at list price, before retries (which are typically 3–5x for product work). Google embeds SynthID in all Veo output and it cannot be disabled; several vendors offer commercial rights only on paid tiers. E-commerce-specific generators (Amazon Ads Video Generator, Creatify, Pippit, HeyGen) take product photos/URLs, not GLBs, and user reviews report product warping when AI actors "hold" the product.

## KQ3. Hybrid pipelines that keep the product pixel-accurate

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a "render the product with alpha, AI-generate only the plate" design: IC-Light (Apache-2.0, SD1.5) provides open foreground- and background-conditioned relighting; a Flux-based IC-Light V2 exists but its weights/licence status is unclear; DepthFlow (AGPL-3.0) gives cheap 2.5D parallax motion from a single generated plate; fSpy (GPL-3.0) solves camera matching from a still so the Blender product camera can match an AI plate. Ad-tech evidence that this approach is standard is thin in reachable sources — the strongest signal is negative evidence (reviews of Creatify reporting warped products when AI generates the product itself).

### Cited Findings
- IC-Light (lllyasviel): Apache-2.0; base model Stable Diffusion 1.5; variants `iclight_sd15_fc` (text + foreground conditioned; initial latent can influence relighting), `iclight_sd15_fcon` (offset-noise variant, slightly lower performance), `iclight_sd15_fbc` (text + foreground + background conditioned); repo notes a Flux-based version "in development" with a Nov 25 discussion update; default background remover BRIA RMBG 1.4 is non-commercial and the README recommends swapping to BiRefNet for commercial use — [lllyasviel/IC-Light](https://github.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light) (verified).
- IC-Light V2 (Flux-based): described as trained on FLUX with a 16-channel VAE and native high resolution, preserving texture/shadow/highlight detail; two model types — text-conditioned relighting and background-conditioned; available as a fal.ai endpoint and ComfyUI node; a community re-upload exists on Hugging Face — [ComfyUI Wiki IC-Light V2](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/models/ic-light/ic-light-v2); [RunComfy IC-Light V2 (fal) node](https://runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI_IC-Light-v2_fal/ic-light-v2); [HF molidiss/iclight-v2](https://huggingface.co/molidiss/iclight-v2); [pricepertoken fal IC-Light v2](https://pricepertoken.com/image/model/fal-ai-iclight-v2). **Open-weight licence for V2 is unverified; the official repo says only "in development"; the HF mirror may be unofficial.**
- IC-Light relighting approach described academically with CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 on the arXiv HTML (this is the paper's licence, not the code's) — [arXiv 2312.03698](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03698v2).
- DepthFlow: AGPL-3.0; converts still images into 2.5D parallax videos via depth estimation + ray-marched GLSL shader; claims up to 8k50fps on an RTX 3060-class GPU; seamless loops, DoF, vignette, lens distortion, optional RealESR upscaler; "commercial use is encouraged" but AGPL copyleft applies to derivative works; also packaged as ComfyUI nodes — [BrokenSource/DepthFlow](https://github.com/BrokenSource/DepthFlow) (verified); [RunComfy Depthflow nodes](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-nodes/ComfyUI-Depthflow-Nodes).
- fSpy: GPL-3.0; open-source cross-platform still-image camera matching; official Blender importer add-on; 562 commits on develop, 82 open issues — [stuffmatic/fSpy](https://github.com/stuffmatic/fSpy) (verified); [80.lv on fSpy for Blender](https://80.lv/articles/improving-blender-workflows-with-still-image-camera-matching-app/).
- Creatify is the one ad-tech vendor that explicitly claims to place "your real product" into the video (vs Arcads' actor library) — [Creatify vs Arcads](https://creatify.ai/compare/arcads); and reviews report product warping when the actor holds it — [Superscale review](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/creatify/review).
- Amazon's generator keeps product pixels from the detail page and adds AI motion/context around them ("showcase products like toys, tools, or worn items") — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f900e696-3d76-4017-b381-6ebe433c6c6d).
- Kling 3.0 "Omni" tiers accept a reference image/element at a premium ($0.22–$0.84 per 5 s vs $0.14–$0.56) — [muapi Kling 3 pricing](https://muapi.ai/kling-3-pricing); Seedance 2.0 has image-to-video and reference-to-video sibling endpoints — [Atlas Cloud Seedance 2](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/guides/seedance-2-cost-per-second).
- Pencil aggregates Bria and Getty models (Bria markets licensed-data image models suited to product/background work) — [Superscale Pencil](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/pencil).

### Inferences
- Recommended hybrid v1 (all open source): Blender renders product beauty pass + alpha + depth + shadow-catcher pass against a generated or HDRI plate; plate from an image model (FLUX/Qwen-Image/Z-Image — not researched here, see Gaps) animated with DepthFlow for parallax, or a short Wan/LTX background clip (prior report); composite in Blender's compositor or FFmpeg `overlay` with the shadow pass multiplied in. IC-Light FBC is used only on the *product still* to harmonise colour/light to the plate for hero frames; per-frame IC-Light on video will flicker (inference from it being an image model; **uncited**).
- Camera match: if the plate is AI-generated, generate it first with a known focal length/eye height in the prompt, solve with fSpy, import into Blender, then render the product with that camera — the product never leaves the deterministic renderer.
- AGPL (DepthFlow) is fine for an internal batch tool; if Symspace exposes DepthFlow through a hosted service, AGPL's network clause would require releasing modifications — keep it as an unmodified CLI subprocess or choose another parallax approach.
- Licence hygiene: IC-Light's default RMBG 1.4 remover is non-commercial; Symspace already has alpha from the renderer so no matting is needed for product shots.

### Gaps
- No reachable ad-tech case study (Pencil, Omneky, Creatify engineering blog) explicitly documenting "render-then-composite" as their production method; search budget ran out on the targeted query.
- FLUX / Qwen-Image / Z-Image licences and suitability for background plates: not researched here (budget); the prior model report may cover.
- "Harmonisation" models beyond IC-Light (e.g., video harmonisation nets) were not surveyed.
- IC-Light V2 weights/licence: unverified; treat as closed/hosted (fal) until the official repo publishes.

## KQ4. Manual intervention that most improves results

### Takeaway
Sources converge on a small set of practices: randomise seeds to explore, then lock seeds and change one variable at a time; generate ~4 candidates per reference/shot and have a human lock the pick before any downstream generation; keep brand elements (logo, fonts, colours, CTAs) as post-hoc overlays rather than generated pixels (this is how Amazon's and Creatify's tools work — overlays are editable); respect platform safe zones for 9:16; and only use music/voice with explicit commercial terms (paid-tier TTS and music plans; CC0 or track-level CC filters).

### Cited Findings
- Seed locking is "a first-class production practice" recommended by Kling; same seed + prompt + settings reproduces output; lock a seed to change one variable at a time — [Cliprise seeds & consistency](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/guides/best-practices/seeds-consistency); [LTX blog on seeds](https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-use-seeds-in-ai-video); [Moonvalley seeding help](https://help.moonvalley.ai/seeding).
- Workflow: randomise seeds in a first pass to explore, lock in the final pass; increment seeds sequentially (12345, 12346, …) for variations that preserve core identity — [Cliprise one image → multiple videos](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/workflows/marketing/one-image-multiple-videos).
- "Generate four options per character sheet and environment reference, select the best, and lock it before any video generation begins — this single step prevents most consistency problems downstream"; experienced producers who lock seeds and iterate CFG methodically report >40% fewer regenerations — [Cliprise AI video pipelines](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/workflows/marketing/ai-video-generation-pipelines); [HackerNoon "there is no git for AI filmmaking"](https://hackernoon.com/there-is-no-git-for-ai-filmmaking-here-is-how-i-build-my-own).

## KQ5. Cost and time per video; SKU-level caching

### Takeaway
At list prices, a pure Blender template video costs only compute (minutes of GPU time or tens of minutes of CPU time), a hosted AI 10–15 s clip costs ~$0.15–$9 per accepted clip after retries, and a hybrid sits in between (one render + one cheap plate/B-roll clip). Rendering once per SKU to a master (product pass with alpha at the largest aspect) and deriving 16:9/1:1/9:16 and platform variants in FFmpeg/Remotion is the cost lever; AI spend should be reserved for the plate or b-roll, not the product.

### Cited Findings
- Render-time inputs: EEVEE ~1 s/frame vs Cycles 7–8 s/frame (unspecified hardware); Cycles 1:14–1:26/frame vs EEVEE 6 s/frame in another test — [GameDev.tv thread](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/rendering-an-animation-challenge-eevee-vs-cycles/185423); [Super Renders Farm](https://superrendersfarm.com/article/eevee-vs-cycles-cloud-render-farm-comparison-2026). RTX 4090 OptiX 3–7.4 s/frame on Blender benchmark scenes; OptiX ~6–7x faster than CPU-only — [OpenBenchmarking](https://mail.openbenchmarking.org/result/2509258-NE-GPUV4409013); [CGDirector](https://www.cgdirector.com/blender-benchmark-results-updated-scores/).
- Hosted per-second prices (see KQ2): $0.01/s (Vidu Q2 Turbo) → $0.60/s (Veo 3.1 4K); Sora 2 batch $0.05/s; Gen-4 Turbo $0.05/s; Kling 2.5 Turbo $0.07/s; Nova Reel $0.08/s; Veo 3.1 Fast $0.10–0.12/s; Sora 2 $0.10/s; Wan 2.6 $0.10/s; Gen-4.5 $0.12/s; Luma Ray 3.2 1080p $0.24/s; Veo 3.1 $0.40/s — sources as cited in KQ2.
- Template/assembly layer prices: Shotstack from $0.045/min at volume to $49/mo for 300 min; JSON2Video 1 credit = 1 s Full HD, $49.95/mo for 200 min; Remotion Automators $0.01/render with $100/mo minimum — [Submagic Shotstack](https://www.submagic.co/blog/shotstack-pricing); [JSON2Video plans](https://json2video.com/docs/v2/pricing/plans); [Remotion company licenses](https://www.remotion.dev/blog/company-licenses).
- Turnaround claims: Amazon's generator produces six variants in ~5 minutes — [Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f900e696-3d76-4017-b381-6ebe433c6c6d). Creatify charges 5 credits per 15 s — [Wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/creatify-pricing). Seedance 2.0 / Vidu / Kling generation-time data on Artificial Analysis was not reachable.
- Retry/regeneration reduction >40% with seed locking — [Cliprise pipelines](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/workflows/marketing/ai-video-generation-pipelines).

### Inferences (worked estimates, order-of-magnitude; hardware assumptions stated)
- Pure Blender template, 10 s @ 24 fps 1080p (240 frames):
  - EEVEE on a consumer GPU: ~1–2 s/frame → 4–8 min wall time; on a cloud GPU at ~$0.5–1.0/h (uncited market rate) ≈ $0.05–0.15 per clip.
  - Cycles GPU (RTX 4090-class, 64–128 samples + denoise): 3–8 s/frame → 12–32 min ≈ $0.10–0.50 at the same cloud rates.
  - Cycles CPU-only (16-core): 6–7x slower than OptiX → 1.5–3.5 h; viable only as overnight batch.
  - Deriving 1:1 and 9:16 from a master with overlays via FFmpeg: seconds per variant, negligible cost.
- Hosted AI 10–15 s clip: list $0.10–$9; with a 3–5x retry factor to hit product accuracy, budget $0.50–$30 per accepted clip depending on model; turnaround minutes per attempt, so a 4-candidate human pick costs 4x list and ~5–15 min of queue + review.
- Hybrid (render product + AI plate/b-roll): render cost as above + one still plate ($0.01–0.10 class image model, uncited) animated with DepthFlow (seconds on GPU) or one 5 s background clip from a $0.02–0.07/s model (~$0.10–0.35) → roughly $0.20–1.00 per clip with near-deterministic product identity.
- SKU-level caching strategy: (1) cache the imported/normalised GLB (scale, pivot, material fixes) per SKU; (2) render one *master* product pass per SKU per template at the largest needed frame (e.g., 2160x2160 square or 1920x1920 with generous margins) with alpha + depth + shadow passes, EXR/PNG sequence; (3) store per-SKU "hero frame" PNG for thumbnails and as the reference image for any AI conditioning; (4) derive aspect variants (16:9, 1:1, 9:16) by crop/pan from the master in FFmpeg, applying safe-zone margins per platform; (5) brand overlays and music are applied last, so a brand-kit change never triggers a re-render; (6) key the cache on (GLB hash, template id, HDRI id, renderer settings version) so identical inputs are never re-rendered; (7) AI plates/b-roll are cached per (category, mood) rather than per SKU and reused across SKUs.
- Turnaround SLA suggestion: template path — minutes (automatic, no review) to <1 h with a single human approval; hybrid — same day; fully generative — 1–2 business days to allow pick-of-4 review and retries.

- No measured timings on a real Symspace product GLB; the per-frame numbers are from benchmark scenes and forum tests.
- Vendor SLAs/queue times for hosted video APIs not obtained (Artificial Analysis generation-time pages unreachable).
