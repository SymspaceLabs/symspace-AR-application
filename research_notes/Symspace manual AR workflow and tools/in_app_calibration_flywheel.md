## KQ1. UX precedents for manual fit adjustment in AR try-on (what is exposed, how persisted, impact)

### Takeaway
Consumer try-on products split into two camps: body-anchored try-on (Wanna, Perfect Corp, Warby Parker, Snap) exposes essentially no free-form nudge/scale UI to the shopper and instead invests in sizing *inputs* (on-screen finger/wrist rulers, TrueDepth PD, credit-card calibration), while placement AR (Apple AR Quick Look, IKEA Place, Amazon AR View) exposes drag/two-finger-rotate/pinch gestures with an explicit "true size" reset or a hard scale lock. Published business impact numbers exist only as vendor/retailer claims (Farfetch/Wanna, Snap, IKEA); no independent study of conversion/return impact of *manual adjustment* specifically was found.

**HCI evidence on touch manipulation in handheld AR**
- ISMAR 2014 (TUM): three techniques for 3D manipulation on mobile touch devices; "A study with 18 participants shows that the two-thumb method tops the other techniques, performing better with respect to the total manipulation time and total number of gestures" (snippet) — [TUM publication record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/touch-gestures-for-improved-3d-object-manipulation-in-mobile-augm/); [ISMAR 2014 poster](https://ismar2015.vgtc.org/ismar/2014/poster/touch-gestures-improved-3d-object-manipulation-mobile-augmented-reality)
- VRST 2018 poster (TH Köln): for constrained object manipulation in AR, "a touch-based interface is significantly more efficient, although gestures are being subjectively more accepted"; the touch approach maps vertical/horizontal swipes to scale/rotate (snippet) — [TH Köln](https://cg.web.th-koeln.de/?p=1535); [PDF](https://cg.web.th-koeln.de/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/VRST2018_PosterAbstract_Evaluation_Smartphone_Interaction.pdf)
- Artanim: handheld collaborative AR manipulation using "touch and slide to translate, device orientation to rotate, and pinch and spread to scale" (snippet) — [Artanim PDF](https://www.artanim.ch/pdf/publications/67.pdf)

### Inferences
- For wearables the industry norm is "measure then auto-fit", not "let the user drag the ring"; for placement objects the norm is "gesture-manipulate with a true-size reset". Symspace's plan (expose nudge/scale for wearables as an interim) is therefore unusual but defensible if framed as a *fit-tuning* step whose result is captured as data, and if the controls are constrained (one slider for size, one for slide-along-finger/wrist, one for roll) rather than free 6-DoF.
- Apple's double-tap-to-reset and `allowsContentScaling` are the two reusable UX primitives: always offer a "true size" reset, and for SKUs whose dimensions are trusted, lock scale (or clamp to a narrow band) to prevent the user from "fixing" a bad pivot by scaling.
- The two-thumb/indirect-gesture finding (ISMAR 2014) and the constrained-swipe finding (VRST 2018) support mapping Symspace's adjustments to single-axis sliders or edge-of-screen swipes rather than on-object direct manipulation, which is hard when the target is a small ring on a moving hand.
- No source exposed per-SKU/per-user persistence of manual adjustments; the closest analogues are Warby Parker/Perfect Corp storing a *measurement* (PD, finger mm) that drives automatic fit. Persisting the user's size measurement is privacy-sensitive (Warby Parker explicitly limits storage), so Symspace should store derived numbers (ring size, wrist circumference) with consent rather than raw face/hand data.

- No independent study quantifying the effect of exposing manual fit adjustment on conversion or returns.

## KQ2. Merchant/operator self-service configuration tools in commerce AR platforms

### Takeaway
Self-service merchant tooling in 2026 is overwhelmingly "upload a correctly authored GLB/USDZ at real-world scale with a specified origin" (Shopify, Zakeke, Snap catalog feeds); platforms do not let merchants interactively place an item on a reference hand/face/foot in a web tool. Body-anchored try-on vendors either make the creator do that placement in a desktop authoring tool (Snap Lens Studio templates with Wrist/Foot/Ring/Head binding and reference occluders) or keep it as a vendor-side service (Wanna positioning guides; Perfect Corp "upload images").

### Cited Findings
- Shopify product 3D models: "A 3D model on a Shopify product page must be a GLB or USDZ file of up to 500 MB"; Shopify "supports GLB model uploads and automatically creates USDZ models"; "the 3D model should be the same size as the real product when compared in AR. The model's scale is measured in meters" per glTF; origin "sits at the product's base" (snippets, Shopify + third-party guides) — [Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com.au/blog/3d-model-products); [Tripo3D Shopify guide](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/shopify-3d-product-models-glb-and-ar-workflow-guide)
- Shopify Partners 3D modeling checklist: GLB or USDZ with "1 base color texture, 1 normal map texture, 1 texture with ambient occlusion, roughness, and metalness mapped to the RGB channels respectively, and 1 emissive map if applicable"; "Model file size should be less than 15 MB, with 3D models typically about 4 MB" (snippet; page blocked) — [Shopify 3D modeling checklist](https://help.shopify.com/partners/resources/creating-3d-models/3d-model-standards-checklist); [Shopify: hiring a partner to create 3D models](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/expert-3d-model)
- Zakeke (self-service): "Install the app, upload a 3D model, and set personalization rules for your product, that's it!"; merchants "upload a 3D model in standard obj format for each product" (snippet) — [Bluestone PIM: Zakeke](https://www.bluestonepim.com/zakeke-3d-ar-product-showcase); [Adobe Commerce Marketplace: Zakeke](https://commercemarketplace.adobe.com/futurenext-zakekeconfigurator.html)
- Threekit (per Zakeke's own comparison page, so biased): "not a self-service Plug & Play solution, with setup and onboarding only handled with the help of their team or partners" (snippet) — [Zakeke vs Threekit](https://www.zakeke.com/?p=60411)
- Dopple FAQ mentions "a future Self Serve portal" (snippet) — [Dopple FAQ](https://dopple.io/faq)
- VNTANA: "converts CAD files into lightweight 3D models"; consumers use AR "to get a sense of an object's size" (snippet) — [CB Insights: VNTANA](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/vntana/customers); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2229696)
- Snap Shopping Lens structure: "A Shopping Lens consists of the Lens (which contains the AR product experience) and the Product Catalog ... linked together through a Product Tagging process that happens after Lens Submission in the Snapchat Business Account" (snippet) — [Snap: Surface Objects shopping template](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/sponsored/sponsored-lens-templates/shopping/surface-objects)
- Snap Wristwear Try-On template: "you will need the Wrist Binding Custom Component, a Wrist Occluder asset, and a 3D model that will be used as wristwear"; template has Left Wrist / Right Wrist children each with Watch and Bracelet objects and "an Occluder SceneObject that prevents the user from seeing the side of the wristwear 3D object that is not facing the camera" (snippet) — [Snap Wristwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/4.55.1/references/templates/object/Try-On/wristwear-try-on)
- Snap Footwear Try-On template: Foot Tracking custom component "handles how the shoe object attaches and tracks to a user's foot"; options "Add Default Occluder" (occluder mesh representing tracked feet), "Default Occluder Right/Left" (replace with custom mesh), "Occluder Material"; a Carousel to switch garments (snippet) — [Snap Footwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/references/templates/object/Try-On/foot-tracking)
- Perfect Corp ring try-on: "Merchants only need to upload images to easily create their AR Ring or Engagement Ring Try Ons" (vendor claim, snippet) — [Perfect Corp AR virtual ring](https://perfectcorp.com/business/products/ar-virtual-ring)
- Wanna publishes category-specific positioning guides for brands/3D artists (e.g., watches) and a known-issues page (index only; blocked) — [Wanna help center](https://wanna.helpjuice.com/watches-category/watches-positioning-guide)

### Inferences
- The merchant-facing contract in every platform is the asset, not an interactive fit tool: real-world scale in meters, Y-up, documented origin (base-center for placement objects; vendor-specific reference pose for wearables). Symspace's operator tool should therefore collect, at minimum: category, real-world dimensions (ring inner diameter / watch case diameter & lug-to-lug / glasses total width & lens height / shoe EU size or length), handedness/side, and a confirmed up/forward axis — then compute the initial transform from those numbers instead of trusting the AI-generated GLB's units.
- Snap's template design (reference occluder mesh per body part, left/right instances, binding component with the item parented under it) is the closest public precedent for Symspace's "reference hand/face/foot" authoring: ship a reference mesh per category in the authoring mode and store the item's local transform relative to the binding node.
- Because Shopify/Zakeke do not let merchants place items on avatars, a Symspace web tool with a reference hand/face/foot would be a differentiator but is not required for parity; the interim can be an in-app authoring mode (KQ6).

- Could not read Snap's AR asset requirements for Supplemental Feeds (file type, size, scale, pivot) or the Lens Studio binding components' exact position/scale properties (pages blocked; search budget exhausted).
- No details found for Threekit, Emersya, Sayduck, VNTANA, Dopple on whether merchants enter dimensions or anchor points; only marketing-level descriptions surfaced.
- Whether Shopify's model-viewer `ar-scale` attribute (auto/fixed) is exposed to merchants was not verified (no source fetched); treat as unverified.
- Wanna's positioning-guide specifics (reference wrist, pivot, strap orientation) are unknown.

## KQ3. Cheap, accurate per-user body calibration (ring, wrist, shoe, PD/face width)

### Takeaway
The cheapest accurate inputs are (a) TrueDepth/ARKit-based PD (independent study: ~0.5 mm MAE for Warby Parker's app vs a pupillometer), (b) on-screen rulers calibrated with a credit card (ring/wrist; vendor claims only), and (c) foot length from a photo with an A4 sheet (academic prototypes report ~1.5–3.4 mm error depending on camera angle; commercial apps are generally judged inadequate for custom footwear). Nike's "within 2 mm" claim for AR foot scanning is a vendor claim with no independent validation found.

### Cited Findings

**Pupillary distance / glasses**
- Cureus 2023 study, 44 subjects, three smartphone apps vs a trained examiner with a digital pupillometer: "The Eye Measure and Warby Parker applications had significantly lower mean absolute error (MAE) of IPD measurements (0.511364 mm) compared to the PDCheck AR application (1.375 mm). The Warby Parker application most frequently obtained accurate IPD measurements within multiple accuracy ranges" (snippet; independent) — [PMC10389117](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10389117/); [Cureus PDF](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/169122/20230731-26144-h2ws3k.pdf)
- A 2024 study compared IPD from pupillometer, PD ruler and autorefractor with a non-contact mobile app (snippet; details not read) — [PMC11654209](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654209)
- ARKit `ARFaceAnchor.leftEyeTransform` / `rightEyeTransform` are `simd_float4x4`; "The translation aspect of each matrix indicates the position of the center of the eyeball, relative to the position represented by the anchor's transform. The positive z-axis points from the center of the eyeball in the direction of the pupil." (snippet of Apple docs) — [Apple: rightEyeTransform](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/righteyetransform.md); [Apple: leftEyeTransform](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/lefteyetransform.md)
- Warby Parker's TrueDepth PD tool: "no card or ruler necessary" (vendor) — [Warby Parker app page](https://ca.warbyparker.com/app)
- Zenni/Ditto calibration: credit card held to forehead or PD entry (vendor) — [Zenni FAQ](https://www.zennioptical.com/c/faq?count=1)

**Ring / wrist**
- Perfect Corp on-screen finger/wrist ruler (described in KQ1); output "finger size in millimeters" convertible to regional ring sizes (vendor) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230524005034/en); [Perfect Corp ring page](https://perfectcorp.com/business/products/ar-virtual-ring)
- Consumer ring-sizer apps: methods include "placing a ring on the screen, measuring a finger on the screen, using a camera with a bank card for scale, or following guided steps for string/paper measurement"; screen calibration "with any standard payment card"; one photo-based app states "Without a reference card, the algorithm uses average hand proportions (±1 size), but with a credit card visible, precision improves to ±0.5 sizes" (vendor claims, snippets; app attribution ambiguous) — [SizeMyRing listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/sizemyring-ring-sizer/6791215499); [ringsize.ai](https://ringsize.ai/en/measure); [Camweara Ring Sizer](https://camweara.com/?p=19838)

**Foot / shoe**
- Nike Fit (announced May 2019; vendor claim): scans "collecting 13 data points mapping your foot morphology for both feet within a matter of seconds"; "accuracy within 2 millimetres"; "A scan can take less than 15 seconds"; matches to "the internal volume already known for each of its shoes, and the purchase data of people with similar-sized feet" (snippets) — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/nike-unveils-ar-feature-to-help-customers-find-the-perfect-shoe-fit/2019051043102); [Tech Advisor](https://www.techadvisor.com/article/734167/nike-launches-nike-fit-to-counteract-ill-fitting-footwear.html); [designboom](https://www.designboom.com/technology/nike-fit-app-uses-ar-to-measure-feet-with-perfect-accuracy-05-10-2019/)
- JMIR mHealth uHealth scoping review (March 2021) of 26 foot-measurement apps: "All apps failed to meet even half of the measurement-specific criteria required for the proper manufacturing of custom-made footwear"; overall measurement functionality "mean of 1.93 out of 5"; "Only 23% (6/26) of the apps reportedly used external scanners or advanced algorithms to reconstruct 3D models" (snippet; independent) — [PMC7974751](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7974751/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33661124/)
- A4-sheet reference algorithm (foot in black sock, 3 photos): "if the angle is 10° the measurement error is equal to 1.5 mm, but if the angle is 15 degrees the error becomes 3.4 mm" (snippet; academic) — [PSU (Belarus) paper PDF](https://elib.psu.by/bitstream/123456789/31131/1/191-195.pdf)
- Web-based A4-reference system (UNIMAS): "Mean Absolute Error of just 0.18 EU sizes based on 10 tested samples—80% of which were exact matches, and 90% within ±0.5 size, with best results at 40 cm phone height, angled at 90°" (snippet; tiny sample) — [UNIMAS ScholarHub](https://scholarhub.unimas.my/handle/123456789/348)
- OptiFit (Sensors, Dec 2022): measured "length, width, arch height, and instep girth with an accuracy of 95.23%, 96.54%, 89.14%, and 99.52%, respectively" (snippet; academic) — [PMC9739363](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9739363/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/27ff44e433ac4a2090627f3e2480173d)

### Inferences
- Glasses: Symspace can compute an IPD proxy directly from the ARKit face anchor it already uses (distance between `leftEyeTransform` and `rightEyeTransform` translations). Note this is eyeball-center distance, not pupil distance at a given vergence; expect a systematic offset vs. clinical PD. Given the Cureus result (~0.5 mm MAE for TrueDepth-based apps), a TrueDepth-derived IPD and face width (from face-mesh extents at the temples) is accurate enough to scale glasses by total width without any user input; offer a manual PD/frame-width entry as an override only.
- Rings/wrists: hand-landmark-based sizing from a monocular Vision plugin lacks absolute scale; the pragmatic path is Perfect Corp's pattern (on-screen ruler calibrated by a payment card, or a photo with a card next to the finger) producing a finger diameter in mm, which both sets ring scale and is the field to persist per user.
- Shoes: ARKit body joints give coarse foot position/orientation, not length; a one-time shoe-size entry (EU/US) is the cheapest reliable input and maps to a last length table; an A4-photo measurement is a later upgrade with documented angle sensitivity.
- Store measurements (not raw scans) per user, behind explicit consent, and version them by method (entered / card-calibrated / TrueDepth) so the flywheel can weight corrections by input quality.

### Gaps
- No independent validation of Nike Fit's 2 mm claim or of Perfect Corp's on-screen rulers was found.
- No source on accuracy of hand size from MediaPipe/Vision landmarks with depth (LiDAR/TrueDepth); search budget exhausted before this query.
- Face-width-from-ARKit-mesh accuracy numbers were not found.

## KQ4. Telemetry and the correction flywheel (adjustments → gold set and per-category defaults)

### Takeaway
The "data flywheel"/human-in-the-loop literature gives the architecture (deploy → capture user corrections as structured records → aggregate → retrain/redeploy), and crowd-labeling statistics give the aggregation rule for continuous corrections (median or robust estimators rather than means). No published AR-commerce paper was found that learns product-fit offsets from shopper adjustments; the closest academic work learns UI-placement priors from crowdsourced layouts.

### Cited Findings
- Data flywheel definition: "continued data collection and user-supplied labels allow models to be improved over time, the so-called 'data flywheel' effect" (snippet) — [arXiv 2104.11757, "Becoming Good at AI for Good"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11757); "a data flywheel connects real-world use directly to continuous model improvement" (snippet) — [Ultralytics glossary](https://www.ultralytics.com/glossary/data-flywheel)
- Correction-record pattern: "When a human saves their correction, a separate, self-contained CorrectionRecord is created that is purpose-built for fine-tuning, storing a clean copy of the original input, the AI's bad attempt, and the human's 'ground truth' correction as a perfect, portable training example" (snippet; practitioner blog) — [fast.io: AI agent data flywheel](https://www.fast.io/resources/ai-agent-data-flywheel.md)
- HITL in MLOps: "productionized models can be improved iteratively by adopting Human-in-the-loop pipeline (Active learning strategy and human annotation) in an MLOps lifecycle" (snippet; conference talk abstract) — [The Fifth Elephant MLOps conference](https://hasgeek.com/fifthelephant/mlops-conference/sub/building-human-in-the-loop-pipeline-in-mlops-7dwbhBAGSDCjDCyR4wwSvp)

### Inferences (design proposal grounded in the above)
- Log one `FitCorrection` record per settle event (user stops adjusting for N seconds, or taps "looks right"/"add to bag"), modelled on the CorrectionRecord pattern: `{sku, category, assetVersion, configVersion, device, os, trackerType (facemesh|visionHand|bodyJoints|plane), landmarkFrameId, initialTransform (from config), finalTransform (relative to landmark frame: position m, rotation quat, uniform scale), deltaTransform, userMeasurement {ringMm|wristMm|shoeEU|pdMm|faceWidthMm, method}, timeToSettleMs, numGestures, abandoned bool, reported bool, sessionId, userId(hashed)}`.
- Express corrections in the *landmark frame* (ring axis along finger bone, watch on wrist-center frame, glasses on the face-anchor frame, shoes on ankle→toe frame) so they are comparable across users and devices; normalise translation by the user's measurement (e.g., mm per unit finger diameter) before aggregating.
- Aggregate per SKU with the median (and MAD) over users, and per category with the median of per-SKU medians; the crowd-labeling literature supports median over mean for outlier-heavy continuous labels. Require a minimum N (e.g., 5 distinct users) and a dispersion bound (e.g., MAD of scale < 5%, position < 2 mm) before auto-promoting a per-SKU default; otherwise route to a reviewer queue. Weight records by measurement quality (card-calibrated > typed > none) and down-weight abandoned sessions.
- Use the accepted per-SKU defaults as the gold set for the automated configurator: input = GLB + category + dimensions; target = the accepted transform. Per-category medians of the delta become the prior ("default offset") applied to new SKUs before any user data exists.
- Keep the "report a fit problem" event separate from silent adjustments: it is the high-precision label (user says wrong) and should both trigger review and count as a negative example for the current default.

### Gaps
- No AR-commerce source describing per-SKU default learning from shopper adjustments; the design above is inference from general flywheel + crowd-aggregation literature.
- The NVIDIA data-flywheel numbers could not be traced to a primary source; treat as unverified.
- No published guidance on confidence thresholds (N, dispersion) for auto-applying crowd-derived continuous corrections; the numbers above are proposals, not sourced.

## KQ5. Unity implementation patterns (XRI 3.x on mobile AR, constraints, persistence, remote config)

### Takeaway
XRI 3.x already provides the full touchscreen manipulation stack Symspace needs (TouchscreenGestureInputController → Screen Space Select/Rotate/Pinch-Scale inputs → XR Ray Interactor with AR raycasting → `ARTransformer` for plane-anchored objects, or `XRGeneralGrabTransformer` with per-axis constraints for body-anchored ones), including built-in min/max scale clamps with elastic "snap-back", and smoothing/ease-in on `XRGrabInteractable`. Persistence should split into device-local convenience (PlayerPrefs) and shared, reviewable state (Firestore), with Firebase Remote Config supplying per-category defaults, feature flags and A/B variants.

### Inferences (implementation pattern for Symspace, XRI 3.2.1 / AR Foundation 6.2)
- Placement items (floor/table): use the stock chain `Screen Space Ray Interactor` → `XRGrabInteractable` + `ARTransformer`, set `objectPlaneTranslationMode` to Horizontal/Vertical per category, enable `enablePlaneClassificationFilter` with `planeClassifications` (Floor/Table) from AR Foundation 6, and tighten `minScale`/`maxScale` (e.g., 0.9–1.1) when SKU dimensions are trusted; the built-in `elasticity` + lerp-back is the "snap-back" UX. Add a double-tap "true size" reset mirroring AR Quick Look.
- Wearables (ring/watch/glasses/shoe): the item is parented under a landmark-driven anchor transform (face anchor, hand-landmark frame, ankle joint). Do not use `ARTransformer` (plane raycasts are irrelevant); instead use `XRGrabInteractable` with `XRGeneralGrabTransformer` and set `permittedDisplacementAxes` to the single allowed axis (slide along finger/wrist/temple), `constrainedAxisDisplacementMode` object-relative, `allowTwoHandedScaling` false, `allowOneHandedScaling` true with `clampScaling` and `minimumScaleRatio`/`maximumScaleRatio` around the calibrated size, and ignore rotation except roll about the attachment axis via a custom `IXRGrabTransformer`. Because direct on-object manipulation of a small moving target is hard (ISMAR 2014/VRST 2018 evidence), expose the same three degrees of freedom as on-screen sliders/steppers that write to the same anchor-relative offset.
- Persist in two tiers: PlayerPrefs for the *current* per-SKU-per-user offset (instant, offline); Firestore for `users/{uid}/fitOverrides/{sku}` (cross-device) and `fitCorrections` telemetry (KQ4). Treat PlayerPrefs as a cache that is reconciled from Firestore on sign-in.
- Remote Config holds `fit_defaults_v{N}` as a JSON parameter per category (e.g., `{"ring":{"offset":[0,0,0],"scale":1.0,"axis":"fingerBone"}}`), plus feature flags `fit_manual_enabled`, `fit_auto_apply_crowd_defaults`, and A/B variants of the default offsets; conditions by app version and platform. Version the schema explicitly in the key name and in a `schemaVersion` field so a client on an older build ignores newer templates. Per-SKU overrides should not live in Remote Config (parameter/size limits apply; not verified here) but in Firestore `skus/{sku}/fitDefault`, fetched with the product.
- Smoothing: apply `Smooth Position Amount`/`Tighten Position`-style exponential smoothing to the *landmark* pose, not to the user's offset, so that adjustments feel immediate while tracker jitter is damped.

### Gaps
- Exact Remote Config parameter/value-size limits and JSON-type support were not verified (docs blocked); treat as known-but-unverified.

- The XRI 3.2.1-specific documentation pages were not fetched; property names were verified on the package master branch and the changelog shows they predate 3.2.x, but minor default-value changes between 3.2.1 and master cannot be ruled out.

## KQ6. Operational interim: concierge configuration by staff, and the "report a fit problem" loop

### Takeaway
No vendor publishes time-per-SKU for manual AR placement; what is public is that Snap, Wanna and Perfect Corp all rely on a human (creator, 3D artist, or vendor team) authoring the item against a reference body part or guide before the shopper ever sees it. A device-side authoring mode that reuses Symspace's own manipulation stack and saves the anchor-relative transform as the SKU default is consistent with these precedents and feeds the KQ4 flywheel directly.

### Cited Findings
- Snap's templates require the creator to place the 3D model under the binding component with left/right instances and occluders (desktop authoring before publication) — [Snap Wristwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/4.55.1/references/templates/object/Try-On/wristwear-try-on); [Snap Footwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/references/templates/object/Try-On/foot-tracking)
- Wanna publishes brand-facing positioning guides per category and known-issues/QA pages, implying a human QA step per asset — [Wanna help center](https://wanna.helpjuice.com/watches-category/watches-positioning-guide)
- Snap's Lens Web Builder claim of building a beauty Shopping Lens "in as fast as two minutes" is the only time figure found, and applies to makeup, not 3D fit (vendor claim) — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2022/01/snapchat-rolls-out-catalog-powered-shopping-lenses)
- Threekit onboarding is handled "with the help of their team or partners" (competitor's claim) — [Zakeke vs Threekit](https://www.zakeke.com/?p=60411)

### Inferences
- Concierge mode = the same `XRGrabInteractable`/transformer path as the consumer mode, gated by a staff role flag, with a "Save as SKU default" button writing `skus/{sku}/fitDefault = {transform in landmark frame, authorMeasurement, device, assetVersion}` to Firestore. Record author's calibrated measurement so the default can be normalised to other users.
- Per-SKU time is dominated by asset fixes (pivot/scale/axis) rather than placement; a pre-step that reads glTF bounds and the merchant-entered dimensions to auto-rescale and re-pivot will cut most of the manual work. Treat time-per-SKU as an internal metric to measure in the first week, not a number to source externally.
- "Report a fit problem" should capture the same `FitCorrection` payload plus a reason enum (too big/small, wrong spot, wrong hand/side, floats/clips) and a screenshot with landmarks overlaid; route to a review queue; reviewer acceptance writes the new SKU default and marks the record as gold.

### Gaps
- No published time-per-SKU or staffing benchmarks for manual AR asset configuration were found.
- No public description of Wanna's or Perfect Corp's internal QA tooling beyond page titles.
