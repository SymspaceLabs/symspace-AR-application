# TRELLIS.2 anchors open image-to-3D for AR

As of October 2026 the best open-weight image-to-3D models for a commercial Unity AR app are **Microsoft TRELLIS.2** (Dec 2025, MIT, 4B parameters, native PBR, GLB export) and its pixel-aligned derivative **TencentARC Pixal3D** (SIGGRAPH 2026, MIT, built on the TRELLIS.2 backbone since May 2026), with **Meta SAM 3D Objects** (Nov 2025, SAM License) as the specialist for cluttered real-world photos and **Tencent Hunyuan3D-2.1** (Jun 2025) as the strongest open PBR texturing stack but one whose license excludes the EU, UK and South Korea and caps deployment at 1M monthly active users. Independent evidence shows the open leaders are within noise of each other on geometry and within ~20 ELO of closed products as of the last verifiable leaderboard snapshot, while the clear remaining gap is pipeline features (quad topology, auto-rigging, part separation), not mesh fidelity. Text conditioning does help after the image step, but not in the way the question implies: native text-to-3D models are weaker than image-conditioned ones by the admission of their own authors, so the production pattern is text → text-to-image → image-to-3D, with text re-entering as (a) a regenerated reference image fed to a mesh-conditioned PBR painter (Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1 or TRELLIS.2's shape-conditioned texturing), (b) feed-forward instruction editors in TRELLIS latent space (Steer3D, Nano3D), and (c) the only quantitative evidence of joint text+image conditioning beating image-only (TIGON, CVPR 2026), which has no released weights. For Symspace, the decision-ready stack is Pixal3D or TRELLIS.2 on a 24 GB Linux GPU server, an Apache-2.0 text-to-image front end (Z-Image-Turbo or FLUX.1 schnell), MIT background removal (BiRefNet), decimation to 10k–50k triangles with 1K–2K KTX2 textures via glTF-Transform, runtime loading through Unity's official glTFast, and offline GLB→USDZ conversion only where iOS Quick Look is needed outside the app.

## Two MIT models define the open frontier, with four useful alternates

The open-weight frontier moved exactly twice in the past year, and both moves came from the same lineage. **TRELLIS.2**, released 16 December 2025 and accepted as a CVPR 2026 oral, is a 4-billion-parameter model built on "O-Voxel", a field-free sparse-voxel representation paired with a sparse 3D VAE that downsamples 16× spatially, so a 1024³ textured asset compresses to roughly 9.6K latent tokens ([TRELLIS.2 GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [arXiv 2512.14692](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14692v1)). It is the first open model to emit a full PBR set natively, including **base color, roughness, metallic and opacity**, and it exports GLB directly with optional WebP texture compression. On an H100 it generates a 512³ asset in about **3 s (2 s shape + 1 s material), 1024³ in ~17 s and 1536³ in ~60 s**; Microsoft requires an NVIDIA GPU with at least 24 GB and has tested only on Linux with CUDA 12.4 ([TRELLIS.2 README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Code, weights, texture-generation code and training code are all released under MIT, with only the nvdiffrast/nvdiffrec dependencies under separate NVIDIA terms ([TRELLIS.2 README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). Its known defects, documented by 2026 follow-up papers that build on it, are that meshes "occasionally contain holes, since it does not enforce a watertight-mesh assumption" and that textures "exhibit a color-shifting artifact, which could result in the generation of metallic surfaces for transparent objects" ([arXiv 2607.00382](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00382); [SymTRELLIS, arXiv 2606.04108](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04108)). TRELLIS.2 also has the healthiest maintenance signal of any open model: the repository was last pushed in July 2026 and had 11,487 stars at the 7 October 2026 GitHub API snapshot, and ComfyUI now ships native core nodes for it ([GitHub API snapshot via benchmarks note](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [ComfyUI blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/trellis2-and-pixal3d-are-now-native)).

**Pixal3D** ("Pixel-Aligned 3D Generation from Images", SIGGRAPH 2026, arXiv 2605.10922) is the newest frontier-quality open release and the one most worth evaluating first. Its contribution is conditioning rather than representation: instead of injecting image features only through attention, it explicitly back-projects pixel features into 3D to establish direct pixel-to-3D correspondences, targeting "near-reconstruction-level fidelity with detailed geometry and PBR textures" ([Pixal3D GitHub](https://github.com/TencentARC/Pixal3D)). The main branch is an improved implementation on the TRELLIS.2 backbone released in May 2026, while a `paper` branch preserves the original Direct3D-S2-based implementation; the three-stage cascade runs sparse structure (32→64), shape (256→512→1024) and texture (256→512→1024), outputs GLB with PBR, supports 1536 resolution in standard mode and a low-VRAM mode at 1024 with on-demand model loading, and gained **multi-view inference in September 2026** ([Pixal3D GitHub](https://github.com/TencentARC/Pixal3D)). Code is MIT with third-party components retaining their licenses via a NOTICE file, weights are on Hugging Face as `TencentARC/Pixal3D`, and the ecosystem already includes native ComfyUI support, a community wrapper, an Apple Silicon port and fal.ai hosting ([Pixal3D GitHub](https://github.com/TencentARC/Pixal3D); [Pixal3D-mac](https://github.com/pawel-mazurkiewicz/Pixal3D-mac)). The honest caveat is that no VRAM figures, generation times, parameter count or independent benchmark against TRELLIS.2 were retrievable; the fidelity claim rests on the authors' paper and should be verified on Symspace's own product images.

Two models from outside the TRELLIS lineage remain genuinely important. **Hunyuan3D-2.1** (released 13 June 2025) pairs a 3.3B flow-matching shape DiT with a 2B PBR paint model producing albedo, metallic and roughness maps, needs **10 GB VRAM for shape, 21 GB for texture and 29 GB end-to-end**, and is the last open Hunyuan3D weight drop: versions 2.5, PolyGen, 3.0 and 3.1 are hosted-only, and a community issue asking about open-sourcing them has sat unanswered since 17 July 2025 ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1); [Issue #111](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/issues/111)). Its paint model is still widely regarded as the strongest open texturing stage, and Tencent's own table shows Paint-2.1 improving CLIP-FID from 26.44 to 24.78 and CMMD from 2.318 to 2.191 over Paint-2.0 ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/README.md)). Tencent also shipped **Hunyuan3D-Omni** (25 Sep 2025, 3.3B, 10 GB VRAM) which adds point-cloud, voxel, bounding-box and skeletal-pose control through a unified encoder, and **Hunyuan3D-Part** for native 3D part segmentation and completion ([Hunyuan3D-Omni GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Omni); [Hunyuan3D-Part GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Part)). **SAM 3D Objects** (Meta, 19 Nov 2025) is a different kind of model: it takes a single image plus an object mask, predicts shape, texture and object pose/layout, and is tuned for "occlusion and clutter" in real scenes rather than clean product renders; Meta claims at least a 5:1 human-preference win rate on objects over prior models, and on the clean-object ISO3D set its Uni3D score of 0.3707 versus TRELLIS's 0.3698 is a statistical tie ([sam-3d-objects GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects); [Roboflow summary](https://blog.roboflow.com/sam-3d/); [SAM 3D review](https://liner.com/review/sam-3d-3dfy-anything-in-images)). Its documented export is a Gaussian-splat PLY, mesh/GLB export is not documented in the README, and Meta added encoder weights on 1 June 2026 and an artist-mesh leaderboard the next day ([sam-3d-objects README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects/blob/main/README.md)).

The practical second tier is geometry-only or capacity-limited. **TripoSG** (VAST, Mar 2025) is a 1.5B rectified-flow transformer over an SDF VAE, MIT-licensed, runs in 8 GB, exports GLB with a face-count limit and produces no texture ([TripoSG GitHub](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSG)). **Step1X-3D** (StepFun, May 2025) is fully Apache-2.0 with a 1.3B watertight-TSDF geometry stage and a 3.5B SD-XL texture stage, but needs 27–29 GB and about 152 s per asset and has had no release since v1.0.1 on 14 May 2025 ([Step1X-3D GitHub](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step1X-3D); [Step1X-3D releases](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step1X-3D/releases)). **Direct3D-S2** (DreamTech, NeurIPS 2025) reaches 1024³ via spatial sparse attention, is MIT, but outputs untextured OBJ and needs ~24 GB at the resolution its authors actually recommend ([Direct3D-S2 GitHub](https://github.com/DreamTechAI/Direct3D-S2)). Stability's **SF3D** and **SPAR3D** are the lightest "game-engine-ready" generators, with UV unwrapping, delighting, material parameters and quad/triangle remeshing to target counts at 6–10.5 GB, but sit under a revenue-capped license ([SF3D GitHub](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-fast-3d); [SPAR3D GitHub](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-point-aware-3d)). The 2024 multi-view-plus-LRM generation (InstantMesh, CRM, LGM, Unique3D, Wonder3D) is superseded on quality and in InstantMesh's case inherits Zero123++'s CC-BY-NC weight license ([Zero123++ GitHub](https://github.com/SUDO-AI-3D/zero123plus)). Models often marketed as open but not usable commercially include NVIDIA PartPacker (non-commercial source license), Roblox Cube/CubePart (Research-Only RAIL-MS), Sparc3D (never released; now Hitem3D's proprietary model) and ByteDance Seed3D (API only) ([PartPacker license](https://github.com/NVlabs/PartPacker/blob/main/license.md); [Roblox/cube LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/cube/main/LICENSE); [Sparc3D issue #22](https://github.com/lizhihao6/Sparc3D/issues/22)).

## Independent benchmarks show a near-tie among open leaders and parity with closed models on clean inputs

The single largest independent human-preference dataset is Hugging Face's 3D Arena, which by its June 2025 paper had collected **123,243 votes from 8,096 users across 19 models**; that snapshot placed closed CSM/Cube first at 1405 ELO, TRELLIS-3DGS second at 1384, TRELLIS mesh at 1306 and Hunyuan3D-2 at 1298 ([3D Arena paper, arXiv 2506.18787](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18787v1)). Two methodological findings from that paper matter more than the ranking: Gaussian-splat outputs carry a **+16.6 ELO advantage over meshes** and textured models a **+144.1 ELO advantage over untextured**, and the same TRELLIS model gains +78 ELO merely from being rendered as splats ([3D Arena paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18787v1)). ELO on 3D Arena is therefore a poor proxy for AR mesh usability, and the ~8 ELO gap between TRELLIS and Hunyuan3D-2 is noise. The research team could not open the live arena from the sandbox, so October 2026 standings for TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D or SAM 3D Objects are unverified; worse, the "May 2026" rankings circulating on SEO sites reproduce the June 2025 values (1306/1298) verbatim while relabelling TRELLIS as "TRELLIS 2", so they should be disregarded ([triposplat.com, flagged](https://triposplat.com/blog/best-3d-ai-generators-2026)).

Academic scoring benchmarks tell a consistent story. Hi3DEval (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks) scores image-to-3D models on geometry plausibility, geometry detail, texture quality, geometry-texture consistency and prompt alignment; its object-level table places the never-released Hunyuan3D 2.5 first at 16.56 overall, Hunyuan3D 2.0 at 16.20, TRELLIS at 15.20 and SPAR3D at 15.00, with TRELLIS actually leading on prompt alignment (3.50 vs 3.47) despite losing on texture ([Hi3DEval, arXiv 2508.05609](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05609v1)). Cue3D (NeurIPS 2025) measured geometry directly and found TRELLIS slightly ahead of Hunyuan3D-2 on Chamfer distance and F-score on both GSO (39.64 vs 41.82 CD×1000) and Toys4K (37.78 vs 38.65), while Hunyuan3D-2 was better on symmetry (0.894 vs 0.867), concluding that native 3D generators "clearly outperform other methods across both datasets" ([Cue3D, arXiv 2511.22121](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22121v1)). Tencent's own ULIP/Uni3D shape table confirms the compression at the top: Hunyuan3D-Shape-2.1 (Uni3D-I 0.3213), Step1X-3D (0.3195), TripoSG (0.3129) and TRELLIS (0.3116) differ only in the second and third decimal, with Direct3D-S2 (0.2930) clearly behind ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/README.md)).

Vendor studies for the newest models are large but domain-specific. Microsoft's TRELLIS.2 paper reports CLIP similarity of 0.894 versus 0.869 for Hunyuan3D 2.1 and 0.876 for TRELLIS v1, and a ~40-participant user study on 100 AI-generated image prompts in which **TRELLIS.2-4B was preferred 66.5% of the time versus 13.3% for Hunyuan3D 2.1** ([TRELLIS.2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14692v1)). Meta's SAM 3D paper reports preference rates above 80% against TRELLIS and Hunyuan3D on in-the-wild sets drawn from robotic-manipulation and egocentric photos, yet essentially ties TRELLIS on the clean ISO3D set ([SAM 3D paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16624); [SAM 3D review](https://liner.com/review/sam-3d-3dfy-anything-in-images)). Both results are plausible and both are conditional: TRELLIS.2 was judged on clean synthetic inputs, SAM 3D on cluttered real ones, which maps directly onto the two input types an AR commerce app will see (catalogue renders vs user photos). The only independent head-to-head of the two open leaders at equal output size comes from a Japanese hobbyist on an AMD Ryzen AI MAX+ 395: at a 40k-polygon budget across nine images, **TRELLIS.2 averaged 306.7 s with 8.65 GiB peak memory versus Hunyuan3D-2.1 at 637.0 s and 28.80 GiB**, with quality called a draw ([hawkymisc blog](https://hawkymisc.github.io/blog/trellis2-vs-hunyuan3d-gfx1151.html)). That 8.65 GiB figure, consistent with community ComfyUI claims of 6–8 GB operation via GGUF quantisation, shows Microsoft's 24 GB requirement is a worst-case 1536³ number and that a 12–16 GB card is realistic at 512³–1024³ ([trellis2.app low-VRAM guide](https://trellis2.app/blog/trellis-2-low-vram)).

On open versus closed, the evidence points to parity on raw single-object fidelity and a persistent gap on pipeline features. In June 2025 the open/closed gap on 3D Arena was 21 ELO ([3D Arena paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18787v1)). Since then closed vendors shipped Hunyuan3D 3.0/3.1 (1536³, hosted only), Meshy 7 (Aug 2026, "geometry alignment"), Tripo P1.0/H3.1/P2.0 (P2.0 on 21 Sep 2026 claiming native quad meshes) and Rodin Gen-2, while the open frontier moved once with TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D ([Tencent press release](https://www.tencent.com/en-us/articles/2202235.html); [PR Newswire Meshy 7](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20813/release/20260812EN24698); [TechTimes Tripo P1.0](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/315291/20260319/tripo-ai-debuts-production-grade-native-3d-diffusion-model-following-gdc-2026-showcase.htm)). Every source that praises topology names a closed product; every open model, including TRELLIS.2 and Hunyuan3D-2.1, emits dense marching-cubes triangles that need decimation or retopology before mobile use. The open retopology options are real but capped: LATO.2 (Meshy, Jul 2026, MIT) rebuilds a mesh at 200–5,000 vertices in ~5 s on 8 GB but warns outputs "may still contain holes and incorrect connectivity"; DeepMesh (Apache-2.0) reaches ~30k faces; MeshRipple (CVPR 2026) ships 10k/20k-face weights with no stated license; MeshAnything V2 caps at 1,600 faces and "performs poorly with feed-forward 3D generation outputs" ([LATO.2 GitHub](https://github.com/LoHhhha/LATO.2); [DeepMesh GitHub](https://github.com/zhaorw02/DeepMesh); [MeshRipple GitHub](https://github.com/MayMhappy/MeshRipple); [MeshAnythingV2 GitHub](https://github.com/buaacyw/MeshAnythingV2)).

## Licensing, not quality, eliminates Hunyuan3D and Stability for a global app

For an AR product that may ship in Europe, the license text is decisive. The Tencent Hunyuan 3D 2.1 Community License defines its Territory as "worldwide territory, excluding the territory of the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea", requires that "if ... the monthly active users of all products or services made available by or for Licensee is greater than 1 million monthly active users in the preceding calendar month, You must request a license from Tencent" which Tencent "may grant ... in its sole discretion", forbids using outputs "to improve any other AI model", requires generated content to be labelled machine-generated, and requires a prominent disclosure that Tencent is not affiliated with the product ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/LICENSE)). The 2.0 license and Hunyuan3D-Omni's license carry the same territorial and MAU clauses ([Hunyuan3D-2 LICENSE](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE); [Hunyuan3D-Omni license summary](https://deepwiki.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Omni/6-reference)). A July 2026 report that "Hunyuan 3.0 switched to Apache 2.0, removing EU/UK/South Korea restrictions" refers to the Hunyuan LLM, not the 3D models, and should not be relied on ([aicoin news flash](https://www.aicoin.com/en/news-flash/2969704)). Whether routing Hunyuan3D through a hosted API (fal, Replicate, Tencent Cloud) removes the end-user territorial restriction is unresolved and needs counsel; the research found no primary source either way. The Stability AI Community License on SF3D/SPAR3D is free for commercial use only below USD 1M annual revenue, after which it terminates and an enterprise license is required, and demands "Powered by Stability AI" attribution ([SPAR3D LICENSE.md](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-point-aware-3d/blob/main/LICENSE.md)).

The clean set is substantial. TRELLIS, TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D, TripoSG, Direct3D-S2, Hi3DGen, PartCrafter, HoloPart, LATO.2 and VAST's SkinTokens auto-rigger are MIT; Step1X-3D, DeepMesh and InstantMesh are Apache-2.0; SAM 3D Objects is under the SAM License, which is a "non-exclusive, worldwide, non-transferable and royalty-free limited license" permitting commercial use with no territory exclusion or MAU threshold, but prohibiting ITAR/trade-control-restricted and military end uses, with patent-litigation termination and gated Hugging Face access ([sam-3d-objects LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects/main/LICENSE); [SkinTokens GitHub](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/SkinTokens)). Two sub-dependency traps deserve attention: TRELLIS.2 and TRELLIS rely on NVIDIA's nvdiffrast/nvdiffrec and modified FlexiCubes under their own terms, which is exactly why the Hi3DGen authors "specifically removed [TRELLIS's] dependencies on certain NVIDIA libraries (kaolin, nvdiffrast, flexicube) to ensure this adapted version can be used commercially" ([Hi3DGen README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Stable-X/Hi3DGen/main/README.md)); and rembg's default background-removal model, `bria-rmbg`, "is released under a BRIA license that requires a paid agreement for commercial use", so the pipeline should use BiRefNet (MIT, ~58 ms per 1024² image on an RTX 4090) or rembg's `isnet-general-use` instead ([rembg GitHub](https://github.com/danielgatis/rembg); [BiRefNet GitHub](https://github.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet)).

One risk is shared by every model on the list and cannot be solved by model choice. TRELLIS was trained on TRELLIS-500K curated from Objaverse(-XL), ABO, 3D-FUTURE, HSSD and Toys4K; TRELLIS.2 on the Objaverse-XL Sketchfab subset; Step1X-3D on 800K Objaverse/Objaverse-XL UIDs plus proprietary assets ([TRELLIS GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS); [TRELLIS.2 GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [Step1X-3D GitHub](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step1X-3D)). Objaverse scraped Sketchfab under mixed Creative Commons licenses without informing authors, Sketchfab's terms prohibit generative-AI use of user content, the dataset includes NoAI-tagged models, and at least one reported complaint alleges that commercial generative-3D systems were built on Objaverse-XL assets with copyright-management information stripped ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sketchfab-objaverse-ai-copyright-dispute-enters-third-dimension/); [P4SC4L summary](https://p4sc4l.beehiiv.com/p/defendants-allegedly-built-or-powered-commercial-generative-3d-systems-using-3d-assets-harvested-via)). The practical response is to document provenance, keep generated assets separable from licensed ones, and prefer models whose authors disclose their training sources.

## Text helps after the image step, but through images and latent editors rather than native text-to-3D

The direct answer to the second question is yes, text conditioning can improve the result after image-to-3D, but the mechanism matters, because native text-to-3D is the wrong tool. Microsoft ships four TRELLIS models including TRELLIS-text-xlarge (2.0B) and states verbatim that "it is always recommended to use the image conditioned version of the models for better performance" because "text-conditioned models are less creative and detailed due to data limitations" ([TRELLIS README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS)). Tencent's reference text-to-3D implementation is literally text-to-image followed by image-to-3D: the Hunyuan3D-2 Gradio app's `--enable_t23d` flag loads a distilled HunyuanDiT pipeline, calls `image = t2i_worker(caption)`, then runs the ordinary shape and paint stages ([Hunyuan3D-2 gradio_app.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2/main/gradio_app.py)). TRELLIS.2, Hunyuan3D-2.1, Pixal3D and Step1X-3D expose no text input at all, and Roblox Cube, the one 2025 native text-to-shape model with weights, outputs untextured OBJ under a research-only license ([TRELLIS.2 README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [Roblox/cube README](https://github.com/Roblox/cube)). The strongest research evidence that text has value alongside an image is TIGON (CVPR 2026), which reports that "image-conditioned models achieve high visual fidelity but suffer from viewpoint bias when the input view is limited or ambiguous, while text-conditioned models provide broad semantic guidance yet lack low-level visual detail", and that "even simple late fusion of text- and image-conditioned predictions outperforms single-modality models" ([TIGON, CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Cen_Text-Image_Conditioned_3D_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)). TIGON has no released code or weights, so its practical lesson is where text helps most: disambiguating the unseen back side of a single-view input, which today is achieved by multi-image conditioning (TRELLIS multi-image, Pixal3D multi-view, Hunyuan3D-2mv) or by a better reference image.

The most practical way to apply text to an existing image-to-3D mesh is **text-guided retexturing through a regenerated reference image**, because both production-grade open painters are mesh-plus-image conditioned. Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1 takes a `.glb` mesh and a `.png` reference, returns a textured GLB with albedo, metallic-roughness and normal maps at 512 or 768 resolution using 6–12 views, needs about 21 GB, and the top-level README explicitly shows `paint_pipeline(mesh_path, image_path=...)` on custom meshes ([hy3dpaint README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/hy3dpaint/README.md); [Hunyuan3D-2.1 README](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1)). TRELLIS.2 ships `example_texturing.py` for "shape-conditioned texture generation" that produces PBR maps for a given shape in roughly 1 s at 512³ to 25 s at 1536³ on an H100 ([TRELLIS.2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2/main/README.md)). The loop is therefore: render the mesh, edit that render with an instruction-following image editor driven by the user's prompt (Qwen-Image or FLUX Kontext for "make it brushed brass"), and re-run the painter on the unchanged geometry. This is exactly how VAST's SeqTex implements its own text mode, where `use_generated_img_cond=true` "enables SDXL/FLUX-based image conditioning from text" ([SeqTex README](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/SeqTex)). Native text-to-texture tools exist but are research-grade: Material Anything (CVPR 2025 Highlight, MIT) takes textureless meshes with optional text prompts and emits albedo, roughness, metallic and bump maps in UV space; Paint3D (Apache-2.0) produces 2K lighting-less UV albedo from text or image on SD 1.5; MVPaint and SyncMVD produce text-driven textures with reduced seams; SeqTex is the strongest but was tested on a single 80 GB A100 ([MaterialAnything README](https://github.com/3DTopia/MaterialAnything); [Paint3D README](https://github.com/OpenTexture/Paint3D); [MVPaint README](https://github.com/3DTopia/MVPaint); [SeqTex README](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/SeqTex)). Only Hunyuan3D-Paint 2.1 and TRELLIS.2 emit metallic-roughness plus normal maps inside a GLB that Unity's glTF importers consume without a baking step, which makes them the two realistic choices for Symspace.

**Text-guided geometry editing** of an existing asset has matured into a cluster of feed-forward editors operating in TRELLIS's structured-latent space, though none yet targets TRELLIS.2 or Hunyuan3D latents. Steer3D (Caltech, Dec 2025) augments image-to-3D with text steerability through a ControlNet-style adapter on TRELLIS, accepting an image plus an instruction such as "Remove the entire bottom base" or "Turn the entire cone into metallic silver texture", trained on 100k synthetic edit pairs and claimed 2.4–28.5× faster than competing editors, with code and weights released but no stated license and a non-standard stage-1 checkpoint ([Steer3D GitHub](https://github.com/ziqi-ma/Steer3D)). Nano3D (ICLR 2026, MIT) is training-free: it applies the instruction to the front-view render with Qwen-Image-Lightning, lifts the edit through TRELLIS with FlowEdit, and merges only the affected voxels so untouched regions are preserved; its 60 GB VRAM requirement comes from the Qwen-Image component rather than TRELLIS ([Nano3D README](https://github.com/JAMESYJL/Nano3D)). VoxHammer (Aug 2025) performs training-free inversion with a 3D mask, and Easy3E (CVPR 2026) performs single-pass geometric and appearance edits "directly in TRELLIS's sparse voxel latent space" but has no public code ([VoxHammer arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19247); [Easy3E, CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Hu_Easy3E_Feed-Forward_3D_Asset_Editing_via_Rectified_Voxel_Flow_CVPR_2026_paper.html)). Hunyuan3D-Omni offers the non-text alternative of regenerating a shape constrained by a point cloud sampled from the existing mesh, which is the most direct open way to "refine" a coarse geometry while the image fixes appearance, although this usage is inferred rather than documented ([Hunyuan3D-Omni README](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Omni)). The architectural implication for Symspace is that standardising on the TRELLIS lineage keeps the door open to every current text editor, whereas a Hunyuan-only pipeline does not.

Classic optimisation-based refinement is no longer worth the cost. threestudio (Apache-2.0) still implements Magic3D's NeRF→DMTet refinement, Fantasia3D with `shape_init=mesh`, and InstructNeRF2NeRF, typically needing 6–30 GB and "10,000+ iterations", but its last listed update is August 2024 and no 2025–2026 recipe couples SDS refinement to TRELLIS or Hunyuan outputs ([threestudio README](https://github.com/threestudio-project/threestudio)). BoostDream framed the trade-off in 2024, feed-forward speed versus SDS fidelity, but the field has since replaced coarse-to-fine optimisation with resolution scaling inside the feed-forward model itself: TRELLIS.2 goes from 512³ to 1536³ in 3–60 s ([BoostDream, IJCAI 2024](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/598); [TRELLIS.2 README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)). For a mobile asset pipeline, minutes-to-hours of per-asset SDS optimisation that reintroduces over-saturation and smoothing is dominated by a seconds-long retexture or a feed-forward edit.

## A GLB-first pipeline fits Unity AR Foundation; USDZ stays an offline side export

Every frontier model emits far more geometry and texture than a phone needs, so the pipeline is defined by post-processing. TRELLIS.2's README example decimates to a `decimation_target` of 1,000,000 faces with `texture_size=4096` and remeshing enabled, and Hunyuan3D-2.1 ships pymeshlab-based `FloaterRemover`, `DegenerateFaceRemover` and `FaceReducer` with a 40,000-face default plus normalisation to a unit sphere ([TRELLIS.2 README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2); [Hunyuan3D-2.1 postprocessors.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/hy3dshape/hy3dshape/postprocessors.py)). TRELLIS v1's `to_glb` is the most complete reference implementation: quadric decimation, invisible-face removal via multi-view rasterisation and graph mincut, pymeshfix hole repair, xatlas UV parametrisation, texture baking from 100 views at 1024–2048 px, and a Z-up to Y-up rotation before export ([TRELLIS postprocessing_utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TRELLIS/main/trellis/utils/postprocessing_utils.py)). The only official mobile budget located is Apple's, reaffirmed by Apple staff in November 2021: **100K polygons, one set of 2048×2048 textures and 10 s of animation** so that AR Quick Look loads on devices back to iPhone 7, with RealityKit apps having "a bit more wiggle room" ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/693867)). Android guidance is far tighter, in the hundreds to low thousands of polygons per mesh ([Android geometry optimisation](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/geometry?hl=en)). A 10k–50k triangle target with 1K–2K textures is the sensible band; because every model normalises to a unit cube or sphere, real-world scale is lost and the backend must rescale from product dimensions and move the pivot to the base of the bounding box before export.

Compression and delivery use standard glTF tooling. glTF-Transform (MIT) provides `simplify`, `resize --width 1024`, `uastc`/`etc1s` KTX2 encoding, `webp`, `draco` and `meshopt`, while gltfpack adds `-si` simplification, `-c`/`-cc` meshopt compression and `-tc` KTX2 conversion ([glTF-Transform](https://github.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform); [meshoptimizer gltfpack](https://github.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/blob/master/gltf/README.md)). On the Unity side, `com.unity.cloud.gltfast` is the official Unity glTF package under Apache-2.0, supports Draco, meshopt and KTX2/BasisU through the companion `com.unity.cloud.draco` and `com.unity.cloud.ktx` packages, targets URP/HDRP/Built-in and Android/iOS, and loads at runtime with `gameObject.AddComponent<GLTFast.GltfAsset>().url = ...`; version 6.15.0 (17 Nov 2025) is the latest concrete evidence, and Draco for Unity covers iOS arm64/armv7a and Android x86/arm ([com.unity.cloud.gltfast](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.cloud.gltfast); [glTFast README](https://github.com/atteneder/glTFast); [Draco for Unity prerequisites](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cloud.draco@5.1/manual/prerequisites.html)). Khronos's UnityGLTF (pure C#, 2.18.8 in Feb 2026) is the alternative with broader KHR_materials coverage and can coexist with glTFast ([UnityGLTF](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF)). Both Hunyuan3D-2.1 and TRELLIS.2 already write metallic-roughness glTF materials, which map directly onto URP Lit; TRELLIS v1, TripoSG and InstantMesh give base color or vertex colour only. AR Foundation 6.x on Unity 6 is current, ARCore Extensions support AR Foundation 4–6, and ARKit XR Plugin 6.4+ now requires Xcode 26 ([ARKit changelog](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.arkit@6.5/changelog/CHANGELOG.html); [Google ARCore docs](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/unity-arf/upgrade-to-ar-foundation-6)). One open verification item is whether glTFast handles TRELLIS.2's WebP-compressed textures (EXT_texture_webp) on mobile; re-encoding to KTX2 in the glTF-Transform pass sidesteps the question.

USDZ should be treated as a side export for iOS Quick Look or share sheets, not the app's runtime format, because Unity has no built-in runtime USDZ export and community requests remain unresolved ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/export-to-reality-format/766147)). Apple's Reality Converter converts OBJ/glTF/FBX/USD to USDZ with texture swapping and IBL preview, while the older Python `usdzconvert` has been retired from Apple's downloads; Blender's USD exporter and RapidCompact's CLI are the scriptable alternatives ([cgpress on Reality Converter](https://cgpress.org/archives/reality-converter-converts-3d-formats-to-usdz.html); [AOUSD forum on usdzconvert](https://forum.aousd.org/t/obj-gltf-to-usdz-usdzconvert-discontinued/1648); [RapidCompact CLI](https://www.rapidcompact.com/doc/cli/v03/Usdz/index.html)). Reported gotchas are specific: missing UV layouts make PBR materials render black on iOS, 8K textures break export, oversized files silently fail to display, and some Blender-exported USDZs appear white because textures are unlinked, so each USDZ should be validated in Reality Converter before publishing ([Apple forums 777035](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/777035); [Apple forums 107094](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/107094)).

Generation itself must be server-side; even the lightest usable model needs 8 GB of GPU memory. Hunyuan3D-2.1's own `api_server.py` demonstrates the right mobile pattern with `POST /send` returning a task id and `GET /status/{uid}` returning the GLB when done, with semaphore-limited concurrency ([Hunyuan3D-2.1 api_server.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/api_server.py)). Independent latency data put Hunyuan3D-2.1 at a **median 139 s across 900+ generations on RTX 4090s** and about 116 s on an L40S via Replicate, against TRELLIS.2's 3–60 s on an H100 and roughly 5 min at 40k polys on AMD integrated silicon ([SaladCloud](https://blog.salad.com/hunyuan3d-2-1/); [Replicate hunyuan3d-2](https://replicate.com/ndreca/hunyuan3d-2); [hawkymisc](https://hawkymisc.github.io/blog/trellis2-vs-hunyuan3d-gfx1151.html)). Third-party price summaries, which need re-verification against official pages, put self-hosted GPU time at roughly $0.01–0.10 per asset (RTX 4090 $0.34–0.69/h on RunPod; L40S $0.79–1.95/h; A100 80 GB $1.19–2.50/h) versus $0.06–0.35 per run on hosted endpoints (Replicate TRELLIS ~$0.058, fal TRELLIS.2 $0.25–0.35, Eachlabs Hunyuan3D 2.1 $0.30) ([deploybase RunPod pricing](https://deploybase.ai/articles/runpod-gpu-pricing); [cloudprice fal TRELLIS](https://cloudprice.net/models/fal-ai-trellis); [Eachlabs](https://www.eachlabs.ai/tencent/hunyuan-3d/hunyuan-3d-v2-1)). Multi-gigabyte weights make scale-to-zero cold starts long, so the sensible design is a queue-backed FastAPI or Modal worker with one warm instance, hosted endpoints for burst, GLB/KTX2 written to object storage behind a CDN, and the phone notified by push or polling rather than holding a two-minute HTTP request.

Input quality and the text front end close the loop. Community guidance that is consistent with how every training set is built is a single centred object on a plain background in a three-quarter view with even lighting at 512 px or more, with background removal called "the single biggest thing you can do to improve quality" ([3D AI Studio tutorial](https://www.3daistudio.com/blog/how-to-use-trellis-2-online-image-to-3d-tutorial)). When back-side fidelity matters, Hunyuan3D-2mv accepts front/back/left/right views 90° apart and TRELLIS offers tuning-free multi-image conditioning ([ComfyUI Hunyuan3D-2 docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/3d/hunyuan3d-2); [TRELLIS README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS)). For the text-to-image stage that turns a prompt into that reference image, the commercially safe choices are Apache-2.0: Z-Image-Turbo (Tongyi, 6B, Nov 2025, runs on 16 GB cards), Qwen-Image, FLUX.1 [schnell] and FLUX.2-klein 4B; by contrast FLUX.1 [dev], FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] and FLUX.2 [dev] are non-commercial without a paid Black Forest Labs license, and HunyuanImage 3.0 is an 80B MoE needing at least three 80 GB GPUs ([black-forest-labs/flux](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux); [comfyui-wiki Z-Image](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/models/z-image/z-image); [HunyuanImage-3.0](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanImage-3.0)). Note that FLUX Kontext [dev], the natural image editor for the text-guided retexture loop, is therefore excluded from production unless licensed; Qwen-Image's edit variant is the Apache-2.0 substitute. An automated QC gate in the worker should assert watertightness with trimesh or pymeshfix, count connected components, verify UVs and power-of-two textures, check the triangle count against the ceiling, rescale to real dimensions and seat the bounding box at y=0, since none of the models infers gravity or scale.

## Conclusion

The open image-to-3D field has consolidated around a single lineage: TRELLIS.2's sparse-voxel latents are now the substrate for Pixal3D, SymTRELLIS, Steer3D, Nano3D, Easy3E and most 2026 editing research, which means choosing it buys not just today's best MIT-licensed geometry and PBR but the entire emerging toolchain for text-guided refinement, much as choosing Hunyuan3D-2.1 buys a superior paint model attached to a frozen, geographically restricted line. The practical corollary for Symspace is that "text-to-3D after image-to-3D" should be designed as two image-mediated loops rather than one text-conditioned model: regenerate the reference image from the prompt and repaint the fixed mesh for material changes, and route shape instructions through a TRELLIS-space editor or a re-generation from an edited render, keeping the geometry pipeline MIT end to end.

Three uncertainties should shape the next steps. Pixal3D's fidelity advantage over TRELLIS.2 is unverified outside the authors' paper, so a head-to-head on 20–30 real Symspace product photos at a fixed 40k-triangle export is the single most valuable experiment. The independent leaderboards have not caught up with any post-2025 model, so quality decisions must rest on in-house tests rather than ELO. And the Objaverse provenance dispute hangs over every candidate equally, so the architecture should keep generated assets segregated and swappable, treating the generator as a replaceable component behind the GLB contract rather than a foundation the app depends on.
