# Fit, verify, render: automating Symspace AR assets

No open-source tool turns an AI-generated product mesh into an AR-ready asset, so the architecture Symspace needs is a server-side Python "AR configurator" that sits between the TRELLIS.2/Pixal3D generator chosen in the earlier report and the Unity client, and that emits every GLB together with a versioned sidecar JSON describing scale, pivot, front axis, placement category and attachment frame. The configurator is a composition of permissively licensed parts: a Qwen-VL-class vision-language model with constrained JSON decoding classifies the product photo into an AR mode, per-category anthropometric prior tables (ISO ring sizes, wrist and head circumferences, eyewear "52-18-140" widths, Mondopoint foot lengths) supply real-world scale because single-image metric size is formally ill-posed, Orient Anything (CC-BY-4.0) resolves the front axis from the input photo, and trimesh plus pyRANSAC-3D (Apache-2.0) compute pivots and attachment frames geometrically (ring hole axis by genus test and cylinder fit, glasses bridge by mirror-plane detection, shoe sole by plane fit). This reaches "no manual tuning" for rigid categories (furniture, décor, rings, watches, glasses, earrings, necklaces, hats, shoes) only when backed by a 50–100-asset-per-mode gold set and a three-layer QA battery that gates every asset into pass, review or fail; it does not reach it for garments, where every open auto-rigger (UniRig/SkinTokens, MIT) predicts arbitrary skeletons from body-less meshes it was never trained on, SMPL body models cost €1,500–25,000 a year, and the entire industry (Google, Perfect Corp, Walmart/Zeekit) has moved apparel try-on to 2D generative images, for which Leffa (MIT) is the only commercially clean open model. For video, the decision is to render first and generate second: a headless Blender turntable of the GLB is identity-perfect and costs about a cent, and uniqueness comes from Wan 2.2 VACE/Fun-Control (Apache-2.0) restyling that render under depth and edge control, with LTX-2.5 (free under US$10M revenue) for audio-bearing social cuts, ACE-Step for music and Kokoro or Chatterbox for voice, at roughly US$0.10–0.60 per 30-second video self-hosted on an RTX 4090. On the Unity side the stack stays on UnityGLTF (upgraded to 2.20), moves to AR Foundation 6.6, deletes the deprecated Unity MARS, and wraps ARKit face/body tracking, the existing iOS Vision hand plugin and a new Sentis- or MediaPipe-driven BlazeHand/BlazePose backend behind one landmark-provider interface so rings, watches and shoes work on Android, where ARCore offers no hand, body or human-occlusion tracking.

## A sidecar-config pipeline is the architecture because every AR platform pushes scale and pivot onto the asset

Every commerce AR surface that publishes a spec takes the same position: the asset must arrive correct, and the viewer exposes only a coarse placement flag. The Khronos 3D Commerce guidelines, co-authored with Wayfair and Target, require that "the size of the model must be 1:1 the size of the real item. In glTF 1 unit = 1 meter", recommend that "the bottom center of the product to be placed at 0,0,0", and note that "top-center for a ceiling-mounted product" is the exception ([Khronos RealtimeAssetCreationGuidelines](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3DC-Asset-Creation/blob/main/asset-creation-guidelines/RealtimeAssetCreationGuidelines.md)). Google's `<model-viewer>`, which Shopify uses, offers only `ar-placement` with values `floor | wall` ("the back (negative Z) of the object's bounding box will be placed against the wall") and `ar-scale` with `auto | fixed` ([model-viewer docs.json](https://github.com/google/model-viewer/blob/master/packages/modelviewer.dev/data/docs.json)). Apple's AR Quick Look anchors are horizontal, vertical, image, face and object, with an option to "disable content scaling so that users will always view the object at the correct and intended size" ([WWDC19 session 612](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/612)). Amazon's AR View requires a pivot at the origin, floor models resting on Y=0, a 200,000-triangle cap and a clean glTF-Validator pass, and warns that "incorrect dimensions ... or poor model orientation can delay approval" ([cgifurniture summary of Amazon spec](https://cgifurniture.com/blog/amazon-ar-view/)). None of these vendors documents inferring scale from imagery, and none of the commercial try-on SDKs does either: Snap's Ring Binding offers "14 positions to place a ring on" per hand on a tracked hand mesh and expects the ring mesh to be authored as a child with its pivot already correct ([Snap Ring Binding](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/ring-binding-component)). The automation Symspace wants is therefore exactly the step the industry leaves to artists, which is why no library provides it and why the configurator must be built in-house from geometry and vision primitives.

The generators make the problem concrete rather than abstract. TRELLIS exports a GLB after a fixed Z-up-to-Y-up rotation (`vertices @ [[1,0,0],[0,0,-1],[0,1,0]]`) with no centering or normalisation ([TRELLIS postprocessing_utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TRELLIS/main/trellis/utils/postprocessing_utils.py)), while Hunyuan3D-2 rescales every output to a unit-diameter sphere, and its 2.1 paper documents that training data is fit "within a unit cube centered at the origin" ([Hunyuan3D-2 postprocessors mirror](https://huggingface.co/spaces/kp-forks/Hunyuan3D-2/blame/main/hy3dgen/shapegen/postprocessors.py); [Hunyuan3D 2.1, arXiv 2506.15442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15442)). In both cases the mesh leaves the generator with **no metric scale, a generator-canonical (not product-canonical) orientation, and an arbitrary pivot**, so scale, orientation and pivot all have to be recovered downstream.

The right contract between pipeline and client is a versioned sidecar JSON (`{sku}.ar.json`) that is schema-validated in CI and mirrored verbatim into the glTF root `extras` so the GLB is self-describing. The glTF 2.0 spec permits application-specific `extras` on every object; unlike a vendor extension, extras need no `extensionsUsed` declaration, survive the glTF-Transform decimation and KTX2 passes described in the earlier report, and raise no validator warnings. On the Unity side, UnityGLTF exposes import plugins with per-node and per-scene callbacks (`OnAfterImportNode(Node node, int nodeIndex, GameObject nodeObject)`, `OnAfterImportScene`) that third-party plugins already use to "reconstruct components from extras data" ([UnityGLTF README](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF); [realvirtual import-plugin API](https://realvirtual.io/apidoc/classrealvirtual_1_1realvirtual_import_plugin_context.html)), and Unity's glTFast documents the equivalent "custom extras" Add-on API should the loader ever change ([glTFast UseCaseCustomExtras](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cloud.gltfast@6.9/manual/UseCaseCustomExtras.html)). The vocabulary should be a superset of the public ones (model-viewer's `floor|wall`, Apple's `horizontal|vertical|face`) so assets can be re-exported for web or Quick Look later without re-authoring. The schema the research converged on is:

```json
{
  "schema": "symspace.ar-config/1.0",
  "asset_id": "sku-123", "generator": {"name": "pixal3d", "version": "2026.09"},
  "units": "m", "coordinate_space": "gltf_rh_yup_front+z",
  "category": {"fine": "ring", "confidence": 0.93, "source": "qwen3-vl"},
  "ar_mode": "wear_hand_finger",
  "placement": {"anchor": "hand", "surface_offset_m": 0.0, "wall_normal": [0,0,-1],
                "allow_user_scale": false, "allow_user_rotate_y": true},
  "dimensions_m": {"x": 0.0205, "y": 0.0085, "z": 0.0205,
                   "source": "category_prior", "confidence": 0.6},
  "pivot": {"convention": "attachment", "baked": true},
  "attachment": {"target": "finger_proximal_ring", "offset_m": [0,0,0],
                 "rotation_quat_gltf": [0,0,0,1], "inner_diameter_m": 0.0173,
                 "mirror_for_right": true},
  "front_axis": "+z",
  "qa": {"status": "pass", "checks": {"watertight": true, "components": 1,
         "stable_base": true, "vlm_views_ok": true}, "gold_set_version": "2026-10-01"}
}
```

Two design rules follow from the Unity notes. First, bake the final transform into the vertices (`mesh.apply_transform`) and export with an identity root node, because both glTF-Transform and UnityGLTF preserve node transforms and a baked mesh keeps `Renderer.bounds` meaningful at load; if any parent node carries scale, a Unity "fit to size" script must read world-space `Renderer.bounds`, not `MeshFilter.sharedMesh.bounds`. Second, specify every vector in glTF space (right-handed, +Y up, +Z front) and convert once in Unity: UnityGLTF handles the left-handed conversion by negating X, so the attachment quaternion needs X and W negated on import, a detail that should be confirmed against `Runtime/Scripts/SchemaExtensions.cs` in the installed package since the researchers could not fetch the documentation. The `size_source` and `confidence` fields are not decoration: they drive `allow_user_scale`, mirroring model-viewer's `ar-scale="auto"` semantics, so the client unlocks pinch-resize only when the dimension came from a VLM estimate rather than client metadata or a locked prior.

## A VLM classifies, priors size, Orient Anything orients, and geometry pivots

The first decision for each upload is its AR mode, and the practical, proven route is a 2D vision-language model rather than a 3D classifier. Qwen-VL in Roboflow Workflows already supports "Structured Output Generation" returning JSON with specified fields alongside single- and multi-label classification, and Roboflow's own guidance is that "classification, depth, OCR, and VQA tasks benefit from Qwen3.5 models" ([Roboflow Qwen-VL block](https://docs.roboflow.com/workflows/blocks/blocks/run-a-model/qwen-vl); [Roboflow Qwen 3.6 API block](https://docs.roboflow.com/workflows/blocks/blocks/run-a-model/qwen3-6-api)). The failure mode is format, not semantics: a community benchmark of Qwen VLMs as zero-shot image-to-JSON labelers found that emitting "exactly a specific schema every time ... with no markdown fences is a different problem" and that grammar-constrained decoding "can lift the schema-valid rate by 2–27 points" ([qwen-vlmbench](https://huggingface.co/AbstractPhil/qwen-benchmark)), so the classifier must run under vLLM, llama.cpp GBNF or Outlines with the enumerated taxonomy (`floor_horizontal`, `table_horizontal`, `wall_vertical`, `ceiling`, `wear_hand_finger`, `wear_wrist`, `wear_face_glasses`, `wear_ears`, `wear_neck`, `wear_head_top`, `wear_feet`, `garment_body`) enforced as a grammar. 3D-native zero-shot classifiers are open but far weaker at fine granularity: **ULIP-2 reaches 50.6% and OpenShape 46.8% top-1 on the 1,156-class Objaverse-LVIS** ([ULIP-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08275); [OpenShape](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10764)), which makes them a useful secondary check that the generated geometry still embeds nearest to "ring" rather than "bracelet" (catching generator hallucinations) but not the primary classifier. The robust pattern is to classify twice, once on the uploaded photo (richest in packaging, scale and context cues) and once on a four-view render grid of the generated mesh, and to route disagreement to human review; that disagreement is a cheap, high-precision flag. "Table versus floor" is then a size decision made after dimension estimation (below ~0.5 m tall is table-capable), not a classifier output. No paper benchmarks VLM accuracy on an AR-anchor taxonomy, so the six-way accuracy figure must come from Symspace's own gold set.

Real-world scale is the step most likely to be oversold by vendors, and the research is blunt about why. The 2026 Metric VQA benchmark, which poses "How tall is this microwave?" over 10,813 queries, frames single-image size estimation as "an ill-posed diagnostic setting ... because physical size cannot be determined from a single uncalibrated image, models must rely on imperfect cues including category priors, target appearance, local context, apparent image size, and scene geometry" ([Metric VQA, arXiv 2606.24335](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24335)). A VLM dimension guess is therefore a smart category prior, not a measurement, and the pipeline should encode the authoritative ordering explicitly: client-supplied metadata first; a per-category prior table second, with the VLM asked only to choose a size class ("women's/men's ring", "38/42/46 mm watch", "narrow/medium/wide frame") rather than a number; a numeric VLM estimate third, acceptable only for furniture and décor where ±15–20% is tolerable in a preview; and metric monocular depth last, only when the photo is a real scene with an estimable focal length. For that last case Depth Pro is the practical choice because it estimates absolute scale "without relying on camera intrinsics metadata", includes "a direct field-of-view estimation module" and produces a 2.25 MP depth map in 0.3 s ([Depth Pro, arXiv 2410.02073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073)), while Depth Anything 3's metric variant leads UniDepth v2 on ETH3D (δ1 0.917 vs 0.863) ([Depth Anything 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10647)); on a white-background packshot there is no scene geometry and these models add nothing. The best open source of real product dimension statistics is Amazon Berkeley Objects, with 147,702 listings carrying "up to 18 unique metadata attributes (category, color, material, weight, dimensions, etc.)", but it is CC BY-NC 4.0, so derive aggregated prior statistics offline and take a legal read before shipping them ([ABO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06199v1)).

For wearables the priors are tighter and better documented than for furniture, which is fortunate because a ring 10% too large reads as the wrong size. ISO 8653:2016 defines ring sizes by inner circumference, and US whole sizes step by 0.81 mm of inner diameter (**US 6 ≈ 16.5 mm, US 7 ≈ 17.3 mm, US 8 ≈ 18.2 mm, US 10 ≈ 19.8 mm**) ([Ring size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_size)). Wrist perimeter medians in a published anthropometric study are 14.68 cm for women and 16.53 cm for men, with 5th–95th percentiles of 13.53–16.10 cm and 15.20–17.98 cm ([PMC10308009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10308009/table/T1)). Eyewear is specified on the temple as lens-bridge-temple, with lens widths of 40–62 mm, bridges of 14–24 mm and temples of 120–150 mm ([Warby Parker](https://www.warbyparker.com/learn/eyeglasses-measurements)). Mondopoint is foot length in millimetres (225 mm → US 5, 250 mm → US 7, 280 mm → US 10–11) and EU size ≈ (foot length cm + 1.5 cm) × 1.5 ([SkatePro](https://www.skatepro.com/en-us/z192.htm); [Haflinger](https://haflinger.com/en/blogs/news/schuhgroessee-berechnen-mit-dem-pariser-stich)), with adult foot-length means around 24–27 cm for men and 22–24 cm for women across populations ([HK Practitioner](https://www.hkcfp.org.hk/Upload/HK_Practitioner/2006/hkp2006vol28may/images/p205t07.html)). These tables let the configurator set wearable scale from the fitted inner diameter or heel-to-toe length and let the Unity client override once per session from measured landmarks (finger width, wrist width, foot length) with the estimate median-filtered over the first seconds and then locked, since per-frame scale jitter is the most visible artifact.

Orientation is less of a problem than it looks, because generated meshes are not arbitrarily posed: TRELLIS's export rotation is fixed, Hunyuan outputs are generator-canonical, and image-to-3D models condition on the very photo whose view direction can be estimated. Orient Anything (ICML 2025, CC-BY-4.0, 23 M to 305 M parameters) predicts "azimuth, polar angle, and rotation, plus a confidence score" from a single object image after training on 2 M renders with annotated fronts ([Orient Anything](https://github.com/SpatialVision/Orient-Anything)), and V2 (NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight, code December 2025) extends it to "objects with diverse rotational symmetries" and claims state-of-the-art zero-shot results across 11 benchmarks ([Orient Anything V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05573)). The recipe is to run it on the input photo, map camera-relative azimuth to the generator's canonical frame using a per-generator calibration measured once on ~50 labelled assets, apply yaw only so the front sits on glTF +Z ("upright and forward" in Khronos terms), and mark rotationally symmetric categories (rings, round tables, lamps) `front_axis: "symmetric"` so Unity never tries to face them toward the user. Pure PCA or oriented-bounding-box analysis cannot resolve the 180° front/back ambiguity and fails on thin open shapes, and learned upright estimators such as Upright-Net, which classifies "points on a 3D model that form the natural base" ([Upright-Net, CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Pang_Upright-Net_Learning_Upright_Orientation_for_3D_Point_Cloud_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)), were trained on ModelNet-style categories and should be a fallback only where gold-set calibration shows a generator's up axis drifting for a category. Whether TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D align the input view to a fixed mesh axis is undocumented and must be measured empirically; that calibration is the first gold-set task.

Pivot normalisation is deterministic once orientation is fixed. trimesh supplies `bounding_box_oriented`, `apply_obb`, `principal_inertia_transform`, cross-sections, ray queries, watertight and Euler-number checks and arbitrary transforms ([trimesh README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mikedh/trimesh/main/README.md)). For floor and table objects, drop floater components from `mesh.split(only_watertight=False)`, translate so `min_y = 0`, and centre X/Z on the convex hull of the support polygon (vertices within ~0.5% of height of the base) rather than the AABB centre, so a chair's protruding backrest does not offset the pivot; then project `center_mass` onto that hull and flag `unstable_base` when it falls outside, which catches tipped-over generations. For wall objects, place the back face at z = 0 with −Z as the wall side to match model-viewer and record `wall_normal`; for ceiling objects, put top-centre at the origin. For wearables, the pivot becomes an attachment frame computed as described next.

## Geometry recipes derive wearable attachment frames per category, seeded by part segmentation

No library emits "attachment frame for ring, watch, glasses or shoe" from an arbitrary mesh, and no public keypoint dataset covers accessories: KeypointNet's 16 categories run from airplane to vessel and omit glasses, shoes and rings ([KeypointNet](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qq456cvb/KeypointNet/master/README.md)). What exists is a toolbox from which a per-category recipe can be built. pyRANSAC-3D (Apache-2.0) fits planes, cylinders, spheres, circles, cuboids and cones, with the cylinder fit returning centre, axis, radius and inliers ([pyRANSAC-3D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leomariga/pyRANSAC-3D/master/README.md)), though RANSAC cylinder fitting "is not reliable as a cylinder detector without filtering false detections" and needs pre-segmentation ([primitive fitting, arXiv 2205.15426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15426)). Topology gives the cleanest ring test: a watertight solid has Euler characteristic χ = V − E + F = 2, while "a block with a single through-hole (topologically equivalent to a torus) ... the Euler characteristic χ = 0" ([trimesh core operations](https://tessl.io/registry/tessl/pypi-trimesh/4.8.0/files/docs/core-operations.md)). For isolating the band, lens, sole or hook before fitting, three open part segmenters exist: P3-SAM, released with Hunyuan3D-Part in September 2025, is "a native 3D Point-Promptable Part segmentation model designed to fully automate the segmentation of any complex 3D objects" trained on ~3.7 million models, and X-Part "works best with scanned or AI-generated meshes" ([P3-SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06784v4); [Hunyuan3D-Part README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Part/main/README.md)); NVIDIA's PartField returns hierarchical clusterings from .obj/.glb input ([PartField](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nv-tlabs/PartField/main/README.md)); SAMPart3D renders 16 views and clusters DINOv2/SAM features ([SAMPart3D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Pointcept/SAMPart3D/main/README.md)). Hunyuan3D-Part carries the Tencent Community licence, which "does not apply in the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea" and requires a licence request at 1 million MAU ([Hunyuan3D-Part LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-Part/main/LICENSE)), so PartField or SAMPart3D is the safer default for a global product. Where a semantic label is needed ("which part is the hook?"), the quickest route without training is a VLM on multi-view renders, the approach Holodeck used with four orthogonal renders to output category and dimensions per asset and ZeroKey used to name salient 3D keypoints, with the caveat that cross-view text aggregation "is susceptible to hallucinations" ([Holodeck](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09067); [ZeroKey](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06292v1); [VLM annotation of 3D objects](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.17851v2)).

The recipes below are the research team's synthesis, not any single source, and each one must be validated on the gold set; the commercial conventions they mirror are cited where they exist.

| Category | Frame origin and axes | Geometric method | Scale rule | Runtime anchor (iOS / Android) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ring | Origin = hole centre; +Y = hole (finger) axis; +Z = toward stone/head | Repair to watertight, require χ = 0; RANSAC cylinder on inward-facing vertices gives axis and inner radius; ray-cast along axis must miss geometry | Inner diameter = size table (17.3 mm US 7) or measured finger width + clearance | Vision hand pose + depth (existing plugin) / BlazeHand + ARCore depth; pose from MCP→PIP vector and palm normal |
| Watch, bracelet | Origin = inner loop centre; +Y = forearm axis; +Z = dial normal (dorsal) | Genus-1 or open "C"; RANSAC cylinder/ellipse on inner surface; dial = largest outward planar region on the segmented case | Inner circumference to wrist prior (14.7–17.2 cm) or landmark-measured wrist | Wrist landmark + forearm direction from hand landmarks |
| Glasses | Origin = bridge midpoint; +Z = lens normal toward viewer; temples −Z | Mirror-plane via PCA + reflect-and-ICP; lens plane by RANSAC on two largest flat regions; temple tips = farthest −Z vertices | Total width 125–150 mm or scaled to face-mesh temple width | ARKit eye transforms + anchor / ARCore nose-tip and forehead region poses |
| Earrings | Origin = top of hook/post; +Y = world up | Thin topmost component by part segmentation or small cross-section near top of principal axis | Keep metric size from category prior | Per-provider ear-edge vertex offset (ARCore/MediaPipe 234/454 cheek-edge); pendulum joint under gravity |
| Necklace | Origin = loop centre (closed) or clasp; pendant marks front (−Y extreme) | Genus-1 with large inner radius; pendant = farthest vertex from loop plane | Loop circumference from prior | Chin vertex (152 on 468 mesh) projected down −Y 8–12 cm, or shoulder joints when body in frame |
| Hat | Origin = inner brim centre; +Y = crown axis | PCA minor axis of inner cavity | Inner circumference = head circumference from face mesh + margin | Face anchor + top-of-head offset |
| Shoe | Origin = heel bottom-centre; +Z = toe; +Y = sole normal | RANSAC plane with largest downward-facing planar inlier area; heel/toe = extremes along long axis on the sole plane | Heel-to-toe = foot length + 1–1.5 cm allowance or size table | ARKit `leftFoot`/`rightFoot` joints / BlazePose ankle-heel-foot-index + depth; mirror with X = −1 and flipped winding for the other foot |

Several conventions in that table are drawn from shipped systems. A glasses try-on patent attaches via "a nose point and at least one ear point" on the face and "a nose connection point and at least one earpiece connection point" on the product ([US9990780](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9990780)). A 2026 ring try-on paper runs MediaPipe's 21 hand landmarks, localises the ring with YOLO-v8 and resizes to a region defined by the target finger's landmarks, with no GPU required ([Virtual Ring Try-On, arXiv 2606.28792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28792)); an edge wristwatch framework estimates the watch's 3D pose "from three key hand landmarks" with "buffer-based geometric parameter smoothing" at over 30 fps ([IJIMAI 2026](https://reunir.unir.net/items/55e33ae7-685c-40e6-a9f5-a0f4285706ae/full)). DeepAR's shoe setup expects a glTF shoe under a foot-tracking node with the occluder mirrored by "setting the X scale to −1" ([DeepAR shoe setup](https://docs.deepar.ai/archive/shoe-try-on-quick-setup)), and Amazon's shoe patent predicts "an orientation, scale, and position" of the foot then "removes portions of the 3D model of the shoe that intersect" with a foot/leg/garment mask ([US11900559B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11900559)). Perfect Corp's AgileHand "is able to determine wrist & finger sizes automatically" without markers ([Perfect Corp AgileHand](https://www.perfectcorp.com/business/technologies/agile-hand-tracking)), which is the existence proof that landmark-derived metric sizing is viable at product quality; the open approximation is MediaPipe world landmarks, which are "real-world 3D coordinates in meters with the origin at the hand's approximate geometric center" ([MediaPipe hands.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/docs/solutions/hands.md)), combined with AR depth for absolute position.

The runtime anchors must be data-driven per provider because the two face meshes differ. ARKit's face anchor uses a metre-scaled coordinate space "centered behind the face" with +Z toward the viewer, exposes `leftEyeTransform`, `rightEyeTransform`, `lookAtPoint` and blend shapes, and its geometry has constant topology (commonly 1,220 vertices) that can double as occlusion geometry ([Apple ARFaceAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor); [Apple ARFaceGeometry](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfacegeometry)). ARCore provides "a center pose, three region poses (left forehead, right forehead, and nose tip), and a 468-point 3D face mesh" via `ARCoreFaceSubsystem.GetRegionPoses()`, and no eye transforms or blend shapes ([ARCore face tracking in AR Foundation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arcore/master/Documentation~/features/face-tracking.md); [AR Foundation face-tracking platform support](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arfoundation/master/Documentation~/features/face-tracking/platform-support.md)). Developers who hard-coded mesh indices found it "only works on specific devices because the face mesh can vary across different devices" ([arfoundation-samples issue #754](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/arfoundation-samples/issues/754)), so the existing hand-placed empties on Symspace's face prefab should be replaced by per-provider index tables resolved as weighted vertex averages, with pose-level anchors (ARKit eye transforms, ARCore region poses) preferred wherever they suffice. Neither mesh contains ears; on the 468 topology the face-oval points 234/127/162 and 454/356/389 sit at the cheek-temple edge from which an earlobe is a fixed lateral and downward offset ([face_mesh_connections.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/python/solutions/face_mesh_connections.py); [MediaPipe index cheat sheet](https://hackernoon.com/mediapipe-face-mesh-landmark-indices-cheat-sheet)), and on ARKit a comparable edge vertex has to be picked once from the canonical mesh because no published anatomical index list exists.

Stability tuning is the one place where concrete numbers are public. MediaPipe's own pose pipeline applies a One-Euro filter with **min_cutoff 0.05 and beta 80 on normalized landmarks, 0.1 and 40 on world landmarks, and 0.01 and 10 on auxiliary ROI landmarks** ([pose_landmark_filtering.pbtxt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/modules/pose_landmark/pose_landmark_filtering.pbtxt)); Casiez's tuning procedure is to set beta to zero and min-cutoff near 1 Hz, lower min-cutoff until static jitter disappears, then raise beta in ×10 steps from 0.0001 until fast motion stops lagging ([1€ filter](https://cristal.univ-lille.fr/~casiez/1euro)). The hand and face landmarker graphs ship no smoothing at all (face smooths only in single-face stream mode) ([hand_landmarker_graph.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/master/mediapipe/tasks/cc/vision/hand_landmarker/hand_landmarker_graph.cc)), and the try-on literature smooths the derived rigid transform ("origin stabilization" in ARShoe) rather than raw points ([ARShoe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10515)). A single-file MIT C# port covering float, Vector3 and Quaternion exists for Unity ([OneEuroFilterUnity](https://github.com/unitycoder/oneeurofilterunity)). The Unity implementation should filter the attachment pose, add a few-millimetre and ~1° dead-band, hold the last pose for N frames when confidence drops, and lock scale after an initial median.

## Garments break the automation promise, so use a proxy body and 2D generative try-on

Garments are the category where "down to a T" cannot honestly be promised, for three independent reasons. The first is rigging. UniRig (SIGGRAPH 2025, MIT, Tsinghua and Tripo) "autoregressively predicts a topologically valid skeleton hierarchy" for "humans, animals, objects" from .obj/.fbx/.glb/.vrm input on an 8 GB GPU, and its successor SkinTokens claims "98%–133% improvement in skinning accuracy and 17%–22% improvement in bone prediction" ([UniRig](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig)); but the skeletons are arbitrary, not Unity-Humanoid or ARKit-named, the README never mentions garments, and skinning quality "degrades significantly with inaccurate skeletons". Make-It-Animatable targets humanoids explicitly and rigs "any 3D humanoid model ... in less than one second" ([Make-It-Animatable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18197v1)), which makes it the safer template-based choice, yet no surveyed paper evaluates a body-less dress mesh, so a product-photo garment is out of distribution for all of them. RigNet is GPL-3.0 and superseded ([RigNet](https://github.com/zhan-xu/RigNet)). The second reason is the body model. Open body-shape estimators such as 4D-Humans (MIT code) "require the neutral SMPL model file ... obtained separately from the official SMPL website" ([4D-Humans](https://github.com/shubham-goel/4D-Humans)), and SMPL/SMPL-X files are licensed for "non-commercial scientific research purposes" with commercial licences via Meshcapade reported at **€1,500 per year for micro enterprises and €25,000 per year for SMEs** ([SMPL-X licence](https://smpl-x.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [Meshcapade body models](https://meshcapade.com/assets/body-models)); Multi-HMR is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([Multi-HMR licence](https://huggingface.co/spaces/naver/multi-hmr/blob/bac33d63480e549ddd799f63a7be4f28e0df07b6/Multi-HMR_License.txt)). The third is cloth: VRChat's developers report that "Cloth components with 1500 or more vertices can cause significant hitching" ([VRChat developer update](https://ask.vrchat.com/t/developer-update-28-august-2025/46531?page=2)), Magica Cloth's author warns that MeshCloth "consumes a lot of CPU resources" on mobile while BoneCloth is fine ([Magica Cloth thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/released-magica-cloth/773425?page=49)), and the learned simulators HOOD and ContourCraft (MIT) are desktop-GPU research code tied to SMPL and "restricted to single-layer garments" ([ContourCraft](https://github.com/dolorousrtur/ContourCraft)).

The defensible automation for a 3D garment is therefore not to rig the garment at all. Bind a generic, permissively licensed Unity Humanoid proxy body to the team's existing ARKit `BoneController`, transfer skin weights from that proxy to the garment by nearest-surface projection (a dress inherits the leg weights where it overlaps the legs), and add only BoneCloth-style spring chains on hems and sleeves. This is the design Snap ships commercially, where outfits "automatically deform to any body without needing to be rigged using the External Mesh feature" bound to a tracked body mesh ([Snap Clothing Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/clothing-try-on)), and Meta's `OVRUnityHumanoidSkeletonRetargeter`, which "will automatically find known joints and bone transforms on the character, which should be verified and adjusted", is the reference pattern for tracking-to-humanoid retargeting ([Meta Movement body tracking](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/move-body-tracking/)). Where a garment-only mesh is needed from a flat-lay or on-model photo, the only fully permissive chain found is ChatGarment (Apache-2.0), which "finetunes a VLM to produce GarmentCode, a JSON-based ... format for 2D sewing patterns" draped on a parametric body, built on the MIT GarmentCode framework, with the acknowledged limitation that it "may occasionally produce garments with incorrect lengths or widths from input images" ([ChatGarment](https://github.com/biansy000/chatgarment); [GarmentCode](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode)). Generic image-to-3D of an on-model photo yields a fused body-plus-garment solid that P3-SAM or PartField can segment, but the generator never saw the garment's interior, so the result is a shell to be hollowed; this is inference from how the generators work, untested in any source.

The stronger recommendation is to follow the market and serve garment try-on as a 2D (optionally short-video) generative image on the user's own photo. Google's Shopping try-on lets users "upload a full-length photo of themselves, and see what they might look like wearing the clothing" ([TechCrunch, July 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/googles-new-ai-feature-lets-you-virtually-try-on-clothes)); its Doppl app "first generates a still image... then offers animation options" with a few minutes per outfit ([Doppl coverage](https://getcoai.com/news/googles-doppl-app-creates-virtual-try-on-videos-from-any-outfit)); Perfect Corp launched generative clothes try-on in May 2025 and in January 2026 "nine new APIs for virtual try-on... for watches, bracelets, rings, earrings, necklaces, scarves, hats, shoes, and bags... powered by Generative AI" ([Perfect Corp, Jan 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260115898437/en/Perfect-Corp.-Unveils-Range-of-New-Modular-APIs-to-Power-Next-Generation-of-Fashion-Virtual-Try-On-Experiences-Powered-by-Generative-AI)); Doji raised $14 M for diffusion-based avatars and Walmart bought Zeekit for 2D try-on ([TechCrunch on Doji](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/doji-raises-14m-to-make-virtual-try-ons-fun-through-ai-avatars); [Walmart/Zeekit](https://martechvibe.com/article/walmart-acquires-virtual-clothing-try-on-startup-zeekit)). Live 3D body-tracked garments survive mainly inside Snap's Lens ecosystem. The licence reality for open 2D try-on is harsh: IDM-VTON, CatVTON, FitDiT, CatV2TON and the Wan-based MagicTryOn video model are all CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([IDM-VTON](https://github.com/yisol/IDM-VTON); [FitDiT](https://github.com/BoyuanJiang/FitDiT); [CatV2TON](https://github.com/Zheng-Chong/CatV2TON); [MagicTryOn](https://github.com/vivoCameraResearch/Magic-TryOn)), leaving **Leffa (CVPR 2025, MIT) as the only permissive model with competitive quality, at FID 4.54 on VITON-HD against FitDiT's 4.73 and IDM-VTON's 6.29, 3.91 GB VRAM at 512×384 and about 6 s per image on an A100** ([Leffa](https://github.com/franciszzj/Leffa); [DeCo-VTON VRAM table](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18775); [VITON-HD benchmark](https://hyper.ai/fr/sota/tasks/virtual-try-on/benchmark/virtual-try-on-on-viton-hd)). For video try-on every open option is non-commercial as of October 2026, so garment video is either Leffa stills animated by the render-to-video pipeline below or a paid API. The product split that follows is: 3D garment generated for product visualisation and turntables, 2D Leffa try-on for "how does it look on me", and live AR body tracking kept for a clearly labelled approximate rigid-garment preview on iOS only.

## Render first, then let Wan 2.2 and LTX-2.5 add uniqueness under structural control

Product video has one dominant failure mode and Symspace holds the one asset that defeats it. Practitioner reports agree that in generated video "product logos can become smears or move to the wrong side" and that text "requires precise, pixel-level consistency that conflicts with AI's probabilistic generation" ([pctechmag, Aug 2026](https://pctechmag.com/2026/08/from-face-drift-to-product-distortion-how-to-fix-ai-video-consistency/)), and eBay's CVPR 2026 ConsID-Gen paper frames identity drift under viewpoint change as "a major roadblock for deploying video generation in real-world e-commerce" ([ConsID-Gen, CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Wu_ConsID-Gen_View-Consistent_and_Identity-Preserving_Image-to-Video_Generation_CVPR_2026_paper.html)), though its code and weights remained "under internal review" as of March 2026 ([ebay/consid-gen](https://github.com/ebay/consid-gen)). Because the GLB already exists, the pipeline should render the product deterministically and let diffusion add only what the render lacks. Tier 1 is a headless Blender job (`blender -b file.blend --python turntable.py`) that imports the GLB, lights it with an HDRI, and renders a 5–10 s orbit plus depth and outline passes ([headless Blender](https://hpcdocs.hpc.arizona.edu/running_jobs/visualization/blender/command_line_rendering/); [blenderless](https://pypi.org/project/blenderless/0.1.7/)); FFmpeg or MoviePy add brand text and a music bed. This tier is licence-free, identity-perfect and always succeeds. A granted US patent (US 12,381,848 B1, August 2025) describes "automatically generating 3D rendered videos for e-commerce product pages" from an uploaded model ([US12381848](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12381848)), which is a freedom-to-operate note for counsel rather than a technical obstacle.

Tier 2 makes each video unique while keeping geometry pinned. Wan2.1-VACE (Apache-2.0 for the 1.3B and 14B models) unifies reference-to-video, video-to-video with "depth, pose, and other control signals", and masked inpainting ([VACE](https://github.com/ali-vilab/VACE)); Alibaba PAI's Apache-2.0 Wan2.2-Fun-Control adds Canny, depth, pose, MLSD and trajectory control at 512/768/1024 (A14B at 81 frames/16 fps, 5B at 121 frames/24 fps), and Wan2.2-VACE-Fun-A14B combines those controls with "subject-specific video generation via reference images" ([VideoX-Fun](https://github.com/aigc-apps/VideoX-Fun)); the official ComfyUI template is "guided by pose, depth, and edge inputs" ([comfy.org Fun Control workflow](https://comfy.org/workflows/video_wan2_2_14B_fun_control-67a816af8a73/)). A packaged Blender-to-ComfyUI workflow already "reads structure from depth, outline, or pose passes and uses that to drive Wan VACE video generation so your final footage stays aligned with the original animation intent" ([RunComfy Blender to ComfyUI](https://www.runcomfy.com/comfyui-workflows/blender-to-comfyui-ai-renderer-2-0-workflow-cinematic-video-output)). The depth and edge passes constrain shape every frame, the hero render serves as the reference image, and the prompt describes the lifestyle environment; logos survive because they are rendered, not generated. Optionally IC-Light (Apache-2.0) relights the hero still first, with the caveat from its author that "the original BRIA RMBG 1.4 is for non-commercial use ... replace it with other background replacer like BiRefNet" ([IC-Light](https://github.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light)). An automated QA step compares generated frames against the render (silhouette IoU, DINO similarity, OCR on the logo region) and falls back to Tier 1 on failure. No independent quantitative study confirms that depth-conditioned VACE preserves printed text reliably, so this is the in-house test to run first. Reference-driven models (Phantom, Apache-2.0, up to four reference images; MAGREF, Apache-2.0 but ~70 GB VRAM; SkyReels-V3 reference-to-video) were trained primarily on people and hold silhouette and colour but not small text ([Phantom](https://github.com/Phantom-video/Phantom); [MAGREF](https://github.com/MAGREF-Video/MAGREF); [SkyReels-V3](https://github.com/SkyworkAI/SkyReels-V3)); 3D-aware novel-view models (Uni3C at ~50 GB and ~20 minutes on one H20, GEN3C at ~43 GB) are heavy and hallucinate back sides, and Stable Virtual Camera is excluded outright because "the output follows the same non-commercial license" ([Uni3C](https://github.com/ewrfcas/Uni3C); [GEN3C](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/GEN3C); [stable-virtual-camera](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-virtual-camera)).

Model choice is dictated by licence geography as much as quality. Wan 2.2 (July 2025: T2V/I2V-A14B, TI2V-5B, S2V-14B, Animate-14B) is Apache-2.0 and the last open Wan, since 2.5 and 2.6 "were never published to HuggingFace, GitHub, or ModelScope" ([Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2); [Wan 2.6 status](https://www.wan27.org/blog/wan-2-6-open-source-guide)). LTX-2.5 (22B, August 2026) generates synchronized audio and video, UHD, up to 121 frames, with native multi-shot generation, but its "LTX-2.x Community License Agreement" requires a paid licence for entities "with annual revenues of at least $10,000,000" and bars "any product, service, or application that directly competes with Licensor's commercial products"; aggregator claims that it is Apache-2.0 are wrong ([LTX-2](https://github.com/Lightricks/LTX-2); [LICENSE-2_x](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Lightricks/LTX-2/main/LICENSE-2_x)). HunyuanVideo 1.5 (8.3B, 14 GB with offload, 75 s for distilled 480p I2V on a 4090) is the cheapest to run but its licence territory is "worldwide territory, excluding the territory of the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea" ([HunyuanVideo-1.5](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-1.5); [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-1.5/main/LICENSE)). MiniMax H3 scores near the top of the Artificial Analysis image-to-video arena, but its community licence reportedly "explicitly blocks local deployment in the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, and South Korea" ([MiniMax-H3](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/MiniMax-H3); [comfyui-wiki on H3](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2026-08-03-minimax-h3-open-weights-comfyui)); MAGI-2 Preview is Apache-2.0 but a 114B MoE with unknown VRAM ([MAGI-2 news](https://comfyui-wiki.com/zh/news/2026-08-05-magi-2-preview)). On human preference the best deployable open model trails closed leaders by roughly 175 Elo (LTX-2.5 Pro ≈ 1,016 versus Seedance 2.0 ≈ 1,191 in the latest snippet), a gap that matters for cinematics and little for a static product on a camera orbit ([Artificial Analysis I2V leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). LTX-2.x lacks depth/pose control in its 2.x line (only generic IC-LoRA video-to-video), so the hybrid is Wan for structure-locked passes and LTX-2.5 for audio-bearing B-roll.

The commercially clean audio stack is ACE-Step 1.5 (Apache-2.0, January 2026, about 1.74 s per minute of music on a 4090) ([ACE-Step](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step)), Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) or Chatterbox (MIT, zero-shot cloning, every output watermarked) for voice-over ([kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro); [chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)); avoid MMAudio and F5-TTS checkpoints (CC-BY-NC), HunyuanVideo-Foley (Tencent territory clause) and Remotion for text overlays, which needs a paid licence above three employees ([MMAudio](https://github.com/hkchengrex/MMAudio); [HunyuanVideo-Foley LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-Foley/main/LICENSE); [Remotion LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Both the Hunyuan and LTX licences require disclosure of machine-generated content, which is a compliance step for client-facing marketing videos. Orchestration is headless ComfyUI driven as a job server (POST `/prompt`, watch `/ws`, pull `/history/{id}`) ([ComfyUI websockets API example](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI/master/script_examples/websockets_api_example.py)), with kijai's WanVideoWrapper covering VACE, Phantom, Uni3C and MAGREF with FP8/GGUF loaders and block-swap offloading ("512×512×81 frame generation used approximately 16GB with 20/40 blocks offloaded") ([ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper](https://github.com/kijai/ComfyUI-WanVideoWrapper)) and DiffSynth-Studio (Apache-2.0) as the training-capable alternative ([DiffSynth-Studio](https://github.com/modelscope/DiffSynth-Studio)). Cost arithmetic from vendor figures: an RTX 4090 at $0.34–0.69/h on RunPod ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)) produces a 10 s 1080p LTX-2 clip in about 2:46 at FP8 ([nemovideo bench](https://www.nemovideo.com/es-ES/blog/ltx-2-fp4-vs-fp8-rtx-guide)) for $0.016–0.032, and a 5 s 720p Wan2.2-5B clip in "under 9 minutes" ([Wan2.2](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2)) for $0.05–0.10; **a 30-second product video of three to six clips plus a turntable lands at roughly $0.10–0.60 self-hosted or $0.50–3 through APIs** (Replicate Wan 2.2 fast at $0.05 per 5 s 480p clip; fal LTX-2.3 Fast at $0.04/s), with a 2–3× re-roll budget ([pruna on Replicate pricing](https://www.pruna.ai/blog/wan-2-2-video-juiced-fastest-and-cheapest-video-generation); [nemovideo LTX pricing](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ltx-2-3-pricing)). The 14B Wan models barely fit a 24 GB card and are the one case where an H100 at $1.99–3.95/h is cheaper per clip.

## In Unity, upgrade AR Foundation, keep UnityGLTF, and abstract every landmark source

The current AR Foundation feature matrix settles what each platform can do natively. Plane detection, anchors, image tracking, environment probes and occlusion work on both ARCore and ARKit; **face tracking is on both, body tracking is ARKit-only, meshing is ARKit-only among phones, and hand tracking is absent on both** ([AR Foundation manual index](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arfoundation/master/Documentation~/index.md)), since "the OpenXR package is the only plug-in provider that supports hand tracking" and XR Hands' changelog mentions only OpenXR, Meta Quest and Android XR headsets ([XR Hands manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.hands/master/Documentation~/index.md); [XR Hands CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.hands/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Human segmentation depth and stencil, which give fingers-over-ring and trouser-over-shoe occlusion, are ARKit-only; ARCore exposes environment depth with confidence and temporal smoothing but "human segmentation textures are not supported" ([occlusion platform support](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arfoundation/master/Documentation~/features/occlusion/platform-support.md); [ARCore occlusion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arcore/master/Documentation~/features/occlusion.md)). Three housekeeping decisions follow. Upgrade from AR Foundation 6.2.0 to 6.6.0 (July 2026), which brings `XRFaceSubsystem.TryGetBlendShapes` (ARKit 6.3), persistent anchors on ARCore (6.2), dynamic anchor attachment via `supportsTrackableAttachmentsDelegate` and Vulkan project validation, noting that ARKit 6.4+ requires Xcode 26 and 6.7 is still pre-release ([AR Foundation CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arfoundation/master/CHANGELOG.md); [ARKit CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arkit/master/CHANGELOG.md); [ARCore CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arcore/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Remove `com.unity.mars` 2.0.0 and its AR Foundation providers, because "starting with Unity 6.1, Unity MARS is considered deprecated... no longer recommended for production use" ([Unity MARS deprecation](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unity-mars-is-now-deprecated/1630939)). Drop Niantic Lightship from consideration: ARDK 3.17 is "the last full release", features work only "through February 28th, 2027", it has known incompatibilities with AR Foundation 6, and it never shipped hand or body tracking ([Lightship 3.17 docs](https://nianticspatial.com/docs/nsdk/3.17.0/); [Niantic community on body pose](https://community.nianticspatial.com/t/does-lightship-has-a-way-to-detect-human-body-and-poses/788)). One operational risk needs tracking: developers report that since iOS 26.0 "ARBodyTrackingConfiguration no longer detects a valid ARBodyAnchor on devices with LiDAR... all joints return nil", reproducing in Apple's own sample with no documented response ([Apple Developer Forums thread 804899](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804899)), so the shoe path cannot rely on ARKit body tracking alone even on iOS.

The cross-platform gap filler is a single `ILandmarkProvider` interface with interchangeable backends behind it. Backend one is Unity's inference package (`com.unity.ai.inference`, renamed back to Sentis at 2.4.0, now 2.6.1 with ONNX opset 25 and CPU, GPUCompute and GPUPixel backends) running Unity's own ONNX ports of BlazeHand (hand detector 4.59 MB, landmarker 10.9 MB, output 21 keypoints) and BlazePose (lite/full/heavy) ([Sentis CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.ai.inference/master/CHANGELOG.md); [unity/inference-engine-blaze-hand](https://huggingface.co/unity/inference-engine-blaze-hand)); this is pure C# with no native build pain but no published mobile FPS. Backend two is homuler's MediaPipeUnityPlugin (MIT, iOS and Android with GPU, wraps MediaPipe 0.10.22 and supports Unity 6), which supplies the fuller tracking stack of palm detector, landmarker, world landmarks and handedness, at the cost that its latest release is v0.16.3 from November 2024 and its README warns "Your application may crash!" over native-library bugs ([MediaPipeUnityPlugin](https://github.com/homuler/MediaPipeUnityPlugin); [releases](https://github.com/homuler/MediaPipeUnityPlugin/releases)). Backend three is the existing iOS Vision plugin, kept as the iOS fallback; Apple engineers confirm Vision hand pose "provides landmarks in 2D only" and that 3D comes from "analyzing the depth texture" ([Apple forum 650149](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/650149)), which is what the plugin already does with `AROcclusionManager`. Backend four wraps `ARHumanBodyManager` so BlazePose takes over when ARKit yields no joints. Feet use Pose Landmarker's ankle (27/28), heel (29/30) and foot-index (31/32) landmarks ([MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker)); three points per foot give position, yaw and pitch but weak roll, and the legacy Pixel 3 numbers (Lite 20 ms, Full 25 ms, Heavy 53 ms) ([mediapipe pose.md](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md)) imply a 30 fps budget on mid-range Android. The dedicated foot-pose networks in the literature (ARShoe's keypoint-plus-PnP with origin stabilisation, the 2025 CPU real-time mobile system) are not open-sourced ([ARShoe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10515); [Complex & Intelligent Systems 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-025-02188-x)), so matching Snap's "SnapML model that better detects the user's foot" ([Snap Footwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/foot-tracking)) would need an in-house labelled foot dataset. Lifting any 2D landmark to world space is the same on both platforms: sample the smoothed environment-depth texture at the landmark (respecting the depth image's display matrix), unproject with the AR camera intrinsics, build the attachment frame from landmark vectors, filter it, and re-parent world-placed items to an `ARAnchor` after placement.

The loader decision reverses the earlier report's preference for glTFast, and the reasons are specific to this project. UnityGLTF (Khronos repo maintained by prefrontal cortex and Needle, MIT, 2.20.0 in July 2026) supports Unity 6000.0+, URP, async runtime import through `GLTFSceneImporter` and `UnityWebRequestLoader`, Draco, KTX2 and meshopt 0.2 through Unity's `com.unity.cloud.draco`, `com.unity.cloud.ktx` and `com.unity.meshopt.decompress` packages, KHR_materials transmission/volume/iridescence/clearcoat/sheen, KHR_animation_pointer, skinned meshes and blend shapes ([UnityGLTF README](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF); [UnityGLTF releases](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/releases)). glTFast 6.20.0 (Apache-2.0) has the cleaner Burst/Jobs path but lists iridescence and KHR_animation_pointer as unsupported and sheen/transmission as partial, and its 7.0.0-exp.1 release renames the namespace and async methods, making it an unstable migration target in October 2026 ([glTFast features](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.cloud.gltfast/master/Documentation~/features.md); [glTFast CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.cloud.gltfast/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Unity itself notes the two "can coexist in the same project" ([Unity runtime asset loading guide](https://unity.com/resources/runtime-asset-loading-technology-for-rt3d)). The project already runs UnityGLTF 2.18.5, so the move is an upgrade to 2.20.0 for meshopt 0.2, Draco safety checks and lower texture-import memory, plus the Draco and KTX packages so KTX2 textures transcode to ASTC on iOS and ETC2 on Android and "typically cut texture memory 4× to 8×" ([Khronos KTX2 page](https://www.khronos.org/developers/linkto/compressed-texture-transmission-ktx2-basis-universal)). The sidecar is applied with a wrapper-root pattern: instantiate under a disabled wrapper, compute combined renderer bounds, shift the imported root so the wrapper's origin is the configured pivot, apply uniform scale from `dimensions_m`, read `ar_mode` to select `ARPlaneManager` detection mode or the face, hand, foot or body attachment, and parent the wrapper under the trackable, leaving the imported hierarchy untouched so skinned garments keep their skeleton bindings.

Delivery, occlusion and testing round out the runtime. Raw `.glb` (Draco plus KTX2 at ≤ 2048², 10k–50k triangles per the earlier report) and `.ar.json` go to Firebase Storage behind a CDN at content-hashed immutable paths, cached in `Application.persistentDataPath`, while Addressables are reserved for app-owned prefabs, shaders and ML models, matching Unity's own framing of glTF for "flexible loading of native 3D data" and Addressables for Unity-authored live content ([Unity runtime asset loading guide](https://unity.com/resources/runtime-asset-loading-technology-for-rt3d)). Occlusion policy per mode: environment depth with temporal smoothing for plane placement on both platforms; the ARKit face mesh as an invisible depth-only occluder for glasses temples and earrings, since Apple documents it for exactly that ([Apple ARFaceAnchor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor)); ARKit human depth (A12+) for rings and shoes ([ARKit occlusion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arkit/master/Documentation~/arkit-occlusion.md)); and on Android a landmark-driven proxy occluder (finger capsules, a wrist capsule, a heel-toe-ankle foot wedge, rendered depth-only), which is the open equivalent of Snap's Hand Occluder and "Feet Removal" occluder meshes ([Snap hand mesh](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/ar-tracking/hand/hand-mesh); [Snap Footwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/foot-tracking)). Light estimation feeds the URP directional light and ambient probe as in the AR Foundation samples' `HDRLightEstimation.cs` ([arfoundation-samples](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/arfoundation-samples)), and a transparent shadow-receiver shader goes under shoes and floor items. For testing, XR Simulation covers planes, images, meshing, point clouds and occlusion in Play Mode but not faces or bodies and "should not be considered a replacement for on-device testing" ([XR Simulation overview](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arfoundation/master/Documentation~/xr-simulation/simulation-overview.md)); ARCore session recording replays MP4 sensor data but "does not always produce the same output, which means trackables might not be consistent between playbacks" ([ARCore session recording](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.arcore/master/Documentation~/arcore-session-recording.md)); BrowserStack offers camera image injection only on Pro/Enterprise plans and AWS Device Farm "does not provide an in-house solution for mocking camera input" ([BrowserStack camera injection](https://www.browserstack.com/docs/app-automate/appium/advanced-features/camera-image-injection); [AWS re:Post](https://www.repost.aws/questions/QUNTY6td-HQHWHRi1QBDQvUw/is-mocking-camera-input-possible-with-aws-device-farm)). The test pyramid is therefore EditMode tests for the pivot/scale math and landmark-to-pose solver on synthetic landmarks, PlayMode tests under XR Simulation (run headless with `-runTests`) for plane placement and GLB load, mocked `XRFaceSubsystem`/`XRHumanBodySubsystem` providers for face and body attachment, an offline replay harness that feeds recorded frames with hand-annotated landmarks through the solver and asserts reprojection error and silhouette IoU, and manual on-device sessions for tracking quality, which no device farm can make deterministic.

Hardware is modest by the standards of the generators themselves. The configurator's heavy parts are a VLM for classification and a VLM render-judge; everything geometric runs on CPU in seconds. UniRig inference needs "a CUDA-enabled GPU with at least 8GB VRAM" ([UniRig](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/UniRig)); Leffa needs 3.91 GB at 512×384 ([DeCo-VTON](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18775)); the Wan2.2 5B and LTX-2.5 FP8 video models fit a 24 GB card while Wan2.2-A14B FP8 fits 24 GB slowly and is better on 80 GB; MAGREF and Uni3C need 70–80 GB. A single RTX 4090-class worker at $0.34–0.69/h therefore covers configuration, QA, 2D try-on and Tier 1/Tier 2 video for a catalogue, with an H100 at $1.99–3.95/h reserved for 14B structure-locked passes ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing); [Modal pricing via spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/modal-gpu-pricing-2026-per-second-billing/)). Per-asset marginal cost is cents: the earlier report put generation at $0.01–0.10 self-hosted, the configurator adds a few seconds of VLM time, and the video adds $0.10–0.60 per 30-second piece. The real cost is the gold set: 50–100 human-verified assets per AR mode with ground-truth dimensions, pivot and front, re-run nightly to track dimension error, yaw error, pivot error and classifier accuracy, failing the build on regression.

The QA battery that makes "no manual tuning" credible has three layers, and the literature tells you how much to trust each. The deterministic layer is a clean glTF-Validator pass ([glTF-Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator)), a single connected component after floater removal, extents inside the category's plausible range (ring inner diameter 12–25 mm, glasses 120–160 mm, shoes 200–330 mm, sofa 1.2–3.5 m), and per-category rules such as exactly one through-hole for a ring, two symmetric lens regions and two −Z temples for glasses, and a flat sole for shoes. The orientation layer renders the normalised mesh from +Z, runs Orient Anything on the render and asserts azimuth ≈ 0 within tolerance using its confidence output, then reprojects the mesh from the estimated input view and compares silhouette IoU against the BiRefNet mask of the photo. The judgment layer renders a four-to-six-view grid and asks a VLM for a JSON rubric (`is_upright`, `front_matches_photo`, `looks_like_category`, `visible_defects`, `plausible_scale_given_reference` with a reference cube in frame), which is the approach the 3D evaluation literature converged on: GPTEval3D uses multi-view renderings for pairwise VLM judgments, MATE-3D built 107,520 human annotations to train a multi-dimensional evaluator, Eval3D measures consistency among foundation models, and Hi3DEval adds part-level and material scoring ([MATE-3D, ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Zhang_Benchmarking_and_Learning_Multi-Dimensional_Quality_Evaluator_for_Text-to-3D_Generation_ICCV_2025_paper.html); [Eval3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18509v1); [Hi3DEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05609v1)). Because 2026's 3D-DefectBench exists precisely to test "VLMs used as automated judges that inspect multi-view renders" ([3D-DefectBench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.10826)), VLM verdicts should gate "review", never "pass" on their own; a "fail" is a hard block, "uncertain" goes to a human. No published work benchmarks automated QA of AR placement configuration itself, so the gold set is the only ground truth available.

What cannot yet be fully automated is a short, specific list. Garment rigging and fit from a body-less generated mesh has no validated open solution and depends on a licensed body model for shape; girth cannot be measured from ARKit joints or a single photo without SMPL-class estimators, so size recommendation beyond height and limb proportions needs user input. Single-image metric scale for furniture is bounded by the ±15–20% a VLM prior delivers unless the client supplies dimensions or a scene photo. The generator-to-front-axis mapping, ARKit ear and neck vertex indices, and One-Euro constants for each landmark stream must each be calibrated once by hand. Foot roll from three pose landmarks is weak, and Android hand-over-ring occlusion will be visibly worse than iOS without a segmentation model. Deterministic AR tracking tests on real devices do not exist on any farm. And the VLM classifier's accuracy on the six-way placement taxonomy is unmeasured in any paper, so the first sprint's output is a labelled gold set rather than code.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "automatically configured down to a T" from a model-selection problem into a measurement problem. The generators, the VLMs and the geometry libraries are all available and permissively licensed; what no vendor publishes and no paper benchmarks is the mapping from a specific generator's canonical output to a specific AR anchor's frame, and that mapping is cheap to establish once and expensive to guess. Symspace's competitive asset is therefore not the configurator code but the gold set and the nightly regression that keep it honest as generators are swapped behind the GLB-plus-sidecar contract, exactly the swappability the earlier report argued for on provenance grounds. The same logic explains the video recommendation: a rendered turntable is a ground truth the diffusion model is constrained against, so uniqueness and fidelity stop being a trade-off.

Two implications follow for roadmap order. Rigid wearables and plane-placed products can reach full automation within the current stack once the ILandmarkProvider abstraction gives Android hands and feet, and that work also de-risks the iOS 26 body-tracking regression, so it should precede any garment investment. Garments should ship as 2D Leffa try-on and rendered video first, with live 3D garment AR treated as an iOS-only, explicitly approximate feature until an open auto-rigger demonstrates results on body-less meshes or a Meshcapade licence is budgeted; the market leaders have already made that call, and the licence map shows why.
