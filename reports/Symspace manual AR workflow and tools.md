# Ship AR assets by hand, then automate

Symspace can ship reliably today by inserting one deliberate human step between the TRELLIS.2/Pixal3D generator and Unity: a **5–15 minute review-and-configure pass per rigid asset** (scale, pivot, front axis, one to three attachment anchors) in a small custom three.js review tool backed by glTF-Transform, a **20–60 minute Blender 4.5 LTS clean-up SOP** for the assets that fail review, and a **half-day artist pass per garment**, with every one of those decisions written into the sidecar JSON the earlier report designed and logged as a labelled correction. The market offers no shortcut around that step: hosted generative APIs (Tripo P2.0, Meshy, Rodin Gen-2.5, Hunyuan 3D 3.1) now deliver a textured mesh for roughly **$0.15–$1.50** and some emit quad topology or an auto-rig, but none emits wearable anchors, none guarantees commerce readiness, and real scale exists only as a model guess (Tripo `autoSize`) or a user-supplied box (Rodin `bounding_box`); AI-plus-human services (Modelry from **$60/model** with QA, Kaedim at roughly **$400 per finished asset**, agencies at **$200–$800**) are the right escape hatch for reflective jewelry, eyewear and garments rather than a default. True metric scale comes from a mandatory client dimensions field or LiDAR-assisted capture, never from the photo, so the cheapest intervention is a form field. In the app, the industry pattern is "measure, then auto-fit" for wearables (card-calibrated finger and wrist rulers, TrueDepth pupillary distance at ~0.5 mm error, typed shoe size) and "gesture plus true-size reset" for placed objects; Symspace should expose three constrained sliders built on XRI 3.x transformers, add a staff-only concierge mode that saves a fit as the SKU default, and log each adjustment as a mesh-invariant correction record. Aggregated by median per SKU and per category, those records become the gold set and priors that let each category flip from 100% human review to auto-approve with audit sampling, which is the phased path proposed here: manual in weeks 0–4, semi-automated by month 3, automated per category as its agreement rate crosses a threshold. One caveat governs every price in this report: the research sandbox could not reach most vendor pricing pages, so nearly all figures come from search excerpts or third-party trackers and must be re-checked before they appear in any client-facing document.

## Hosted 3D APIs sell meshes under $1.50 but not AR-ready assets

The hosted image-to-3D market in October 2026 has consolidated around four production-grade APIs, and they matter to Symspace less as a replacement for the self-hosted TRELLIS.2/Pixal3D stack than as burst capacity, a multi-view fallback and a source of features the open models lack. Tripo unveiled **P2.0 on 21 September 2026** as a "3D-native foundation model" with "native quad topology up to 25,000 faces" and triangle meshes to 50,000 faces, plus multiple face-count versions per prompt and a Mesh Edit feature ([Webull press release](https://www.webull.com/news/15619871280677888); [Tripo August update](https://www.tripo3d.ai/de/blog/tripo-august-update)). Its credit table, read from search excerpts of the vendor's help pages, prices "Image to Draft Model" at **30 credits**, retopology at a base of 5 credits (+5 for quad, +30 for Smart Lowpoly) and rigging at 20–25 credits depending on the page, with API credits sold at **$0.01 each and 2,000 free on key creation** ([Tripo help: credits](https://www.tripo3d.ai/help/credit/how-do-credits-work); [Tripo OpenAPI announcement](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/announcement-tripo-openapi-platform-payment-system)), so a draft model is about $0.30 and a retopologised, rigged one under $1. Its one scale-related feature is an `autoSize` boolean that "automatically scales the model to real-world dimensions, with the unit in meters", with GLB default output following the glTF metre and +Y-up convention ([Runware: Tripo v3.1](https://runware.ai/docs/models/tripo-v3-1)); that is an AI estimate, not a measurement. Meshy's API charges **30 credits for a textured Meshy-6 image-to-3D job (35 with 8K texture) and 5 credits per auto-rigging call**, restricts API access to Pro tier and above, and since October 2025 returns a `rigged_character_glb_url` on rigging tasks ([Meshy API pricing](https://docs.meshy.ai/api/pricing); [Meshy changelog](https://docs.meshy.ai/api/changelog)); the Pro plan is about $20 a month and a reseller quotes roughly **$1.20 per textured Meshy 7 model** plus $0.20 for auto-rigging, which likely includes markup ([TrustRadius Meshy](https://www.trustradius.com/products/meshy/pricing); [muapi Meshy v7](https://muapi.ai/it/playground/meshy-v7-image-to-3d)). Hyper3D's Rodin Gen-2 is the 10-billion-parameter model that accepts "up to 5 input images", offers quad-dominant and raw mesh modes, PBR materials, T/A-pose control and a `bounding_box` constraint, and exports GLB, FBX, OBJ, USDZ and STL ([Runware: Rodin Gen-2](https://runware.ai/docs/models/hyper3d-rodin-gen-2)); Gen-2.5 (May 2026) added a quality ladder and HD texture controls ([Scenario: Rodin Gen-2.5](https://help.scenario.com/articles/3791851266-rodin-hyper3d-gen-2-5-models-the-essentials)). Rodin's own pricing lists **Business with API access at $96/month billed annually** and $1.50 per direct credit on the pay-as-you-go tier, while a reseller bills about $0.80 per generation ([hyper3d.ai pricing](https://hyper3d.ai/pricing); [EmpirioLabs](https://empiriolabs.ai/models/hyper3d-gen2)). The `bounding_box` takes "exactly 3 integers" as width, height, length, but no accessible source states the unit ([Leonardo docs: Rodin v2](https://docs.leonardo.ai/me/docs/rodin-v2)), so it cannot be trusted for metric scale until tested. Tencent's hosted Hunyuan 3D 3.1 reconstructs from **up to eight reference views** with 4K PBR, watertight quad topology and 40K–1.5M configurable polygons ([3D AI Studio: Hunyuan3D 3.1](https://www.3daistudio.com/Models/Hunyuan3D-3-1)); resellers price it between $0.12 and $0.375 per model, a conflict this research could not resolve ([AtlasCloud Tencent](https://www.atlascloud.ai/tr/providers/tencent); [poyo.ai](https://poyo.ai/zh/providers/hunyuan)). Tencent Cloud's own endpoints are listed for China, Singapore, Jakarta, Bangkok, Seoul, Tokyo, Virginia and Silicon Valley, with **no EU region**, which means client photos from European merchants leave the EU ([Tencent Cloud intl docs](https://intl.cloud.tencent.com/document/product/1284/75533)).

The second tier is either expensive, parametric or shut down. Shutterstock's NVIDIA Edify-based API, marketed as trained "exclusively on Shutterstock content", charges 300 credits for a full image-to-3D model on $25-per-2,500-credit packs, about **$3 per model**, and its 2026 status was not re-verified ([CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/News/2024/Shutterstock-launches-first-ethical-generative-3.aspx)). CSM's Cube spans free to $2,000 a month across six plans with API access bundled at mid-tier ([costbench CSM](https://costbench.com/software/ai-3d-generation/csm/)); Sloyd is template-parametric and cannot reproduce a specific client product ([Sloyd blog](https://sloyd.ai/blog/3d-ai-price-comparison)); Stability removed its $0.04-per-generation Stable Point Aware 3D endpoint ([Puter](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/stability-ai-api-pricing/)). Adobe Substance 3D Sampler's image-to-3D is a desktop feature inside a $24.99/month Texturing plan, not an API ([schneider.im](https://www.schneider.im/lu/adobe-substance-3d-price-increases-and-innovations/)).

Three facts decide how to use these vendors. First, every hosted auto-rigger (Tripo Rig, Meshy rigging, KIRI's auto-rig) targets humanoid characters; no vendor advertises garment rigging or wearable attachment points, so the earlier report's finding that open auto-rigging is immature is not solved by paying, only made cheaper to try. Second, the quad-topology race (Tripo P2.0 native quad, Rodin quad-dominant, Hunyuan 3.1 quad) maps directly onto Shopify's standard of "four-sided geometry, product origin at the base, and UV shells manually unwrapped on a single texture map", and the same industry article is the only commerce-specific quality statement found: "reflective, monochrome, and very thin geometry reconstruct badly across every generator on the market" ([ecommercefastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/ai-3d-model-generators-shopify-product-pages/)). Third, the multi-view inputs (Rodin 5, Hunyuan 8) are the hosted answer to back-side hallucination that TRELLIS.2, documented only as single-image, cannot give; the Hunyuan3D 1.0 paper documents that single-image conditioning "tended to generate dark shadows in back views" ([arXiv 2411.02293](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02293)), though no independent benchmark quantifies how much multi-view conditioning helps on commerce products. The licence terms are workable only on paid tiers: Tripo grants paid users "full rights to use, modify, distribute, and commercially exploit" outputs while free-tier outputs are CC BY 4.0 or vendor-retained depending on which help page you read ([Tripo: commercial use](https://www.tripo3d.ai/help/privacy-policy/how-to-use-tripo-models-commercially)); Meshy's free tier is likewise CC BY 4.0 with private ownership on Pro and Studio ([terms.law comparison](https://terms.law/FAQ/ai-tools/ai-platform-terms-comparison-faq.html)); and the open Hunyuan3D 2.0 community licence requires a separate licence above **1 million monthly active users** and forbids using outputs "to train other models to perform similarly" ([Hunyuan3D-2 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2/blob/main/LICENSE)). No SLA, uptime commitment or explicit no-training-on-inputs clause was readable for any vendor, which makes those contract questions for an enterprise tier rather than something to assume.

The AI-plus-human tier is where a guarantee finally appears, at one to two orders of magnitude higher cost. Modelry (CGTrader) builds a custom model "from $60/model, which includes 3D expert consultation and Quality Assurance", delivers glTF/GLB/USDZ plus Meta and Google formats, quotes within 24 hours (usually under three) and delivers "within days" ([CGTrader Arsenal FAQ](https://arsenal.cgtrader.com/faq-category/plans-pricing)). Kaedim's plans run **$400/month for 20 credits (Indie) and $1,200/month for 60 (Pro)** with $22 and $16 per extra credit; because the vendor describes Indie as "up to 20 geometries or 1 complete asset per month", a finished textured asset effectively costs about $400 ([costbench Kaedim](https://costbench.com/software/ai-3d-generation/kaedim/)). Kaedim claims a **39-hour average turnaround** against a 70-hour market average ([Kaedim Sifu case study](https://www.kaedim3d.com/case-study/dynamic/sifu)), and a 2023 investigation found its "AI" relied on quality-control workers paid "$1 to $4 per model" and expected to finish each submission in 15 minutes, after which the company relabelled the product as machine learning plus an in-house art team ([ACS Information Age](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2023/ai-startup--magically--generating-3d-images-was-actually-using-humans.html)); that history is useful as a cost floor for what a trained human pass actually costs. Hexa, which Amazon chose to give sellers 3D access, pairs "custom AI and a managed crowd of over 30K artists" for clients including Macy's, Logitech and Crate & Barrel but publishes no per-SKU price ([AWS Marketplace: Hexa](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-txyyk27uo54fy); [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/?p=133318)). Agency and freelance rates bracket the top: "professional 3D product models typically range from $200 to $800 per model" with 2–4 business days for a single render and 3–6 weeks for a catalogue ([Frame Sixty](https://framesixty.com/3d-product-rendering-services/)), while Fiverr's category averages are **$64.97 for jewelry modelling and $105.86 for 3D garment design** and gigs explicitly sell "GLB, GLTF and USDZ models for Shopify, Amazon, AR" ([Fiverr 3D artist cost guide](https://fiverr.com/resources/guides/costs/3d-artist); [Fiverr gig](https://fiverr.com/dima_maksimuk/create-3d-glb-gltf-usdz-models-for-shopify-amazon-and-ar)). The most instructive benchmark is Amazon's own free programme: brand-registered sellers scan with the Seller app on iOS 17+, "a 3D artist will review each scanned model for quality", and "approximately three weeks are needed" ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/33bb149b-1dfb-4334-ac37-410137358732)). If Amazon, with every resource, still routes a phone scan through a human artist, Symspace's interim human pass is not a weakness but the industry baseline.

## True scale comes from LiDAR, markers or a typed dimension, never the photo

Metric scale is the one property that neither generative AI nor photo-only photogrammetry can produce, and the sourced evidence is unambiguous about where it does come from. Apple's own engineers state that supplying a `depthDataMap` in a `PhotogrammetrySample` "can help PhotogrammetrySession determine the real-world scale of the photographed object", that the LiDAR point cloud "is gathered by `ObjectCaptureSession` and stored in the images it writes", and that "there is no public API for supplying one as an input" ([Apple Developer Forums 840946](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840946)); developers nonetheless report that models "sometimes produce real scale and sometimes don't, even with controlled variables" ([Apple forums WWDC23-10191 tag](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/tags/wwdc2023-10191)). The guided iOS capture needs an iPhone 12 Pro or later or a 5th-generation iPad Pro, and Apple's recommended capture is **a minimum of 100 images at 70% overlap** with the whole object in every frame ([Reality Composer](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1462358802); [Vuforia on Apple Object Capture](https://developer.vuforia.com/library/vuforia-engine/images-and-objects/model-targets/using-3d-scans/model-targets-apples-object-capture/)). That is a 3–10 minute guided session, not the "20–50 casual photos" a merchant will tolerate, and it fixes the role of scanning: a ground-truth source for matte, opaque goods when a client is willing, not the default intake.

The desktop and mobile tools are mature and mostly cheap. Epic's RealityScan 2.0 (the renamed RealityCapture, June 2025) added AI masking and improved alignment and is "free to use for students, educators, and developers with an annual gross revenue below $1 million" ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=167877)). Polycam Pro costs about **$26.99/month**, allows up to 2,000 images per capture and exports USDZ, OBJ, GLB and FBX, while the free tier restricts export to glTF ([aipicks Polycam guide](https://aipicks.jp/mag/polycam-guide-2026); [Skyebrowse comparison](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/polycam-vs-scaniverse)). KIRI Engine's free plan includes LiDAR object capture and unlimited exports, and its **Pro plan at $79.99/year** adds featureless-object scanning, PBR materials, quad mesh, auto-rig and a 500-photo cap ([KIRI pricing](https://kiriengine.app/pricing)). Scaniverse is the only app with "free, unlimited, on-device splat processing" and mesh export without subscription ([Skyebrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/polycam-vs-scaniverse)). Luma should be excluded: its Flythroughs product shut down on 1 January 2026 and capture queue times had earlier stretched "from 15 minutes to a day, then to a week" ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/luma-ai-to-sunset-flythroughs-on-january-1-2026)). On the open side, Meshroom is MPL-2.0 ([Meshroom](https://github.com/alicevision/Meshroom)) and COLMAP reconstructions get metric scale from the `aruco-estimator` package, which needs the marker "visible in at least two images" and ideally five ([aruco-estimator](https://pypi.org/project/aruco-estimator)); Gaussian-splat captures need SuGaR or 2DGS mesh extraction before any retopology, which keeps that path research-grade ([SuGaR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12775)). Reflective products defeat all of it: jewelry "surfaces are often reflective and shiny, which complicates scanning, and a washable matte spray can reduce reflections" ([Romanoff](https://romanoff.com/optimized-preparation-and-scanning-process-for-jewelry-models)), cross-polarised lighting is the alternative ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/capturing-reflective-objects-in-3d)), and the realistic in-house device for small shiny items is a structured-light scanner such as the Revopoint MINI 2 at **0.02 mm precision for 10–500 mm objects, about $829** ([3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/a-closer-look-at-revopoints-new-mini-2-3d-scanner-technical-specifications-and-pricing-227920/)), with transparent lenses and gemstones still needing manual material work.

The decision this supports is simple and cheap. Scale certainty ranks LiDAR-assisted Object Capture and structured-light scanners first, marker-based photogrammetry second, and photo-only photogrammetry and every generative model last, so the pipeline should **make product dimensions a mandatory field at upload** and rescale the GLB from that field regardless of which generator produced it, exactly as the earlier report's `dimensions_m.source` ordering specifies. The practical hybrid, when a merchant can take more than one photo, is three to six views (front, back, left, right, optionally top and bottom) plus dimensions, fed to a multi-view model (Rodin's five images or Hunyuan 3.1's eight hosted, or Hunyuan3D-2mv's front/left/back/right in ComfyUI, whose node "integrates multiple image perspectives to enhance the 3D reconstruction accuracy" ([ComfyUI Hunyuan3D multiview node](https://docs.comfy.org/built-in-nodes/Hunyuan3Dv2ConditioningMultiView))) so the back side is observed rather than hallucinated. Where a client ships physical samples of shoes, bags or furniture, a LiDAR Object Capture scan becomes the geometry ground truth and the generator supplies only texture completion, but no off-the-shelf tool fuses the two, so that is custom engineering for a later phase.

## A three.js review tool and a five-minute lint make the human pass fast and data-producing

No open project does what Symspace's reviewer needs: load a product GLB, click one to three attachment points, type dimensions, pick a category and front axis, and export a Unity-oriented sidecar JSON. The building blocks exist and the build is days, not months. The standard web gizmo is three.js `TransformControls`, which Needle Engine wraps with snapping and keyboard shortcuts ([Needle TransformGizmo](https://engine.needle.tools/docs/api/TransformGizmo)), combined with `GLTFLoader`, `OrbitControls` and a `Raycaster` for face picking. The write-back layer should be glTF-Transform (MIT): its CLI already offers `inspect`, `validate`, `center --pivot center|above|below` ("above" puts the model on the floor at bottom-centre), `prune`, `dedup`, `weld`, `simplify`, `resize`, `draco`, `meshopt` and `xmp` for `KHR_xmp_json_ld` packets, but notably **no `scale`, `rotate` or general `transform` command**, so node transforms and `extras` must be written through its Node API ([glTF-Transform cli.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/donmccurdy/glTF-Transform/main/packages/cli/src/cli.ts); [glTF-Transform KHRXMP](https://gltf-transform.donmccurdy.com/modules/extensions/classes/KHRXMP)). For in-browser editing that previews losslessly, `@gltf-transform/view` "keeps the three.js scene graph updated in realtime as changes are made, providing a lossless workflow to load, view, edit, and export glTF assets", in explicit contrast to `THREE.GLTFExporter`, which its author calls "lossy and expensive" ([npm @gltf-transform/view](https://npmjs.com/package/@gltf-transform/view)). The fastest prototype, one to two days, is Google's `<model-viewer>` editor: its hotspot workflow already records a clicked surface's `data-position` and `data-normal`, which is precisely an attachment point plus normal ([model-viewer annotations](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/annotations/)), but it lacks dimension entry, pivot and front-axis controls, so it is a stepping stone rather than the end state. Babylon's Sandbox and the three.js editor should be used as viewers only, because Babylon's GLB re-export "is inherently a lossy step" ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/sandbox-glb-exporter-question/9856/9)) and three.js editor scripts cannot reliably write `userData` ([three.js discourse](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-can-i-set-an-objects-userdata-using-a-script-in-the-editor/76935)). Gestaltor, the one native glTF editor, lost its free community version ([GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/gestaltor-gltf-swiss-army-knife/)), and PlayCanvas's now-MIT editor is a general scene tool rather than an annotation one ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/8/playcanvas-editor-frontend-is-now-open-source-with-mit-license)). If reviewers are 3D-literate, a Blender `bpy` add-on with four buttons ("origin to bottom-centre", "set real height", "add anchor Empty at cursor", "export with Custom Properties") is the strongest alternative, because Empties export as glTF nodes and custom properties land in node `extras` when "Include → Custom Properties" is selected ([glTF-Blender-IO docs](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/blob/main/docs/blender_docs/scene_gltf2.rst)); its cost is a desktop install and Blender's UX for non-artists.

Two design choices make the tool's output survive regeneration and reach Unity without plugins. Store each picked point the way KeypointNet does, as `face_index` plus barycentric `face_uv` plus `xyz`, not as a vertex index, because TRELLIS re-meshing or glTF-Transform `simplify` renumbers vertices while a barycentric point can be re-projected onto a new mesh by nearest surface point ([KeypointNet](https://github.com/qq456cvb/KeypointNet)). And name anchors with a fixed vocabulary that Unity can find by string. There is no Khronos standard for wearable anchors; the precedents are Snap's fixed attach-point list (Head Center, Candide Center, Left and Right Eyeball) ([Snap: head-attached objects](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/ar-tracking/face/head-attached-3d-objects)), Mixamo's `mixamorig:` prefix ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/pre-rigged-blender-character-quot-unable-to-map-your-existing-skeleton-quot/m-p/12196889)), VRM's index-mapped and unique `humanBones` ([VRM 1.0 humanoid](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/humanoid.md)) and Khronos 3D Commerce's `model3d` XMP namespace for AR orientation metadata ([Khronos blog](https://www.khronos.org/blog/pervasive-asset-metadata-in-3dcommerce)). The proposed convention, which is this report's synthesis rather than a published standard, is a reserved `anchor_` prefix in lower snake_case using the 3D Commerce file-name character set, one node per attachment frame as a child of the product root at identity scale, with +Y as the functional axis and +Z as outward: `anchor_ring_axis`, `anchor_watch_band`, `anchor_glasses_bridge`, `anchor_earring_hook`, `anchor_shoe_heel`, `anchor_head_top`, and `anchor_floor` / `anchor_wall` / `anchor_table` for placement categories, plus an `extras.symspace_anchor` block carrying kind, version, source (human or auto) and confidence for tools that read extras. The name stays authoritative because UnityGLTF's README documents import plugins and `IExtension` deserialisation but never states that node names are preserved as GameObject names ([UnityGLTF README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/dev/README.md)); glTFast documents a custom-extras add-on API if that loader is ever adopted ([glTFast custom extras](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cloud.gltfast@6.9/manual/UseCaseCustomExtras.html)). Both must be verified on one asset in the first week.

The reviewer's lint stack is free and already encodes most commerce thresholds. The Khronos glTF Validator CLI writes a JSON report per file, shows warnings with `-a` and batches directories with thread control ([glTF-Validator README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator/main/README.md)); `gltf-transform inspect` prints triangle, primitive, material and texture statistics; and the Khronos glTF Asset Auditor ships defaults of **100,000 triangles, 5 materials and 512–2048 px textures** and checks file size, clean root origin, hard edges, non-manifold edges, dimensions with tolerance, power-of-two textures, PBR colour range, UVs in 0–1, inverted and overlapping UVs and texel density, with the caveat that "files with Draco mesh compression are unsupported" ([3DC-Validator](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3DC-Validator)). Those defaults align with the platform envelopes the notes collected: Apple's 100K polygons, one 2048² texture set and 10 s of animation for Quick Look back to iPhone 7 ([Apple forums 693867](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/693867)); Shopify's "generally less than 15 MB" with one base colour, one normal and one packed occlusion-roughness-metalness map ([Shopify checklist](https://help.shopify.com/partners/resources/creating-3d-models/3d-model-standards-checklist)); Amazon's 200,000 triangles and 5 MB ([Neural4D Amazon guide](https://blog.neural4d.com/image-to-3d/3d-product-rendering-for-ecommerce-amazon-glb-guide-2026/)); WANNA's **65,000 triangles per shoe, 2048² textures, one UV set, left and right as separate models and the toe pointing +X** ([WANNA shoes](https://wanna.helpjuice.com/shoes-category/shoes-3d-models)); and Snap's 100,000 triangles or 60,000 for skinned meshes ([Snap asset guide](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/assets-pipeline/asset-library/asset-library-publishing-guide)). The five-minute checklist therefore reads: validator clean; triangles under 100k (65k shoes, 60k skinned); textures ≤2048² and power-of-two; file ≤10–15 MB; bounding box within ±2% of the spec dimensions; root at origin with the pivot at the contract point; front on +Z in a viewer; anchor nodes present with contract names and extras; single UV set in 0–1 with no inverted islands; metal/rough materials with binary metallic; no stray animations or skins; Draco off until the final pass. Blender's 3D-Print Toolbox (Extensions version 1.4.0, 23 July 2026) covers manifold, intersection, degenerate and thickness checks plus "Make Manifold" ([extensions.blender.org](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/print3d-toolbox/versions/)), and a ten-line `trimesh` script asserts `extents`, `is_watertight` and the presence of anchor names in `scene.graph` ([trimesh](https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh)).

Commercial asset-management and AR platforms do not remove the need for this tool, and most are priced for a different problem. Echo3D offers a free DAM/CDN/optimisation tier and Pro at **$99/month** ([Capterra echo3D](https://www.capterra.com/p/206459/echo3D/pricing/)); Sketchfab Pro is $15/month and Premium $79/month billed yearly, with its store migrating to Epic's Fab ([Capterra Sketchfab](https://www.capterra.com/p/178043/Sketchfab-3D-Visualization/pricing)); Zakeke's self-service configurator runs $19.99 to $149.90 a month by product count, with a conflicting "$68/month plus transaction fees" figure elsewhere ([SoftwareSuggest Zakeke](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/zakeke/pricing); [fibbl](https://fibbl.com/zakeke-pricing/)); Threekit is an enterprise platform estimated at **$50,000–$150,000+ per year** with onboarding "only handled with the help of their team or partners" ([framesixty](https://framesixty.com/3d-configurator/); [Zakeke vs Threekit](https://www.zakeke.com/?p=60411)); VNTANA sells CAD-to-lightweight conversion with "approval workflows ... version control and audit trails" without public pricing ([vntana.com](https://vntana.com/)). Dopple is the only one that documents an in-platform glTF editor with an edit mode, and its pricing is unpublished ([Dopple glTF editor](https://docs.dopple.io/platform/gltf-editor)). None advertises ring-axis or glasses-bridge anchor metadata; the merchant contract everywhere is "upload a correctly authored GLB at real scale with the origin at the base" ([Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com.au/blog/3d-model-products)). Echo3D's free tier or Sketchfab Pro are worth using as hosting, preview and approval links for clients; the anchor configuration stays in-house.

## Blender and Unity SOPs budget 20–60 minutes per rigid asset and half a day per garment

The free toolchain is sufficient for the whole non-rigged path. The Khronos glTF add-on's README states that "4.5.x is the current Blender LTS release" and "5.0 is the current stable release", and the add-on ships bundled since Blender 2.80 ([glTF-Blender-IO README](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO)); the add-on's `main` branch already declares version 5.3.36 against Blender 5.3 ([io_scene_gltf2 `__init__.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/addons/io_scene_gltf2/__init__.py)). Standardise on **Blender 4.5 LTS** for reproducibility. The exporter's exact option strings matter for the SOP: "+Y Up — Export using glTF convention, +Y up" (default on), "Custom Properties — Export custom properties as glTF extras", "Apply Modifiers — Apply modifiers (excluding Armatures) to mesh objects", Draco compression with a 0–6 level, "Images — Output format for images. PNG is lossless and generally preferred", and the skinning trio "Skinning", "Export Deformation Bones Only" and "Use Rest Position Armature" ([io_scene_gltf2 `__init__.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Blender-IO/main/addons/io_scene_gltf2/__init__.py)). The conventions the file must satisfy are Khronos's "in glTF 1 unit = 1 meter", 1:1 real size, "bottom center of the product to be placed at 0,0,0" and "upright and forward" ([Khronos 3DC guidelines v2](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/3DC-Asset-Creation/blob/main/asset-creation-guidelines/RealtimeAssetCreationGuidelines.md)), and Apple's "+Y for up, +Z for forward, and +X for right" ([Apple: validating a model for motion capture](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/validating-a-model-for-motion-capture)). The ordered Blender SOP is: import glTF; set scene units to metric at unit scale 1.0; type the spec dimensions into the N-panel and apply all transforms; verify with MeasureIt (1.8.3, now on the Extensions platform) or the 3D-Print Toolbox "scale to bounds" ([extensions.blender.org MeasureIt](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/measureit/versions/)); snap the 3D cursor to the pivot point (bottom vertex loop for floor items, contact point for wearables) and set origin to cursor; rotate so the product's front faces Blender −Y, which the +Y-up export maps to glTF +Z (an inference from the option and the glTF convention, to be confirmed on one test asset because the Blender manual was unreachable); add Empties named per the anchor convention and parent them to the root; add custom properties such as `category`, `front_axis` and `real_height_m`; export GLB with Custom Properties on, +Y Up on, Apply Modifiers on, Draco off. The cleanup order that fits AI meshes is Merge by Distance, delete loose shells, 3D-Print Toolbox "Make Manifold", then Decimate or Voxel Remesh followed by QuadriFlow (MIT) or Instant Meshes only when the budget demands it ([QuadriFlow](https://github.com/hjwdzh/QuadriFlow); [Instant Meshes](https://github.com/wjakob/instant-meshes)), and a Cycles re-bake of base colour and normal from the original high-poly. One handedness trap deserves a rule: UnityGLTF "converts handedness by multiplying by a negative X scale" ([UnityGLTF issue #257](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF/issues/257)), so anything left/right-dependent (shoes, earrings) must be named by anatomical side and checked in Unity rather than trusted from glTF ±X.

Time per asset has only vendor-blog support, but the numbers are consistent. A 2026 cleanup guide puts medium-complexity prop cleanup at **30–45 minutes for Meshy output and 20–30 minutes for Tripo output**, manual retopology at 30–60 minutes for a prop and 2–4 hours for characters with deformation requirements, and simple props at 5–15 minutes of retopology ([Neural4D cleanup guide](https://newsletter.neural4d.com/p/ai-3d-model-blender-cleanup-complete-mesh-fix-guide-2026)). That yields a planning budget of 20–60 minutes for props, furniture and jewelry that need only scale, pivot, anchors and light cleanup, plus 30–60 minutes when retopology and baking are required, and hours for garments. The Unity side of the SOP is template-driven. UnityGLTF supports editor import of a `.glb` dropped into Assets with Legacy, Mecanim or Humanoid animation import and runtime import through `GLTFComponent` or `GLTFSceneImporter`, on Unity LTS versions 2021.3+, 2022.3+ and 6000.0+ ([UnityGLTF](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/UnityGLTF)); glTFast shows "import settings and eventual warnings/errors in the Inspector" if the team ever wants a second opinion ([glTFast ImportEditor](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cloud.gltfast@6.2/manual/ImportEditor.html)). **Unity 6.3 LTS has been available since 4 December 2025** and Unity 6.2 is no longer supported ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/unity-6-3-lts-is-now-available); [Unity Manual: AR Foundation](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/com.unity.xr.arfoundation.html)). Build one `<Category>_Template` prefab per category: a root with identity transform and an `ARProductConfig` component, a `PivotWrapper` child whose local offset cancels the imported pivot so the root sits at the contract pivot, the imported glTF instance, and anchor children either found by name from the glTF node tree or placed by hand; make prefab variants per SKU so scripts and anchor names are inherited. Neither importer re-pivots on import, so fix the file upstream with `gltf-transform center --pivot above` or accept the wrapper. A small `[MenuItem]` Editor script walks the prefab, reads anchor transforms and root-space `Renderer.bounds`, and writes the sidecar JSON, which closes the loop with the automated configurator's schema. For body-driven items, AR Foundation's samples ship a **91-joint** `BoneController` that matches bones to ARKit joint names by `Enum.TryParse` and applies `localPose` per joint, with the skeleton prefab instantiated under the `ARHumanBody` trackable ([BoneController.cs](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/arfoundation-samples/blob/main/Assets/Scripts/Runtime/BoneController.cs); [HumanBodyTracker.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/arfoundation-samples/main/Assets/Scripts/Runtime/HumanBodyTracker.cs)), while the AR Foundation 6.1 manual describes 93 joints for 3D detection, a discrepancy to check against the current manual ([AR Foundation body tracking](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.arfoundation@6.1/manual/features/body-tracking.html)). XR Simulation does not cover face or body tracking, so those categories need device builds for every review.

Garments are where the SOP becomes a project. ARKit's motion-capture contract is explicit: the model "must use a scene coordinate system that uses +Y for up, +Z for forward, and +X for right", the skeleton "must exactly match ARKit's required joint names", it must be "rigged in a standard T-pose", "no more than four joints should contribute to the movement of any single vertex", and "the Motion Capture feature doesn't retarget motion", so proportions that differ substantially from Apple's Biped-Robot skeleton "may not perform as expected" ([Apple: rigging a model](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/rigging-a-model-for-motion-capture); [Apple: validating a model](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/validating-a-model-for-motion-capture)). The rigging tools are in flux: Mixamo is "still online and still free with an Adobe account, but it's in maintenance mode", with authenticated uploads and downloads failing since **16 June 2025** ([Cinevva: Mixamo and alternatives](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-character-animations-rigging)); Auto-Rig Pro costs **$25 Lite or $50 Full** (Smart biped detection, Unity/Unreal exporters, Remap retargeting) with 10-seat team licences at $162 ([Sorceress blog](https://sorceress.games/blog/auto-rig-pro-free-download-the-honest-2026-path)); Rigify is free in Blender; Marvelous Designer 2026 is **$39/month or $280/year personal**, $199/month enterprise, with a quiet indie tier for studios under $500,000 revenue ([CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/marvelous-designer-introduces-indie-pricing.html); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/01/12/marvelous-designer-adds-indie-pricing-quietly/)). Community practice is to rig the nude mannequin and "copy weights to all of your clothing items" ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/257400/discussions/0/522728814507927054)), which Weightflow Pro and similar paid add-ons automate for "body to clothing" ([Weightflow Pro](https://superhivemarket.com/products/weightflow-pro/docs)). The manual garment SOP is therefore: separate the garment from the generated mannequin; rig the mannequin with Rigify or Auto-Rig Pro; transfer weights by Data Transfer modifier (nearest face interpolated) and Limit Total to four influences; delete the mannequin or keep an invisible depth-only occluder copy, the same idea as Snap's Face Occluder; T-pose, +Z forward, metres; export with Skinning, Deformation Bones Only and Rest Position Armature on; then in Unity either rename bones to ARKit names and drive them with the samples' `BoneController` (no retargeting, best fidelity) or import as Humanoid, Enforce T-Pose and retarget through an Animator. Using the vendor's 1–4 hour character retopology figures plus rigging, weight transfer and device testing, expect **half a day to a full day per garment** from a technical artist; this is an estimate with no published practitioner figure behind it, and it is the strongest argument for the earlier report's recommendation to ship garments as 2D Leffa try-on and rendered video first.

Who does this work is a routing decision, and the costs bracket cleanly. In-house, the review-tool pass is 5–15 minutes for a trained person. Outsourced micro-fixes exist on Fiverr and Blender Artists, where a "GLB file touch-up" job was budgeted at **$40–$80** and scan cleanup gigs run $50–$400 with one-day to multi-week delivery ([Blender Artists job post](https://blenderartists.org/t/paid-3d-model-cleanup-for-3d-printing-glb-file-touch-up-40-80/1646418); [Fiverr scan cleanup](https://fiverr.com/abatusha/clean-repair-and-optimize-3d-scan-files-for-3d-printing)); a retainer at roughly $10–$25 per asset for "pivot, scale, orientation, three anchors" is a plausible extrapolation from those single-job prices, not a sourced rate. Managed services (Modelry at $60, Kaedim's $16–$22 artist credits) are priced for creating models, so using them only to configure AI output overpays unless geometry cleanup is also needed ([Kaedim docs: pricing](https://docs.kaedim3d.com/kaedim-co-creation/pricing)). Crowd QA is suited to binary judgments ("is this the front?", "is the scale plausible against the reference cube?") rather than anchor placement: Toloka's own numbers are "$0.01 for simple tasks and $2 for 3D labeling", with 3D labeling taking "at least half an hour", quality enforced by gold questions, overlap and majority vote ([Toloka blog](https://join.toloka.ai/blog/identifying-objects-tasks); [Toloka docs](https://toloka.ai/docs/guide/save-money/)). Retailers at a Khronos BoF reported "15–20% of effort in asset creation going to quality assurance, and over 30 major parameters" ([Khronos BoF](https://www.khronos.org/developers/linkto/how-do-we-solve-the-challenges-of-gltf-asset-creation-bof)), which is the planning ratio to use. The routing policy that follows: in-house reviewer for every asset; Fiverr or Upwork retainer when the queue exceeds one reviewer's day; Modelry or an agency for reflective jewelry, eyewear with transparent lenses, thin straps and any SKU that fails review twice; a technical artist (in-house or MD-equipped freelancer at Fiverr's $106 garment average) for garments; crowd triage only once there is volume to justify writing gold questions.

## Templated Blender turntables ship product video today while hosted AI supplies only the plate

The earlier report settled the video architecture (render first with headless Blender, restyle under Wan 2.2 control, use LTX-2.5 for audio-bearing cuts); this section covers the tooling around that decision and the manual steps that keep it reliable. The deterministic path is cheap in wall-clock terms: one animation test rendered about **1 s/frame in EEVEE versus 7–8 s/frame in Cycles** without denoising, and another put Cycles at 1:14–1:26 per frame against EEVEE's 6 s ([GameDev.tv thread](https://community.gamedev.tv/t/rendering-an-animation-challenge-eevee-vs-cycles/185423)); on an RTX 4090 with OptiX, Blender's benchmark scenes render in roughly 3–7.4 s per frame and OptiX is 6–7× faster than CPU-only ([OpenBenchmarking](https://mail.openbenchmarking.org/result/2509258-NE-GPUV4409013); [CGDirector](https://www.cgdirector.com/blender-benchmark-results-updated-scores/)). A 10 s, 24 fps, 1080p turntable is 240 frames, so EEVEE takes about four minutes and Cycles on a 4090-class GPU 12–30 minutes; these are extrapolations from benchmark scenes, and a lightly textured product on an HDRI with denoising will usually be faster. Poly Haven's 980+ HDRIs are CC0, usable commercially without attribution ([Poly Haven FAQ](https://polyhaven.com/faq)). Turntable automation is either a ~50-line `bpy` camera-orbit script, the GPL-3.0 "Carousel" add-on whose maintenance against Blender 4.x/5.x is unverified ([Korchy/blender_carousel](https://github.com/Korchy/blender_carousel)), or commercial add-ons such as SpinWiz, which batches multiple models through a render queue on Blender 4.1+ ([SpinWiz FAQ](https://blendermarket.com/products/spinwiz/faq)). Unity is ruled out for the server: the Recorder cannot capture in `-batchmode` because "the graphics pipeline is not initialized", and it works only in the Editor ([Unity Recorder known issues](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.recorder@5.1/manual/KnownIssues.html)). For titles and overlays, Remotion's licence (verified on GitHub) is free only for individuals and companies of up to three employees; larger companies need a company licence at **$0.01 per render with a $100/month minimum** for automated products ([Remotion LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md); [Remotion company licenses](https://www.remotion.dev/blog/company-licenses)), so Motion Canvas (MIT) or MoviePy (MIT, v2.0 with breaking changes, FFmpeg required) are the free alternatives ([Motion Canvas](https://github.com/motion-canvas/motion-canvas); [MoviePy](https://github.com/Zulko/moviepy)). Hosted template APIs cannot render 3D but are a cheap assembly layer for a pre-rendered alpha turntable: Shotstack starts at **$49/month for 300 video minutes** (pay-as-you-go $0.045–$0.195 per minute), Creatomate at $39/month for 1,000 credits, JSON2Video at $49.95/month for 200 Full HD minutes ([Submagic Shotstack](https://www.submagic.co/blog/shotstack-pricing); [Capterra Creatomate](https://www.capterra.com/p/268283/Creatomate/); [JSON2Video plans](https://json2video.com/docs/v2/pricing/plans)).

Hosted generative video is the plate, not the product, and the price band is wide. Per-second list prices in October 2026 run from about **$0.01/s (Vidu Q2 Turbo 540p) to $0.60/s (Veo 3.1 4K)**, with a dense middle at $0.05–$0.12/s: Runway Gen-4 Turbo $0.05/s and Gen-4.5 $0.12/s on $0.01 credits ([Creatify Runway pricing](https://creatify.ai/blog/runway-pricing-(2026)-plans-credits-and-what-you-ll-actually-pay)); Veo 3.1 at $0.40/s (1080p), Fast at $0.10–$0.12/s, Lite at $0.05–$0.08/s ([benchlm Veo](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/veo)); Sora 2 at $0.10/s and $0.05/s batch, Pro to $0.50/s ([benchlm Sora](https://benchlm.ai/media-pricing/sora)); Kling 2.5 Turbo at about $0.07/s ([Akool Kling](https://akool.com/blog-posts/kling-2-5-cost-guide)); Seedance 1.0 Pro at $0.024–$0.122/s by resolution ([Opper Seedance](https://opper.ai/bytedance/seedance-1-0-pro)); Amazon Nova Reel at $0.08/s of 720p ([Amazon press release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/2024/12/introducing-amazon-nova-a-new-generation-of-foundation-models)); Luma Ray 3.2 at ~$0.24/s at 1080p ([Hooked Luma](https://www.hooked.so/compare/luma-ai-pricing)). A 10 s clip therefore lists at $0.10–$6 before the 3–5× retry factor that product-accurate work needs. Two terms issues matter: Google embeds SynthID in every Veo output and it cannot be disabled, with no visible watermark on paid tiers but the provenance mark always present ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/google-expands-digital-watermarks-to-ai-made-video-175232320.html)); and Adobe Firefly Video is the only vendor marketing itself as "commercially safe" on licensed training data, at $9.99–$29.99/month consumer plans and no published API price ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/adobe-launches-commercially-safe-ai-video-generation-model/)). The e-commerce generators are a complement for UGC-style b-roll rather than a substitute: Amazon's Ads Video Generator is free for US sellers and makes six ad variants in about five minutes from detail-page content, with editable text, CTA and logo overlays ([Amazon Seller Forums](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/f900e696-3d76-4017-b381-6ebe433c6c6d)); Creatify is $39–$99/month and claims to "put your real product into the video", yet reviewers report that "forcing an AI actor to 'hold' a complex physical product often produces warped hands or a floating item" ([Capterra Creatify](https://www.capterra.com/p/10020139/Creatify/); [Superscale review](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/creatify/review)). That negative evidence is the strongest available argument for rendering the product and generating only the surroundings.

The hybrid composite stack is open and the manual steps are few. IC-Light (Apache-2.0, SD1.5) relights a foreground against text or a supplied background, and its README recommends swapping the default non-commercial BRIA RMBG remover for BiRefNet ([IC-Light](https://github.com/lllyasviel/IC-Light)); the Flux-based V2 exists on fal and as a community Hugging Face mirror but its open-weight licence is unverified ([ComfyUI Wiki IC-Light V2](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/models/ic-light/ic-light-v2)). DepthFlow (AGPL-3.0) turns a still plate into a 2.5D parallax clip at up to 8K on an RTX 3060-class GPU, so keep it as an unmodified CLI subprocess to avoid the network copyleft clause ([DepthFlow](https://github.com/BrokenSource/DepthFlow)). fSpy (GPL-3.0) solves a camera from a still with an official Blender importer, so an AI-generated plate can be camera-matched and the product rendered into it ([fSpy](https://github.com/stuffmatic/fSpy)). The human interventions that practitioners converge on are seed discipline and overlay discipline: randomise seeds to explore, then "lock a seed to change one variable at a time" ([Cliprise seeds guide](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/guides/best-practices/seeds-consistency)); "generate four options ... select the best, and lock it before any video generation begins", a step one guide credits with over 40% fewer regenerations ([Cliprise pipelines](https://www.cliprise.app/learn/workflows/marketing/ai-video-generation-pipelines)); keep logos, fonts and CTAs as post-hoc overlays the way Amazon's and Creatify's tools do; respect 9:16 safe zones (TikTok's usable centre is about 886×1432 px after a 254 px top and 707 px bottom margin ([TryMyPost](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/tiktok-ad-specs-2026-safe-zones))); and record the music and voice licence per asset, because Suno's free tier is "non-commercial permanently" even after subscribing ([dailyaifixs](https://dailyaifixs.com/blog/suno-pricing-2026-free-songs-stay-non-commercial)), Pixabay Music allows commercial use without attribution ([MusicGPT guide](https://musicgpt.com/blog/truly-safe-copyright-free-music/)), Epidemic Sound's commercial tiers are quoted inconsistently at $20–$49/month ([Recurdash](https://recurdash.com/subscription-pricing/epidemic-sound)), and ElevenLabs grants a commercial licence on every paid plan from $6/month ([bigvu](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plans-credits-commercial-rights-api-costs/)). Render once per SKU to a master with alpha, depth and shadow passes at the largest aspect, derive 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16 by crop in FFmpeg with per-platform margins, apply brand overlays and music last so a brand-kit change never triggers a re-render, cache on (GLB hash, template, HDRI, renderer-settings version), and cache AI plates per category and mood rather than per SKU. The resulting per-clip economics are compute-only for the template path, roughly $0.20–$1.00 for a hybrid with one cheap plate or b-roll clip, and $0.50–$30 per accepted fully generative clip after retries; the template path can be fully automatic, the hybrid same-day with one approval, the generative path one to two business days with a pick-of-four review.

## In-app calibration and a concierge mode make staff and shoppers the fit oracle

Consumer try-on products split cleanly on what they let a shopper adjust, and the split should shape Symspace's interim controls. Body-anchored try-on invests in sizing inputs rather than free-form nudging: Perfect Corp's YouCam asks users to "place their wrist across their phone screen and use the sizing toggle bar to match their wrist width" and to adjust on-screen ruler lines for finger width, recording "finger size in millimeters" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230524005034/en/Perfect-Corp.-Debuts-Advanced-Finger-and-Wrist-Sizing-Technology-for-Accurate-Product-Fitting-and-Realistic-Jewelry-Virtual-Try-On-Effects)); Warby Parker measures pupillary distance with the TrueDepth camera, "no card or ruler necessary", and limits storage of that data ([Warby Parker app](https://ca.warbyparker.com/app)); Zenni asks users to "hold up a plastic credit card to their forehead or enter their pupillary distance" ([Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/technology/eyewear-retailer-offers-try-before-you-buy-online)). Placement AR exposes gestures with a hard reset: AR Quick Look pinch-scales, double-tap resets scale to 100% and two-finger-rotates, and Apple lets developers disable scaling per model with `allowsContentScaling` so "the object scales back down to its true real-world size", which Apple frames as deliberate feedback that the object "cannot be scaled" ([WWDC19 session 612](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/612/); [Kodeco](https://www.kodeco.com/books/apple-augmented-reality-by-tutorials/v2.0/chapters/2-ar-quick-look)); IKEA Place claims 98% automatic scale accuracy and offers no resize ([Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/debug/ikea-place-app/)). The business numbers behind try-on are vendor and retailer claims only: Farfetch reported 10% of AR-engaged clients adding to bag over 14 weeks, +22% add-to-bag on watches and a 4% return-rate decrease, with none of those figures attributable to manual adjustment ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/farfetch-is-making-a-case-for-luxury-virtual-try-on/)). HCI evidence favours indirect, constrained controls over on-object manipulation: an 18-participant ISMAR study found a two-thumb technique beat alternatives on time and gesture count ([TUM record](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/touch-gestures-for-improved-3d-object-manipulation-in-mobile-augm/)), and a VRST 2018 evaluation found "a touch-based interface is significantly more efficient" for constrained manipulation ([TH Köln](https://cg.web.th-koeln.de/?p=1535)). Symspace's interim nudge controls for wearables should therefore be three on-screen sliders or steppers (size, slide along finger/wrist/temple, roll about the attachment axis) rather than free 6-DoF dragging of a small ring on a moving hand, always with a double-tap "true size" reset, and with scale locked or clamped to a narrow band whenever the SKU's dimensions are trusted, so a user cannot "fix" a bad pivot by scaling.

The calibration inputs have measurable accuracy only for glasses. An independent 44-subject study found the Warby Parker and Eye Measure apps achieved a **0.51 mm mean absolute error** on interpupillary distance against a digital pupillometer versus 1.375 mm for PDCheck ([PMC10389117](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10389117/)), so a TrueDepth-derived IPD from ARKit's `leftEyeTransform` and `rightEyeTransform` (eyeball centres, not pupils, so expect a systematic offset) plus temple width from the face mesh is accurate enough to size glasses with no user input, keeping manual PD entry as an override ([Apple: rightEyeTransform](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arfaceanchor/righteyetransform.md)). Ring and wrist sizing from monocular hand landmarks lacks absolute scale, and the pragmatic path is Perfect Corp's: a screen ruler calibrated with a payment card, which one consumer app claims improves precision from ±1 to ±0.5 ring sizes ([Camweara Ring Sizer](https://camweara.com/?p=19838)), a vendor claim without independent validation. Feet are the weak point: a scoping review of 26 foot-measurement apps found "all apps failed to meet even half of the measurement-specific criteria" for custom footwear ([PMC7974751](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7974751/)); an A4-sheet photo method reaches 1.5 mm error at a 10° camera angle but 3.4 mm at 15° ([PSU paper](https://elib.psu.by/bitstream/123456789/31131/1/191-195.pdf)); and Nike Fit's "within 2 millimetres" is an unvalidated vendor claim ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/nike-unveils-ar-feature-to-help-customers-find-the-perfect-shoe-fit/2019051043102)). A one-time typed shoe size mapped to a last-length table is therefore the cheapest reliable input, with the A4 method as a later upgrade. Store derived numbers (ring mm, wrist mm, shoe EU, PD mm) with consent and a `method` tag (typed, card-calibrated, TrueDepth), never raw face or hand data, following Warby Parker's own restraint.

The Unity implementation needs no new dependencies. XRI 3.0 deprecated the individual AR interactables in favour of `ARTransformer`, `ScreenSpaceRayInteractor` and the Input Readers architecture ([XRI What's new in 3.0](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@3.0/manual/whats-new-3.0.html)); `TouchscreenGestureInputController` recognises tap, drag, pinch, twist and two-finger drag ([XRI AR gestures](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@3.0/manual/ar-gestures.html)); and the AR Starter Assets sample ships a `Screen Space Ray Interactor` prefab and an `ARDemoScene` requiring AR Foundation 6.0+ and the URP AR renderer features ([XRI AR Starter Assets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit/master/Documentation~/samples-ar-starter-assets.md)). For placed objects, `ARTransformer` restricts movement to AR planes, filters by plane classification on AR Foundation 6, and ships defaults of `minScale` 0.25, `maxScale` 2, `scaleSensitivity` 0.75 and `elasticity` 0.15 with lerp-back past the limits ([ARTransformer.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit/master/Runtime/Interaction/Transformers/ARTransformer.cs)); tighten that to 0.9–1.1 when dimensions are trusted and the built-in elasticity becomes the snap-back UX. For wearables parented under a landmark-driven anchor, skip `ARTransformer` (plane raycasts are irrelevant) and use `XRGrabInteractable` with `XRGeneralGrabTransformer`, whose `permittedDisplacementAxes`, `constrainedAxisDisplacementMode`, `allowOneHandedScaling`, `clampScaling`, `minimumScaleRatio` and `maximumScaleRatio` give single-axis slide and clamped size with no custom code, plus a small custom `IXRGrabTransformer` for roll ([XRGeneralGrabTransformer.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit/master/Runtime/Interaction/Transformers/XRGeneralGrabTransformer.cs)); apply `XRGrabInteractable`-style smoothing to the landmark pose, not the user's offset, so adjustments feel immediate while tracker jitter is damped ([XRI XR Grab Interactable](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/needle-mirror/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit/master/Documentation~/xr-grab-interactable.md)). These property names were verified on the package's master branch (3.7.0-pre.1) and the changelog shows they predate 3.2.x; minor default changes between 3.2.1 and master are possible. Persist in two tiers, PlayerPrefs as a device-local cache that is "device-locked ... and vulnerable to tampering or clearing" ([Lion Studios docs](https://docs.lionstudios.cc/readme/features/storage/storage-types/playerprefs)) and Firestore for `users/{uid}/fitOverrides/{sku}` and the correction telemetry; and hold per-category defaults, feature flags (`fit_manual_enabled`, `fit_auto_apply_crowd_defaults`) and A/B variants in Firebase Remote Config, which supports in-app defaults, conditional values with top-condition precedence and `FetchAsync`/`ActivateAsync` in Unity ([Firebase Remote Config](https://firebase.google.com/docs/remote-config/parameters/client); [Firebase A/B codelab](https://firebase.google.cn/codelabs/implement-a-b-tests-with-firebase-remote-config)). Per-SKU overrides belong in Firestore with the product, not Remote Config, whose size limits were not verified.

The concierge mode is the same code path gated by a staff role. Every commercial try-on system puts a human before the shopper: Snap's wristwear and footwear templates require the creator to parent the model under Left/Right Wrist or the Foot Tracking component with an occluder and "position it similar to the example models" ([Snap Wristwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/4.55.1/references/templates/object/Try-On/wristwear-try-on); [Snap Footwear Try-On](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/features/try-on/foot-tracking)); Wanna publishes per-category positioning guides and a known-issues page for brand artists ([Wanna help center](https://wanna.helpjuice.com/watches-category/watches-positioning-guide)); Threekit onboarding is done "with the help of their team or partners" ([Zakeke vs Threekit](https://www.zakeke.com/?p=60411)). Symspace's version is a "Save as SKU default" button that writes `skus/{sku}/fitDefault = {transform in landmark frame, authorMeasurement, device, assetVersion}` to Firestore, so a staff member with a known finger or wrist size fits each wearable on their own hand before launch and the default is normalised to other users by their measurement. Pair it with a shopper-facing "report a fit problem" action that captures the same correction payload plus a reason (too big, too small, wrong spot, wrong side, floats, clips) and a landmark-overlaid screenshot into a review queue. No vendor publishes time-per-SKU for this placement step; Snap's "as fast as two minutes" applies to makeup lenses, not 3D fit ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2022/01/snapchat-rolls-out-catalog-powered-shopping-lenses)), so treat per-SKU time as an internal metric to measure in the first week, with the expectation that asset fixes (pivot, scale, axis) dominate and a glTF-bounds-plus-dimensions pre-step removes most of them.

## A correction flywheel and three phases hand the work to automation

Every manual action above is wasted unless it is stored as a labelled example, and the pattern for doing so is standard. The "data flywheel" is the effect by which "continued data collection and user-supplied labels allow models to be improved over time" ([arXiv 2104.11757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11757)); the canonical HITL loop runs "data collection, model inference, confidence scoring, routing logic, human review, and feedback ingestion" ([TrustLayer](https://trustlayer.io/resources/human-in-the-loop-a-practical-automation-framework)); a granted patent describes a fallback that fires "when the deployed model falls below a confidence threshold, but before human intervention is required" ([USPTO 11663409](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11663409)); and the practitioner pattern is a self-contained correction record "storing a clean copy of the original input, the AI's bad attempt, and the human's 'ground truth' correction as a perfect, portable training example" ([fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/ai-agent-data-flywheel.md)). Wayfair, whose 3D pipeline produces "app-ready real-scale 3D models" with machine-learning assistance, still runs "practical audits where employees physically check samples" ([Wayfair tech blog](https://www.aboutwayfair.com/tech-innovation/wayfairs-3d-model-api)), and Amazon describes its catalogue-scale 3D effort as "science-first automation" built on "reusable labeling pipelines, evaluation frameworks, and inference infrastructure" ([Amazon jobs](https://amazon.jobs/en/jobs/2401023/postdoctoral-scientist-visual-innovation-sciences)). No published AR-commerce work learns product-fit offsets from shopper adjustments, so the schema and thresholds below are this report's proposal built on that literature.

Two record types feed one gold set. The asset-level `ConfigCorrection` is written by the review tool, append-only per asset: `{asset_id, product_id, category, generator+version, input_image_hash, image_embedding, auto_config_version, reviewer_id, action: approve|edit|reject, auto_values, final_values: {scale_m, pivot_rule, front_axis, category, anchors: [{name, face_index, bary_uv, xyz, normal, axis_quat}]}, reject_reason, time_spent_s}`; keeping the auto-proposed values beside the final ones makes the delta the training signal. The runtime `FitCorrection` is written on each settle event (the user stops adjusting for a few seconds, taps "looks right" or adds to bag) and on every "report a fit problem": `{sku, category, assetVersion, configVersion, device, os, trackerType, initialTransform, finalTransform in the landmark frame (position m, rotation quat, uniform scale), deltaTransform, userMeasurement {ringMm|wristMm|shoeEU|pdMm, method}, timeToSettleMs, numGestures, abandoned, reported, reason, sessionId, hashed userId}`. Expressing corrections in the landmark frame (ring axis along the finger bone, watch on the wrist-centre frame, glasses on the face anchor, shoe on the ankle-to-toe frame) and normalising translation by the user's measurement makes them comparable across users and devices. Aggregation should use the median, not the mean: the crowd-labeling literature notes that "a more robust alternative often used in federated learning is the median integration rule" and that convex methods offer "innate robustness to outliers" for continuous labels ([arXiv 2407.06902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06902)). The proposed promotion rule, which has no published benchmark behind it, is a per-SKU median with MAD over at least five distinct users, a dispersion bound of under 5% on scale and under 2 mm on position, weighting by measurement quality (card-calibrated above typed above none), down-weighting abandoned sessions and treating explicit reports as high-precision negative labels; SKUs that meet the bound get their default auto-promoted, the rest go to the reviewer queue, and per-category medians of the delta become the prior applied to new SKUs before any user data exists. Nearest-neighbour auto-apply follows directly: embed the product image, retrieve approved records of the same category or product family, seed the configurator with their pivot rule, front axis and dimension prior, and transfer barycentric anchors by nearest surface point onto the new mesh.

The gold set then measures automation instead of guessing at it. Every reviewer-approved configuration is an evaluation case; the metrics are auto-approve rate at the current threshold, field-level agreement (scale within ±5%, front axis exact, anchor position error in millimetres and normal angle), and reviewer edit time; the automated configurator from the earlier report re-runs against the gold set on every model or prompt change. Routing starts at 100% human review and flips per category to auto-approve with random audit sampling once agreement exceeds a target such as 95% on scale and front axis, keeping anchors on human review longest because they carry the highest visible AR cost; VLM verdicts gate "review", never "pass", as the earlier report argued. Crowd triage, when volume justifies it, uses Toloka-style gold questions and overlap of three for binary checks only. The phased plan that results is:

What remains unverified should be stated as bluntly as the plan. Nearly every price above (Tripo, Meshy, Rodin, Hunyuan, Kaedim, Modelry, Polycam, KIRI, Auto-Rig Pro, Marvelous Designer, Echo3D, Zakeke, Remotion, Shotstack, every video API) comes from search excerpts or third-party trackers because the vendor pages were blocked, and several conflict (Tripo rigging at 20 versus 25 credits, Hunyuan 3.1 at $0.12 versus $0.225–0.375, Zakeke's two price ladders, Epidemic Sound's three tiers). Each of these is a first-week test rather than a blocker, and the review tool's own logs will settle the time and accuracy questions faster than any further desk research.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual framing of manual work as a temporary tax. The industry's best-resourced players, Amazon with its three-week artist-reviewed scan programme, Wayfair with its physical sample audits, Snap and Wanna with their creator-authored binding templates, all keep a human between the asset and the shopper, and the hosted generative vendors, for all their quad meshes and auto-rigs, leave scale, pivot and wearable anchors exactly where they were. A five-to-fifteen-minute human pass is therefore not a gap in Symspace's automation story but the mechanism that produces the only data no vendor sells: the mapping from a specific generator's output to a specific anchor frame, measured on real catalogue items. Built as a review tool that writes mesh-invariant correction records, that pass costs less per asset than a Fiverr touch-up and compounds into the gold set the earlier report identified as the company's real asset.

Two consequences follow for how to spend the next quarter. Spend engineering on the review tool, the dimensions field and the correction schema before spending it on any further generator or vendor evaluation, because every vendor can be swapped behind the GLB-plus-sidecar contract and none changes what the reviewer has to decide. And treat the concierge mode and the three-slider shopper controls as instruments rather than features: their logs, aggregated by median and gated by dispersion, are what will let each category graduate from review to audit, and the categories will graduate in the order the data arrives, furniture and rings first, garments last.
